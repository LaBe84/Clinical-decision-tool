CLINICAL WORKFLOW ROUTING

Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material describes an acute safety concern requiring action in
the next few minutes — no active means, no attempt in progress. No immediate-
risk override applies. Routing proceeds normally.

What's actually being asked
On the surface, staff are asking a single question: "does this need an
access restriction?" But that's a CMP/Access Review-shaped question being
asked before the groundwork it depends on exists. The material given is
purely a contact-volume change (roughly 4 calls/week to ~70 attempts/week
over a month) with no existing CMP. Nothing here yet establishes what that
change means clinically or functionally — so the actual first question is
"what has changed in this person's contact, and what is known/unknown about
its function," not "should access be restricted."

Note also: the absence of an existing CMP does not tell us whether a
formulation exists or whether this pattern has been clinically understood
before. That absence is not evidence either way and should not be read as
"nothing has been assessed" or "this is starting from zero."

Routing decision
This is the high-contact ("Nicola") pattern. Sequence:

1. Contact Pattern Analysis — first and mandatory. Establish what has
   actually changed in the contact pattern (frequency, timing/clustering,
   content, engagement, disconnection patterns) and what is currently
   known versus unknown about its function. A ~17x increase in attempts is
   a large, specific change that needs proper characterisation before
   anything downstream is decided — volume alone is not a finding.

2. Branch from what stage 1 establishes:
   - If Contact Pattern Analysis provides enough established context and
     function to answer the access question directly, go straight to
     CMP/Access Review from there. Don't insert PORF Formulation by default
     just because this is a helpline case in suicide-adjacent territory in
     general terms.
   - If the clinical meaning of the change is genuinely unresolved — not
     "a formulation would be nice to have" but something CMP/Access Review
     actually needs and doesn't have (e.g. what's driving the surge, what
     unmet need or risk state it reflects) — run PORF Formulation before
     CMP/Access Review.
   - If suicide-specific information needs establishing before PORF can do
     that work, Suicide Enquiry precedes PORF. Nothing in the material
     provided indicates this is required or ruled out — that determination
     belongs to Contact Pattern Analysis's output, not to this routing step.

3. CRG Triage remains conditional. Only route here if, after whichever of
   the above stages actually ran, a shared formulation or access decision
   genuinely remains unresolved at the current level of care. High volume
   by itself, or having run several stages, does not earn a CRG referral.

What each stage needs from the previous one
- CMP/Access Review needs from Contact Pattern Analysis: a characterisation
  of what changed and why (as far as knowable), and whatever is established
  about function — not just the raw frequency figures.
- PORF Formulation (if triggered) needs from Contact Pattern Analysis: the
  pattern findings as raw material to formulate from, plus an explicit flag
  of what remains unknown about function.
- CMP/Access Review (if PORF runs) needs from PORF: what the pattern means
  for this person and what response is proportionate — not a risk label,
  and not a pre-made access decision.
- Suicide Enquiry (if triggered) needs from Contact Pattern Analysis: the
  specific indication that suicide-specific material needs establishing
  before PORF can proceed responsibly.

Not routed to (yet)
- CMP/Access Review is not the first stop, even though it's the question
  staff literally asked. Routing straight there on volume alone would be
  exactly the shortcut this skill exists to prevent — jumping to "does this
  need restricting" before anyone has established what the change actually
  is or means.
- PORF Formulation and Suicide Enquiry are not pre-committed to this
  sequence. Whether either is needed is Contact Pattern Analysis's call to
  surface, not something to insert here "to be thorough" given the
  suicide-adjacent nature of the setting.
- CRG Triage is not invoked now. There is no stated unresolved decision
  that the current level of care cannot make — that would only become
  relevant if a later stage leaves something genuinely unresolved.

Bottom line for staff: don't decide on an access restriction yet. Run
Contact Pattern Analysis on the volume change first; let its findings
determine whether the access question can be answered directly or whether
PORF (and possibly Suicide Enquiry) needs to run first.
