Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material as presented (a manager's question plus a raw, unanalyzed transcript) is described as an acute, unfolding safety concern — no active means, no attempt in progress, nothing flagged as requiring action in the next few minutes. That said, this statement only holds to the extent the transcript has actually been read with that question in mind; it hasn't been reviewed for this yet. If anyone examining the transcript identifies an immediate danger indicator, organisational immediate-risk/emergency procedures take precedence and apply now, ahead of and independent of any routing decision below. The routing that follows proceeds on the assumption that no such indicator has been found.

What's actually being asked
The manager's literal question — "is this caller high risk?" — is a categorical risk judgement. None of the five skills answers that question directly as framed, and it cannot be answered yet at all, because the groundwork it depends on hasn't been done: nothing has established what was actually said (or not said) about suicidality on this call, and nothing has formulated what the material as a whole means for this caller. The real underlying questions, in order, are: (1) What has actually been established about suicidality in this call? (2) Given that, plus whatever else in the transcript is formulation-relevant, what does this presentation mean for this caller, and what response is proportionate?

Routing decision
Two-stage sequence:

1. **Suicide Enquiry** — first. The manager's question invokes risk, and the transcript is raw and unreviewed, so before anything else is done the material needs to be checked for what has and hasn't been directly established about suicidality, organised by CASE chronology. Since the scenario states suicide was never explicitly discussed on this call, the correct output of this stage is that suicidality is **not established** and direct enquiry did not occur — full stop. This is not the same as a finding of "low risk" or "no risk," and Suicide Enquiry must not scan the transcript for indirect content (hopelessness, tone, disengagement, goodbye-type language, functional decline) and convert that into a suicidality finding by inference. That inference is precisely what Suicide Enquiry exists to refuse to make.

2. **PORF Formulation** — second, and it should run regardless of what stage 1 finds. PORF is broader than suicidality: if the transcript contains other formulation-relevant material — deterioration, agitation, loss, altered functioning, reduced resources, a shift in how the caller is presenting — PORF can and should work with that material directly from the source. PORF's task is to determine what the presentation means for this person and what response is proportionate, explicitly carrying forward "suicidality not established / direct enquiry did not occur" as a named, unresolved gap in the formulation — not as a negative finding, and not as a reason to decline to run PORF at all.

Neither stage produces a categorical "high risk" label. The manager's question needs to be reframed into what these skills actually produce (see note below), not promised a direct yes/no answer.

What each stage needs from the previous one
Stage 2 (PORF) needs one specific input from stage 1 (Suicide Enquiry): the explicit statement that suicidality is not established and direct enquiry did not occur, so PORF carries that forward as a named gap rather than silently treating absence-of-enquiry as evidence of absence-of-risk. PORF does not need to wait on stage 1 to identify other formulation-relevant material already present in the transcript — that material is available to PORF directly from the source.
