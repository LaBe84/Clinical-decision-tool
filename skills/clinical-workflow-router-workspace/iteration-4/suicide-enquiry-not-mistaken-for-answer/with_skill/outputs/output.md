Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material as presented describes an acute safety concern requiring action in the next few minutes (no active means in hand, no attempt in progress, no indication of imminent danger). This routing decision proceeds on that basis. If anything in the counsellor's notes does in fact indicate an unfolding emergency, that overrides this routing entirely — organisational immediate-risk/emergency procedures take precedence and apply now, ahead of and independent of any skill selection, and this router should not be treated as the next step until that's been ruled out or handled.

What's actually being asked
The surface question is "what should happen next with this case." Underneath that, two distinct questions are bundled together and need to be pulled apart:
1. Has what's known about suicidality been captured in a usable, structured form (organised by CASE chronology), or is it currently just raw notes from the conversation?
2. What does what's been established actually mean for this caller, and what response is proportionate now?

The counsellor has completed the direct enquiry — thoughts, plan, and history were all asked about and written down — but "written down" is not the same claim as "organised and interpreted." Having the raw material is not itself the answer to "what happens next," and it shouldn't be treated as if it were.

Routing decision
This is a two-stage sequence, in this order:

1. **Suicide Enquiry** — Run the raw notes through Suicide Enquiry to establish, explicitly and by CASE chronology, what has actually been established about current thoughts, any plan, and history. This is a distinct step from the conversation itself: the counsellor conducted the enquiry, but the notes as they stand are raw material, not yet the structured CASE output PORF needs.
2. **PORF Formulation** — Once the CASE-organised findings exist, use them to determine what this presentation means for this caller now and what response is proportionate. This is the stage that actually answers "what should happen next" — Suicide Enquiry itself does not answer that question.

What each stage needs from the previous one
Suicide Enquiry takes the counsellor's raw documented notes (what was asked, what was said, in what order) and produces a CASE-organised account of current suicidal thoughts, any plan, and history — stating plainly what is established and what, if anything, remains unclear or unasked, rather than assuming completeness just because thoughts/plan/history were all nominally covered.

PORF Formulation takes that CASE-organised output as its input and produces what the presentation means for this person and what response is proportionate — not a categorical risk label, unless the specific version of PORF in use is built to produce one.

Note on the trap in this case specifically: it would be a mistake to treat "the counsellor asked about thoughts, plan, and history and wrote it down" as itself the answer to "what happens next." That's raw enquiry material, not a formulation — stopping there and calling it done is exactly the failure this router exists to prevent. The next step is to get that material properly organised (Suicide Enquiry) and then interpreted (PORF), not to act on the raw notes directly.
