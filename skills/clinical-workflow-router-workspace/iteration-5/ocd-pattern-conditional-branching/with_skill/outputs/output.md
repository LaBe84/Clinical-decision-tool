CLINICAL WORKFLOW ROUTING

Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material describes an acute safety concern requiring action
in the next few minutes (no active means, no attempt in progress, no
immediate-risk indicator mentioned). The immediate-safety override does not
apply. Routing proceeds normally.

What's actually being asked
On the surface, staff are asking a single question: "does this need an
access restriction?" But the material handed over is a raw volume change —
contact frequency going from roughly 4 calls/week to roughly 70
attempts/week over a month — with no formulation, no established
understanding of function, and no existing CMP. The access-restriction
question can't honestly be answered yet, because nothing has established
*what this change means* or *what function the contact is serving*. The
actual first question is: what is happening in this contact pattern, and
what (if anything) is known or unknown about its function.

Routing decision
This is the high-contact pattern. It starts with a fixed first step and then
branches rather than running a fixed pipeline:

1. **Contact Pattern Analysis (first, unconditionally)** — establish what
   has actually changed in the contact (frequency, clustering, timing,
   disconnection/engagement pattern) and what is and isn't known about the
   function behind the change. Nothing downstream — including the access
   question staff are actually asking — can be decided honestly before this
   runs.

2. **Branch point, to be resolved by what stage 1 actually establishes**
   (not decided here, since Contact Pattern Analysis hasn't run yet):
   - If Contact Pattern Analysis produces enough established context/function
     to answer the access question directly, go straight to **CMP/Access
     Review** next — do not insert PORF just because the material sits in
     suicide-adjacent territory in general terms.
   - If the clinical meaning of the change is genuinely unresolved after
     stage 1 — not "a formulation would be nice," but something CMP/Access
     Review actually needs and doesn't have — run **PORF Formulation**
     before CMP/Access Review.
   - If suicide-specific information needs establishing before PORF can do
     that work, **Suicide Enquiry** precedes PORF.

3. **CRG Triage** stays conditional throughout. Only route there if, after
   whichever of the above stages actually ran, a shared formulation or
   decision genuinely remains unresolved — apply CRG Triage's own forcing
   question ("what decision cannot be made at the current level of care") to
   whatever is actually left open. A 17-fold rise in contact volume does not
   by itself earn a CRG referral, and running several stages doesn't either.

What each stage needs from the previous one
- CMP/Access Review needs, from Contact Pattern Analysis, an account of what
  has changed and what is known/unknown about the function the contact is
  serving. Access Review cannot evaluate whether a restriction is justified
  against a volume number alone — it needs to know what the volume change
  represents.
- PORF Formulation, if it's needed, needs from Contact Pattern Analysis the
  same output — what changed and what remains functionally unresolved —
  specifically framed as the open question PORF is being asked to resolve,
  not just "there was a change."
- Suicide Enquiry, if it's needed, needs from PORF (or from the material
  directly) a specific, unresolved suicide-related question — not a general
  sense that the case is high-stakes.

Not routed to (yet)
- **CMP/Access Review** is not routed to first, despite being the question
  staff explicitly asked. Jumping there on volume alone is the exact
  shortcut this pattern exists to block — staff's question can only be
  answered once Contact Pattern Analysis has established what the change
  means.
- **PORF Formulation** and **Suicide Enquiry** are not routed to at this
  point either. Whether they're needed depends entirely on what Contact
  Pattern Analysis establishes, which hasn't happened yet. Note explicitly:
  the absence of an existing CMP says nothing about whether a formulation
  already exists elsewhere — that fact is not being used to infer that PORF
  is (or isn't) needed.
- **CRG Triage** is not routed to. No unresolved decision has been
  identified yet, and none should be assumed just because the volume
  increase is large or because staff are anxious about it.

Bottom line for staff: run Contact Pattern Analysis on this case now. That
output — not the raw call-count jump, and not the absence of a CMP — is what
determines whether the next stage is CMP/Access Review directly, or PORF
(possibly preceded by Suicide Enquiry) first.
