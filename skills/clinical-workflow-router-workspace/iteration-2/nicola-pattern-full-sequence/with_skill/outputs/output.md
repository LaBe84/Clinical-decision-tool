CLINICAL WORKFLOW ROUTING

Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material describes an acute safety concern requiring action
in the next few minutes (no active means, no attempt in progress, no
immediate crisis). No override applies. Routing proceeds normally — this is
not a statement that risk is low, only that nothing here triggers the
bypass-routing pathway.

What's actually being asked
On the surface: "does this need an access restriction?" (i.e. does this
need a CMP). But that's a CMP/Access Review-shaped question being asked
before the groundwork it depends on exists. The material presented is a
large, unexplained change in contact behaviour (~4/week to ~70 attempts/week
over a month) with no existing CMP. The actual first question is: what has
changed, and what is and isn't known about the function of that change —
not yet "should access be restricted."

Routing decision
This matches the high-contact/existing-CMP ("Nicola") pattern, so it starts
from Contact Pattern Analysis rather than jumping to CMP/Access Review, and
proceeds by branching from what that stage establishes rather than
following a fixed pipeline:

1. **Contact Pattern Analysis (run first, unconditionally for this
   pattern).** Establish what has actually changed in the contact pattern
   (the ~4/week → ~70/week shift, attempt vs. connected-call composition,
   clustering, timing, any disconnection/engagement pattern) and what is
   and isn't known about the function the contact is serving. Nothing
   downstream — including whether an access restriction is even a coherent
   question yet — can be decided honestly without this.

2. **Branch point, decided by what stage 1 actually establishes** — not
   predetermined here:
   - If Contact Pattern Analysis yields enough established context/function
     to answer the access question directly, route straight to **CMP/Access
     Review** next.
   - If the clinical meaning of the change is genuinely unresolved (not
     merely "a formulation would be nice to have," but something CMP/Access
     Review actually needs and doesn't have), route to **PORF Formulation**
     before CMP/Access Review.
   - If suicide-specific information needs establishing before PORF can do
     that work, **Suicide Enquiry** precedes PORF.

   Which of these applies cannot be determined from the material given here
   — that determination is Contact Pattern Analysis's output, not something
   this routing step should pre-empt.

3. **CRG Triage** stays conditional throughout. It is not routed to now. It
   only becomes relevant if, after whichever of the above stages actually
   run, a shared formulation or decision genuinely remains unresolved at
   the current level of care — high volume alone does not earn a CRG
   referral, and neither does having run several stages.

What each stage needs from the previous one
- CMP/Access Review (whenever it runs, at step 2 or later) needs from
  Contact Pattern Analysis: a clear account of what changed in the contact
  pattern and what is known about the function it's serving. It cannot
  evaluate whether an access arrangement is justified against an
  unanalysed volume number alone.
- If PORF Formulation is needed: it needs from Contact Pattern Analysis the
  established facts of the change (what, when, how much, what's known vs.
  unknown about function) as the material to formulate from.
- If Suicide Enquiry is needed before PORF: PORF needs CASE-organised
  material from Suicide Enquiry sufficient to determine what the
  presentation means and what response is proportionate — not merely that
  suicide "was asked about" at some point.

Not routed to (yet)
- **CMP/Access Review** is not routed to directly right now. The staff
  question ("does this need an access restriction?") is exactly the
  question this pattern exists to stop from being answered prematurely —
  routing to it before Contact Pattern Analysis would mean deciding on
  restriction against a volume number with no established function behind
  it.
- **PORF Formulation** and **Suicide Enquiry** are not routed to yet either,
  for the same reason: whether they're needed at all is something Contact
  Pattern Analysis's output determines, not something assumed up front
  because the case involves suicide-adjacent territory in general.
- **CRG Triage** is not routed to. No unresolved decision at the current
  level of care has been identified — and won't be, until the stages above
  actually run.

Note on the "no existing CMP" fact
The absence of an existing CMP is not evidence about whether a formulation
exists. It would be an inference error to read "no CMP" as "no
formulation" (or, equally, as "formulation not needed") — that's the same
unknown-as-negative-evidence error this skill family exists to prevent,
relocated into the routing layer. Whether a formulation already exists, or
is needed, is not established by the material provided and should be
stated as such rather than inferred from the CMP's absence. Contact Pattern
Analysis's output — not this adjacent fact — is what determines whether
PORF is actually needed at step 2.
