CLINICAL WORKFLOW ROUTING

Immediate safety check
Nothing in the material describes an acute safety concern requiring action
in the next few minutes (no active means, no attempt in progress, no
immediate risk indicator described). No override applies — normal routing
proceeds.

What's actually being asked
On the surface, staff are asking a single, narrow question: "does this need
an access restriction?" But that framing skips groundwork. The material
describes a dramatic, sustained change in contact behaviour (~4 calls/week
to ~70 attempts/week over a month) with no existing CMP. The actual
underlying questions are:
1. What has actually changed in this person's contact pattern, and what is
   known (versus assumed) about why?
2. Does anything in that pattern raise suicide/risk considerations that need
   formulating?
3. Given 1 (and 2 where relevant), is an access restriction/CMP actually the
   proportionate response, or is something else warranted?
4. Only if a genuine decision remains unresolved after that — does this need
   to go anywhere else (e.g. CRG)?

"Does this need an access restriction" cannot be answered yet — it presumes
the access-review stage has something to work from, and right now it
doesn't.

Routing decision
This is the high-contact/no-existing-CMP pattern (the "Nicola" pattern) and
should not jump straight to CMP/Access Review. Ordered sequence:

1. Contact Pattern Analysis — establish what has actually changed (the
   4/week → 70/week shift), and what is known versus unknown about function:
   is it clustering around specific times/triggers, disconnection pattern,
   engagement quality, staff burden, anything suggestive of escalating
   distress or a change in this person's situation. This stage does not
   decide whether access should be restricted — it establishes the facts an
   access decision would need.

2. PORF Formulation — if Contact Pattern Analysis surfaces anything
   suicide/risk-relevant, or if no formulation for this person currently
   exists (which appears to be the case, since there's no CMP), establish
   what the pattern means for this person now and what response is
   proportionate. This is required groundwork before an access decision,
   not optional.

3. CMP/Access Review — using the outputs of 1 (and 2 where relevant) as
   input, assess whether an access restriction or other structured
   arrangement is actually justified and what it should contain. This is
   the stage that can actually answer the staff's question — and only this
   stage.

4. CRG Triage — not automatic. Apply only if, after 1–3, a decision remains
   genuinely unresolved (e.g. disagreement about the appropriate level of
   care, or a decision that exceeds what can be made at the current level).
   High contact volume alone does not earn a CRG referral; it earns Contact
   Pattern Analysis.

What each stage needs from the previous one
- Stage 2 needs from Stage 1: the established facts of what changed in the
  contact pattern and any risk-relevant signal surfaced during that
  analysis (not just the raw volume figures).
- Stage 3 needs from Stages 1–2: the pattern-analysis findings, plus a
  formulation of what the pattern means for this person, to judge whether a
  restriction is a proportionate and clinically justified response (versus,
  say, a different kind of support).
- Stage 4 (if needed) needs from Stage 3: the specific unresolved decision
  that access review could not settle at the current level of care.

Not routed to
- Suicide Enquiry — not indicated unless Contact Pattern Analysis or PORF
  Formulation surfaces suicide-specific content requiring CASE-chronology
  enquiry. Nothing in the material as presented currently calls for it
  directly; it may become relevant depending on what Stage 1 finds.
- CRG Triage as a first move — the volume increase alone is not itself an
  unresolved decision; routing straight there would skip the groundwork
  that determines whether there even is one.

Note on staff's framing: "there's no existing CMP" is not itself a reason to
route straight to CMP/Access Review, nor a reason to avoid it — it just
means Stage 3, if reached, is evaluating a proposed new arrangement rather
than reviewing an existing one. The absence of a CMP doesn't change the
required order of the sequence.
