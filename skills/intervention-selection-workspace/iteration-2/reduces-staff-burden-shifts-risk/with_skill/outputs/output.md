INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not established as a clinical target. The material as presented gives an
operational aim — reduce this caller's contact with the helpline — rather
than a stated change in the caller's presentation (e.g. reduced risk,
improved regulation, reduced reliance on a specific unhelpful pattern) that
would justify referring them out. A reduction in contact is an effect a
response might have, not itself a legitimate intervention target unless
tied to an established clinical or care need.

Established basis
What is established: the caller is currently using helpline contact as
their main source of stability, and the proposed referral is to an external
service with a long waiting list. What is not established: any upstream
finding (PORF, Suicide Enquiry, Contact Pattern Analysis, CRG Triage) that
this caller's current use of the helpline is reinforcing an unhelpful
pattern, that their needs would be better met elsewhere, or that the
present level of contact is clinically inappropriate. The stated rationale
in front of this skill is staff workload reduction. That is a real
operational consideration, but on its own it is not evidence of a clinical
or care need, and this skill cannot treat it as one.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. Before any change to this caller's contact arrangements can
be proposed, upstream material needs to establish an actual clinical
target: is there a finding that current contact is not helping, is
reinforcing escalation, or that the caller's needs are better met by the
external service on clinical grounds — independent of the waiting-list
referral's effect on staff workload? If that finding doesn't exist yet, it
is Clinical Workflow Router's job to determine whether it needs to be
established, not this skill's job to instruct which upstream skill runs.

As proposed, this intervention does not clear the six decision domains and
should not proceed to destination 3/5 in its current form. The remainder of
this output evaluates the proposal against those domains to show why.

Mechanism
The proposal cannot complete the required causal sentence on clinical
grounds. The available sentence is: "We are reducing this caller's helpline
contact because we expect it to reduce staff workload" — which is a
service-level mechanism, not a therapeutic one. No sentence of the form "we
expect referral to influence [caller's stated difficulty] through [the
external service's specific offer]" can be completed from what's
established here, because the caller's current reliance on the helpline as
their main source of stability is not shown to be a problem this referral
would resolve — it is a need the referral would leave unmet for the
duration of the waiting list.

Proportionality
Cannot be assessed as proportionate. Proportionality is measured against an
established need; the only established driver here is service impact, not
individual need. Whether workload reduction is functioning as the primary
rationale rather than an established clinical or care need is precisely the
question that has to be answered before intensity or destination can be
judged — and on the material presented, it has not been answered in the
intervention's favour.

Risks / unintended effects
- Support gap: a caller currently using the helpline as their main source
  of stability would lose that primary support before the alternative
  service becomes available, given the stated long waiting list. This is a
  foreseeable coverage gap, not a hypothetical one.
- Risk displacement rather than risk reduction: moving the caller off the
  helpline does not remove whatever need the contact is currently meeting —
  it relocates the caller to a period with less support than they have now,
  which is a different outcome from the need being resolved.
- Service containment mistaken for benefit: if this proceeds, staff
  workload will fall and the caller may appear to "no longer need" the
  service simply because contact is no longer occurring — that is a
  reduction in visible demand, not evidence the underlying need was met.
- Fragmented ownership: the caller would sit in a gap between two services
  (reduced/no helpline contact, not yet received by the external service)
  with no established owner during the waiting period.
- Undermining existing stability: if the helpline is functioning as a
  stabilising factor, removing it without a bridging arrangement risks
  destabilising a currently stable pattern rather than improving it.

Ownership
Not assignable yet — no destination beyond "seek additional information"
has been established. If, after that information is obtained, a legitimate
clinical basis for referral is confirmed, ownership of any transition
arrangement (e.g. continued helpline contact until the external service
takes over) is a Care Management Plan/Access Review matter, not decided
here.

Review
Not applicable until a target and mechanism are established. If this
returns as destination 3 or 5 with a genuine clinical basis, the review
criterion would need to explicitly cover the waiting-list period itself:
what supports this caller during that gap, and how continuity of care
across the two services is confirmed.

Residual uncertainty
Whether there is any established clinical or care-need basis for reducing
this caller's contact, independent of staff workload; the actual length of
the waiting list and whether any bridging support is available during it;
what specific need the caller's current helpline use is meeting and whether
the external service is equipped to meet it; and whether service-level
workload pressure has been named explicitly as operational rather than
allowed to stand in for a clinical justification.
