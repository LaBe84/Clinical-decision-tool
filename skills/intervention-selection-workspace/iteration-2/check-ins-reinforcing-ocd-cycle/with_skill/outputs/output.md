INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not fully established as a positive treatment target — what is established is
narrower than "treat OCD-type reassurance-seeking." Contact Pattern Analysis
has established the behavioural sequence: repeated calls seeking reassurance,
each call producing temporary anxiety relief, and the checking pattern
intensifying over weeks. Contact Pattern Analysis describes this as
*resembling* OCD-type reassurance-seeking — that label is a hypothesis about
function, not a confirmed mechanism. This skill preserves that distinction:
the established fact is the behavioural sequence (call → temporary relief →
intensification); the reassurance-seeking/reinforcement explanation for why
it's intensifying is plausible but not established. A candidate target —
reduce reinforcement of an intensifying contact pattern — can be named
provisionally, but the specific mechanism cannot be asserted as fact.

Established basis
- Repeated calls for reassurance checks (established, from Contact Pattern
  Analysis).
- Each call produces temporary anxiety relief (established).
- The pattern is intensifying over weeks (established).
- "Resembling OCD-type reassurance-seeking" — named explicitly upstream as a
  resemblance/hypothesis, not a confirmed clinical formulation or diagnosis.
- No information supplied here about current risk state, the person's own
  goals or preferences regarding contact, any existing care pathway or
  treatment modality, or prior response to attempts to change the contact
  pattern.

Selected response
The proposed intervention — adding more frequent scheduled check-in calls "to
get ahead of it" — is not supported and should not be adopted as proposed.

Destination: **Seek additional information before selecting an intervention**
(destination 6), specifically about the current function of the contact
pattern and any existing care pathway, routed back to Clinical Workflow
Router rather than resolved here. Separately, and regardless of that
information gap, the specific proposed response (increased scheduled contact
frequency) can already be ruled out on the facts as established — see
Contraindications below — so the choice is not "destination 6 vs. destination
3," it is "destination 6, and not this particular addition."

Mechanism
No mechanism can be completed for the proposed response. The honest sentence
is: "We would be adding more scheduled check-ins because it feels protective
to increase contact, not because we can state what Y it would change or
through what Z." That fails the requirement — a causal sentence connecting
more contact to a stated improvement in this pattern cannot be built from
what's established. If anything, the established behavioural sequence (call →
temporary relief → intensification) points the mechanism the other way:
additional contact matches the shape of the sequence already recorded as
intensifying, which is a reason for caution, not a justification for adding
more of it.

Proportionality
Not assessable as proportionate, because no target or mechanism has been
established for it to be proportionate to. The proposal is calibrated to
staff/service discomfort with an intensifying pattern ("get ahead of it"),
not to an established clinical need — that is exactly the pressure this
skill is built to resist: "something must be done" is not evidence a
specific intervention is indicated.

Risks / unintended effects
- Directly matches the pattern already on record: calling produces relief,
  and the checking behaviour has intensified over weeks. Scheduling more
  contact adds more instances of the same call → relief sequence, which is a
  plausible route to reinforcing it further rather than interrupting it —
  this follows from the established behavioural facts, not from asserting
  the unconfirmed OCD/reassurance-seeking mechanism as fact.
- Increases dependency risk: more scheduled reassurance contact could make
  the service itself part of the checking cycle rather than outside it.
- Risks service containment being mistaken for benefit — more frequent
  contact may make the caller calmer and the case look quieter without that
  representing therapeutic change.
- Treats contact frequency as a proxy for intervention intensity/care, which
  this skill's own discipline rules out.
- No review mechanism was proposed alongside the increase, so even if it
  were adopted there is currently no way to distinguish "helping" from
  "temporarily quieter."

Ownership
Not assigned. No specific destination beyond "seek additional information"
is being selected, so no team/role is being tasked with delivering a new
intervention at this point. Whoever is coordinating the case should route
the identified information gap back through Clinical Workflow Router.

Review
Not applicable to a rejected proposal. Once the missing information (current
function of the contact pattern, any relevant care pathway, the person's own
preferences) is established, this skill should be re-run to select a target,
mechanism, and review criterion for whatever response is then justified.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the pattern is in fact reassurance-seeking-driven and reinforced by
each call remains unconfirmed — it is a testable hypothesis, not an
established mechanism. What would actually help (e.g. holding contact steady,
a structured reduction, a different pathway) cannot be determined from the
material available here. The person's own view of the calls, any existing
care plan, and current risk state are all unknown and were not backfilled to
reach this conclusion.
