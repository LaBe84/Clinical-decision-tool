INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Whether the existing safety plan's primary escalation step (call the crisis
line) should be modified. The candidate target is: reduce a specific,
named barrier to use of the safety plan — the caller reports that repeating
her situation to a new person each time is itself distressing, which is a
plausible reason the current step is not functioning as intended for her.
This has not yet been confirmed as the mechanism, only stated by her as her
experience and preference; it is named here as a candidate target, not an
established one.

Established basis
The caller has directly and specifically stated that the current
plan step (calling the crisis line) is distressing to her because it
requires re-explaining her situation to an unfamiliar person each contact,
and that she would strongly prefer contacting one specific trusted person
instead. A person's stated preference about a step in their own safety
plan, given with a specific and coherent reason, is clinically meaningful
information — it is not, by itself, something to be overridden simply
because it conflicts with what's currently written down. A documented plan
reflects a prior decision, not a permanent one; conflict with the existing
plan is not itself evidence against the caller's request.

What is not established here: whether the proposed alternative (the one
trusted person) is a safe, available, and appropriate substitute or
supplement for that step, what should happen if that person is unreachable,
and how the rest of the plan's escalation sequence should be structured
around this change. None of that is decided by this skill.

Selected response
Destination 2 — Modify current response. The target (reduce a
distress-generating barrier in the existing escalation step) is
plausible and directly stated by the caller, but the current
implementation of that step (calling the crisis line as the specified
first escalation action) is not achieving it as currently written for
this person. Modification of the safety plan is potentially indicated.

The operational design of any modification — whether the named trusted
person should be added, how they relate to the crisis line as a
fallback or replacement, what happens if that person is unavailable, and
any other structural detail of the plan — is not decided here. That
belongs to Safety Planning, and should be routed there.

Mechanism
We are naming a candidate change to the escalation step (not designing it)
because we expect that removing or replacing a step the caller experiences
as consistently distressing to use may increase the likelihood she actually
uses her safety plan when needed, through reducing a known barrier to
help-seeking that she herself has identified. Whether this mechanism holds,
and how to implement it safely, is for Safety Planning to establish and
design.

Proportionality
Naming "modification potentially indicated" and routing to Safety Planning
is proportionate to what's established: a specific, coherent, person-
identified problem with one step of an existing plan. It does not require
escalation to a different level of care, immediate safety action, or
additional upstream information — the caller's own account is a sufficient
basis to flag the plan for review. It would be disproportionate either to
dismiss her stated preference outright (destination 1, "maintain
unchanged") or to design the substitute arrangement here rather than
routing it to the skill responsible for that content.

Risks / unintended effects
Not yet evaluable from this skill's remit — whether the proposed alternative
introduces new risks (e.g., over-reliance on a single person, that person's
own capacity/availability, loss of the crisis line's clinical fallback
function) is precisely the kind of question Safety Planning needs to weigh
when designing the modification. Flagging it here: any modification must
preserve a workable escalation path if the named trusted person is
unavailable — but the specific fallback structure is not designed in this
output.

Ownership
Safety Planning owns the redesign of this step, in consultation with the
caller. This skill's role ends at naming that modification is potentially
indicated and routing there.

Review
Not yet applicable — a review criterion belongs to the modified plan once
Safety Planning has designed it, not to this routing decision.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the caller's one trusted person is a safe and reliable substitute or
should function as a supplement alongside (rather than replacement of) the
crisis line; what fallback exists if that person is unreachable; whether
this preference reflects a broader, stable pattern or is specific to recent
experience. All of this is Safety Planning's work, not established or
resolved here.
