INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. No PORF conclusion, contact-pattern finding, or specific
unmet need has been established for this caller — only a shared staff sense
of unease. "Something needs to be done" names a level of concern, not a
target for change. Without a stated target (reduce immediate risk, improve
regulation, clarify uncertainty, reduce reinforcement of a pattern, etc.),
there is nothing here for an intervention to be mechanistically justified
against.

Established basis
None that this skill can act on. What exists is: (a) a case that has been
discussed at length, and (b) staff consensus that concern is warranted. Staff
consensus that something is wrong is a legitimate signal that attention is
needed — it is not, by itself, evidence that any specific intervention is
indicated, and per this skill's boundary rules it must not be treated as
though it were.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. Nothing is proposed at this time. The pressure to act now
should not be resolved by selecting a plausible-sounding intervention in the
absence of a target; it should be resolved by establishing what, specifically,
is concerning.

Mechanism
Not applicable. No target has been stated, so the causal sentence ("we are
doing X because we expect it to influence Y through Z") cannot be completed.
Attempting to complete it now would mean inventing a mechanism to justify an
action already decided on emotional grounds, which is the failure mode this
skill exists to prevent.

Proportionality
Not applicable yet — proportionality can only be assessed once a target
exists. Naming this gap explicitly is itself the proportionate response to
"everyone agrees something must be done": it neither dismisses the concern
nor manufactures a treatment target from it.

Risks / unintended effects
Selecting an intervention now, before a target is established, risks: acting
on staff anxiety rather than the caller's actual situation; creating a
record that implies a clinical basis existed when it didn't; consuming a
service response that may not match whatever the real issue turns out to be;
and setting a precedent that "consensus of unease" alone triggers action,
which erodes the distinction between signal and evidence going forward.

Ownership
Route back to Clinical Workflow Router to determine what upstream material
is missing — e.g., a current risk-state finding, a contact-pattern function,
or clarification of what specifically is driving the shared concern. This
skill does not itself dispatch PORF, Suicide Enquiry, Contact Pattern
Analysis, or CRG Triage; it names that the gap exists and hands the
routing decision back.

Review
Once upstream findings establish a specific target — a risk-state change, a
contact-pattern finding, or a named unmet need — this skill should be
re-run to select a proportionate response against that target. There is no
review clock on "nothing," but the discussion itself should not be allowed
to sit indefinitely as a substitute for either establishing a finding or
formally closing the concern.

Residual uncertainty
Everything concrete: what specifically is concerning, whether it reflects a
change in risk, a contact-pattern issue, an unmet need, or something else
entirely, and whether the shared unease reflects a real signal in the case
or an artifact of prolonged discussion without a clear conclusion. None of
this should be resolved by this skill — it should be resolved upstream, then
brought back here.
