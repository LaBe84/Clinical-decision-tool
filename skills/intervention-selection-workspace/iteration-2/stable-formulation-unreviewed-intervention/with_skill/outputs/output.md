INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. No target was recorded when the fortnightly check-in
calls were set up eighteen months ago, and none is recoverable from the
material available now. Without knowing what the calls were meant to change
(reduce risk, sustain engagement, monitor a specific concern, something
else), there is nothing to test the current intervention against.

Established basis
PORF has found the presentation stable relative to baseline: no material
change in Risk State, no new plan or intent. That finding supports one
conclusion only — there is no basis to escalate. It does not, on its own,
establish that the fortnightly check-in intervention is working, is still
needed, or was ever justified against a stated target. Stability of
presentation and effectiveness of intervention are separate claims, and nothing
here bridges them. Separately, the intervention has never been reviewed
against a target in eighteen months, so continued delivery reflects adherence
to a routine, not evidence of benefit. A perfect delivery record (calls
happening every fortnight, uninterrupted) is not evidence of effect.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. The target and mechanism for the fortnightly check-in calls
cannot be established from what's available. No change is proposed to the
current arrangement on the strength of today's material, but "no change" here
means "insufficient basis to decide," not "confirmed as proportionate and
working."

Mechanism
Cannot be completed. The causal sentence this skill requires — "we are doing
X because we expect it to influence Y through Z" — cannot be filled in for
the existing intervention because no Y (target) was ever recorded. Retrofitting
a plausible-sounding purpose now, after the fact, would be inventing a
justification rather than reporting one that exists.

Proportionality
Cannot be assessed against an unstated target. Proportionality is a
judgement about whether the intensity of a response matches an established
need — with no recorded need, there is nothing to measure fortnightly calls
against, in either direction. This is not a finding that the calls are
proportionate by default because no one is asking to escalate.

Risks / unintended effects
Two risks worth naming explicitly, independent of which way this eventually
resolves:
- Continuing an unreviewed contact pattern for eighteen months without a
  target risks the intervention functioning as service ritual rather than
  treatment, and risks fostering reliance on scheduled contact that was never
  tied to a clinical purpose.
- Equally, stopping or reducing the calls without first establishing what
  they were for would be an equally ungrounded decision in the opposite
  direction — removing a possibly load-bearing contact point on no more
  evidence than the decision to keep it currently rests on.
Neither "keep it because it's always been there" nor "cut it because it's
never been reviewed" is supported by what's established. Both are guesses.

Ownership
To be determined once the missing information is sought; not assigned by
this decision.

Review
Cannot be set. A review criterion presupposes a target to review against.
Establishing the original (or a current) purpose for the fortnightly calls is
a precondition for setting an observable review test, not a parallel task to
do alongside continuing the calls unexamined.

Residual uncertainty
- What the fortnightly check-in calls were intended to achieve when set up,
  and whether that purpose still applies to the current presentation.
- Whether the calls have had any observable effect (positive, absent, or
  counterproductive) over the eighteen months, since no review took place.
- Whether the person's own view of the calls' value has ever been sought.
- Whether an appropriate target can still be identified retrospectively, or
  whether the intervention should be treated as never having had one.

Note on what this does not conclude: PORF's finding of a stable presentation
with no material change and no new plan/intent rules out escalation. It does
not rule in continuation of the current intervention, and it does not
establish that the intervention is proportionate or working. Those are
separate questions this material does not answer. The correct next step is to
establish what the fortnightly calls were meant to achieve — not to run
further upstream skills from within this decision, but to route back for
that missing information to be gathered before an intervention decision
(maintain, modify, or stop) can be made.
