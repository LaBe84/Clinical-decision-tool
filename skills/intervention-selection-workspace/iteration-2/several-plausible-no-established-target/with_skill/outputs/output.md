INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. Safety planning, increased contact, referral to another
service, and CMP review are each plausible in the abstract, but none of them
has a stated target that this material actually supports — no upstream
finding points at a specific need (e.g. a current risk-state change, a
characterised contact-pattern function, or a defined service-fit problem)
that any one of these would address.

Established basis
None of the four candidate interventions has an established basis in what's
been provided. What's missing is a conclusion from the upstream formulation
work that would point at a specific need — for example, a current
risk-state finding, a characterised function for the contact pattern, or a
triage/pathway-fit conclusion. That conclusion isn't present here. Its
absence is what's driving the pull toward "do something plausible," and
that pull is exactly what this skill is built to resist.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. Nothing is proposed at this time. Selecting any one of
safety planning, increased contact, referral, or CMP review now would mean
fitting an available action to the situation rather than fitting the
response to an established need — the sprawl this skill exists to prevent.

Mechanism
Not applicable. No target has been established, so the causal sentence
("we are doing X because we expect it to influence Y through Z") cannot be
completed for any of the candidate interventions without asserting a
mechanism the upstream material doesn't support.

Proportionality
Not applicable — proportionality is assessed against an established need,
and none of the four candidates currently has one. Proposing any of them
now would be calibrated to the discomfort of inaction rather than to a
clinical target.

Risks / unintended effects
Selecting from this list without an established target risks: intervention
sprawl (multiple plausible actions layered on without a clear rationale for
any one of them), fragmenting ownership across services if referral is made
without a defined need, prompting a CMP/access change without the purpose
and review mechanism this skill requires, and reinforcing the idea that
"something must be done" is itself justification for action. It also
delays identifying what's actually needed by consuming attention and
resources on activity that isn't targeted.

Ownership
Route back to Clinical Workflow Router. What's missing is a conclusion
about the person's current state or need from the upstream formulation
work — the router is responsible for determining which upstream process
should produce it. This skill does not name or dispatch that process
itself.

Review
Not applicable until a target is established. Once upstream material
supplies a specific finding, this skill should be re-run against that
finding to select a proportionate response with its own review criterion.

Residual uncertainty
It remains unknown whether any intervention is indicated at all, and if so
which one. The pressure to act ("everyone agrees we need to do something")
is noted but is not, by itself, evidence that a specific intervention is
indicated. Until the upstream material establishes a target, "insufficient
basis to select an intervention" is the correct and complete answer.
