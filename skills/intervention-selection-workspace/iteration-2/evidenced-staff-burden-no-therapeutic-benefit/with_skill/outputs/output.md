INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not established as a clinical/therapeutic target for this caller. Contact
Pattern Analysis evidences an operational target — restoring equitable wait
times / service delivery for other callers — but no target for change in
this caller's own presentation can be stated. Indeed, the material states the
opposite: there is no evidence that reducing this caller's contact would
provide any therapeutic benefit to them, and PORF suggests the contact is
currently one of their few stabilising factors. These are two different
questions and must not be merged into one target.

Established basis
- Operational: documented instances of other callers experiencing extended
  wait times, directly attributable to this caller's contact volume,
  confirmed by call logs. This is evidenced, not merely asserted.
- Clinical: PORF formulation indicates the contact is currently a
  stabilising factor for this caller, and no material establishes
  therapeutic benefit from reducing it.

Selected response
This skill does not decide the access-restriction question here. What is
being proposed by the pattern of facts presented is a change to this
caller's access arrangements — that is CMP/Access Review's territory, not
this skill's. Naming the destination: route to CMP/Access Review, with the
following explicitly carried forward rather than resolved here:

- The service-impact evidence is real and documented (not the "asserted
  service impact" pattern CMP/Access Review's own discipline warns against —
  here it is evidenced by call logs).
- The clinical evidence points against restriction: no therapeutic benefit
  from reduced contact, and a stabilising-factor finding that any restriction
  would need to weigh against.
- This is not this skill's call to make. Selecting or declining an access
  restriction requires CMP/Access Review's own purpose / expected-effect /
  review-mechanism test, which is not something Intervention Selection may
  perform on its behalf.

If CMP/Access Review, applying its own discipline, determines a restriction
is warranted, that decision must state its purpose, expected effect, and
review mechanism explicitly, and must not be justified as producing
therapeutic benefit to this caller — the basis, if any, is operational
(service delivery/equity), not clinical, and must be named as such.

Mechanism
Not completed. No causal "we are doing X because we expect it to influence Y
through Z" sentence can be built for this caller's own outcomes, because the
established evidence is that reducing contact would not influence this
caller's presentation for the better and may remove a stabilising factor.
Any mechanism for an access change here would run in the opposite direction
(protecting other callers' access), which is precisely why it belongs to
CMP/Access Review rather than being written up as a therapeutic
intervention.

Proportionality
Not assessed here — proportionality of an access restriction against
evidenced service impact, weighed against this caller's stabilising-factor
finding, is the proportionality question CMP/Access Review exists to answer.

Risks / unintended effects
Named for the record, not resolved: restricting contact here risks removing
one of this caller's few stabilising factors, with no established
countervailing therapeutic gain — a straightforward case of a service-level
consideration potentially being treated as sufficient justification for an
individual access change, which the boundary rules explicitly prohibit this
skill (and CMP/Access Review, on its own terms) from allowing without
explicit, separate justification.

Ownership
CMP/Access Review owns the access-restriction decision. Intervention
Selection's role ends at naming that destination and carrying forward the
evidenced tension between service impact and clinical stabilisation.

Review
Not applicable — no intervention has been selected here. If CMP/Access
Review proceeds to a decision, its own review criterion applies to that
decision.

Residual uncertainty
Whether any lower-burden operational response (unrelated to this caller's
individual access, e.g. broader service capacity/routing measures) could
address the wait-time impact without touching this caller's contact at all
has not been established either way from the material available, and is
also outside this skill's remit to resolve.
