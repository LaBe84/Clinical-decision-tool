INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. Staff report exhaustion in relation to a particular
caller, but no target for an access restriction can be stated from what's
available — there is no contact pattern analysis identifying a problematic
pattern, and no documented evidence of actual service-delivery impact (other
callers going unserved, delays, resourcing breach, etc.). "Staff are tired
of this caller" is a description of staff experience, not a stated clinical
or operational target that an access restriction would address.

Established basis
None established for an access restriction. What exists is staff-reported
exhaustion — a legitimate signal that attention is needed, but by itself an
unevidenced assertion of service impact, not a finding. No Contact Pattern
Analysis has been done, so there is no basis to characterise the contact
pattern as excessive, reinforcing, or otherwise clinically or operationally
problematic. No upstream PORF, Suicide Enquiry, or CRG Triage material is
cited either, so no clinical target can be drawn from those sources.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. No access restriction, or any other intervention, should be
selected on the current basis. This is also a case named explicitly in this
skill's own list of reasoning shortcuts to avoid: restricting access because
staff are exhausted, when service impact is asserted rather than evidenced.

Mechanism
Cannot be completed. There is no stated "X because we expect it to
influence Y through Z" available — the causal chain would have to run from
an access restriction to a reduction in some established problem, and no
problem has been established (no contact-pattern finding, no documented
service impact). Naming a mechanism now would mean inventing one to fit an
action already wanted, which this skill's boundary rules exclude.

Proportionality
Cannot be assessed without a stated target. Proportionality asks whether the
intensity of the response matches an established need — with no need
established, there is nothing to size the response against. Staff distress
about a caller is real, but distress/exhaustion on the staff side does not
by itself scale an intervention's proportionality any more than caller
distress alone would.

Risks / unintended effects
Restricting access on this basis, if it were done, risks: withdrawing
support based on staff fatigue rather than a clinical or evidenced
operational need (undermining trust and possibly worsening the presenting
problem); setting a precedent that "we found this caller wearing" is
sufficient grounds for restriction without evidence, which would erode the
consistency and defensibility of future access decisions; and treating
service containment (staff feeling less burdened) as if it were therapeutic
benefit, when no therapeutic target has even been named. There is also a
risk in the opposite direction — dismissing staff-reported exhaustion
entirely without follow-up would leave a genuine operational signal
uninvestigated.

Ownership
Two separate gaps need to be closed before any destination other than 6 is
available, and neither is this skill's to resolve directly:
- Whether a contact pattern requiring characterisation exists — that's
  upstream analysis, and the missing-information gap should be routed back
  to Clinical Workflow Router rather than this skill naming which specific
  analysis to run.
- Whether an access change is indicated once evidence exists — that
  decision, including the specifics of any restriction, belongs to
  CMP/Access Review, not to this skill.
Separately, the operational question of staff workload and sustainable
service delivery is legitimate to raise, but it must be routed and named
explicitly as an operational consideration for service management, not
folded into a clinical or access decision about this caller.

Review
No review criterion applies yet, because no intervention has been selected.
Before any intervention (including an access restriction) can be proposed:
we will know there is a basis to proceed if a documented contact-pattern
finding and/or documented evidence of actual service-delivery impact (e.g.
other callers measurably affected, resourcing thresholds breached) becomes
available; we will reconsider proceeding at all if, once gathered, that
evidence still shows no more than staff-reported fatigue; review this
information gap now, before the next case discussion where restriction is
raised again.

Residual uncertainty
Whether a genuine contact pattern or service-impact issue exists at all is
unknown — it has neither been established nor ruled out, because it hasn't
been analysed. Staff exhaustion is a real and worth-taking-seriously signal,
but on its own it cannot be converted into evidence of caller-side or
service-side impact. Nothing in this material indicates immediate danger, so
no immediate safety action is triggered.
