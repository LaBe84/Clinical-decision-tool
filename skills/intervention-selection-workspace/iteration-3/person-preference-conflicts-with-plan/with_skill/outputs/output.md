INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Ensure the caller has an escalation step in her safety plan that she will
actually use in a crisis. The current specified step (call the crisis line)
is the established target of the plan; what's in question is whether that
specific step, as written, is still the right way to achieve it — not
whether an escalation step is needed at all.

Established basis
Stated preference: the caller has directly told us she does not want to keep
using the crisis-line step, that re-explaining her situation to a new person
each time is itself distressing, and that she would strongly prefer
contacting one specific trusted person instead. This is a direct, explicit
statement from the person the plan is for, not a hypothesis or an inference
about her motives. No upstream PORF, Suicide Enquiry, Contact Pattern
Analysis, or CRG Triage material is referenced here, so this decision rests
on the stated preference and the existing plan alone; anything about her
current risk state or contact pattern remains whatever those skills last
established, unchanged by this input.

Selected response
Destination 2 — modify current response. The target (a workable escalation
step in the safety plan) appears right; the current implementation of it
(mandating the crisis line specifically) is not being followed as intended
because the caller finds it distressing, which is itself a plausible reason
a plan step fails in practice. That is grounds to revisit how the step is
written, not grounds to drop the escalation step or to leave the plan as
written on the assumption she'll use it anyway.

This skill does not decide what the revised step should say. Whether her
preferred contact is used as the sole primary step, an alternate first step,
or a supplement to the crisis line, and how any risk introduced by relying
on one named individual is addressed, is Safety Planning's determination,
not this one.

Mechanism
We are revisiting the specified escalation step (X) because we expect that a
step the caller is willing to actually use, rather than one she avoids due
to the distress of repeated re-explanation, will increase the likelihood the
plan is followed at the point of crisis (Y), through better fit between the
plan and her stated barriers to using it (Z). This is a mechanism for why
the step should be reviewed — it is not itself the redesigned step.

Proportionality
A caller's direct statement that a specified step is distressing enough that
she wouldn't use it is meaningful clinical information about a plan's
workability, not a preference to override by default. Revisiting the
wording of one step in an existing plan is proportionate to that
information; it does not require reopening the whole plan or escalating to
a different care pathway.

Risks / unintended effects
Substituting a single named trusted person for a professional crisis line
could narrow the escalation pathway to one point of failure (unavailability,
burnout, or the trusted person being unable to manage a crisis-level call),
and could shift risk onto an untrained individual without the caller's plan
accounting for that. It could also, conversely, genuinely improve the
plan's real-world use if the current step is being avoided altogether.
Neither of these is established here — they are exactly the kind of
trade-off Safety Planning needs to weigh when redesigning the step, not
something to resolve by refusing the preference outright or by adopting it
unexamined.

Ownership
Safety Planning — this skill names that the specified escalation step should
be reviewed and modified; it does not specify what the new step should say,
whether the trusted person becomes primary or supplementary, or how any new
single-point-of-failure risk should be mitigated.

Review
We will know this is helping if the caller uses the revised plan step during
a subsequent crisis rather than avoiding contact altogether. We will
reconsider it if the trusted person is unavailable, unable to manage what's
disclosed, or if reliance on them appears to be increasing rather than
reducing risk. Review by: next contact where the plan is discussed, per
Safety Planning's own schedule.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the caller's stated distress about re-explaining her situation
reflects a broader pattern relevant to Contact Pattern Analysis, and what
current Risk State or triage destination is, are not established by this
input and are not addressed here. Whether the trusted person is willing,
suitable, and briefed to hold this role is also unknown and belongs to
Safety Planning's assessment, not this decision.
