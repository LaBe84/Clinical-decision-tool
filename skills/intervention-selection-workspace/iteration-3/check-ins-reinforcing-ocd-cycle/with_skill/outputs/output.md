INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Reduce reinforcement of an escalating checking/reassurance-seeking contact
pattern that is not resolving the caller's underlying anxiety. This part of
the target is supported directly by the established Contact Pattern Analysis
facts (repeated calls, each producing only temporary relief, frequency
intensifying over weeks) independent of any diagnostic label.

What is NOT established: that this is an OCD-type reassurance-seeking
mechanism specifically. CPA has described the pattern as "resembling"
OCD-type reassurance-seeking — that is a hypothesis about function, not a
confirmed formulation. This skill preserves that distinction rather than
treating "reassurance-seeking" as the settled basis for what follows.

Established basis
- Established (upstream CPA fact): repeated calls, each call producing
  temporary anxiety relief, checking frequency intensifying over weeks.
- Hypothesis, explicitly flagged as such by CPA: the pattern resembles
  OCD-type reassurance-seeking. Not confirmed. Not usable below as the
  stated basis for a specific treatment modality.
- Not established: current risk state, any change in risk, or a diagnosis.
  This skill has not been given, and has not sought, findings from PORF,
  Suicide Enquiry, or CRG Triage — if those are needed they are Clinical
  Workflow Router's call, not named here.

Selected response
The proposed intervention — adding more frequent scheduled check-in calls
"to get ahead of it" — is not selected. It is contraindicated on the facts
already established, independent of whether the OCD hypothesis is ever
confirmed:

- It uses contact frequency as the lever, which the intensifying pattern
  itself (more calls, same or greater distress) suggests is not currently
  working as a lever for this presentation.
- The stated intensification is happening under a pattern of contact already
  providing relief-then-escalation; adding more of the same kind of contact,
  on a schedule, adds another source of temporary relief into a pattern
  already trending upward. That is the shape of a reinforcing response, not
  a corrective one.
- It is motivated by a defensible pressure ("get ahead of it," reduce
  uncertainty for staff) but that pressure is not itself evidence the
  intervention is indicated.

Instead: destination 6, combined with routing to CMP/Access Review once the
target is clarified.

1. Seek additional information before selecting an intervention (destination
   6) — specifically, confirmation of the pattern's actual function. CPA's
   hypothesis is plausible but unconfirmed, and the choice between a
   structured, boundaried contact response and some other response depends
   on which function is actually operating. Naming this gap is as far as
   this skill goes; whether that confirmation comes from a further CPA pass
   or elsewhere is Clinical Workflow Router's decision, not this skill's.
2. Once a target is confirmed, any change to how/when contact happens
   (e.g., a structured or boundaried contact plan rather than an open-ended
   increase) is an access-arrangement question with a required purpose,
   expected effect, and review mechanism — that is CMP/Access Review's
   territory. This skill names that as the likely destination; it does not
   design the plan.

Mechanism
For the proposed response as stated ("more scheduled check-ins"): mechanism
cannot be completed coherently. The claim would have to be "we are adding
more check-in calls because we expect it to reduce anxiety-driven checking
through more reassurance" — but the established pattern (repeated
reassurance already correlating with escalation, not reduction) points the
other way. A mechanism that runs counter to the evidence in hand fails this
domain; the response is not ready to propose regardless of good intent.

Proportionality
Not assessable as "right-sized" because the target and mechanism aren't
settled well enough to size a response against. Proportionality can't be
judged for an intervention that hasn't cleared the mechanism test — sizing
comes after target and mechanism, not before.

Risks / unintended effects
- Increasing dependency: scheduled reassurance contact risks entrenching the
  checking behaviour it's meant to relieve, particularly if the reassurance-
  seeking hypothesis turns out to be correct.
- Rewarding escalation-linked contact: more contact offered in direct
  response to an intensifying pattern can read, functionally, as the pattern
  being rewarded rather than addressed.
- False reassurance to staff: more scheduled contact may reduce staff
  anxiety and make the case "look" more managed without any established
  therapeutic benefit — service containment should not be read as evidence
  the approach is working.
- Fragmented ownership: adding contact informally, outside a reviewed access
  plan, risks the same drift CMP/Access Review exists to prevent.

Ownership
- Confirming the pattern's function: route back to Clinical Workflow Router
  to determine the appropriate next upstream step.
- Any change to contact structure once a target is confirmed: CMP/Access
  Review.
- This skill does not take ownership of designing the contact plan itself.

Review
- We will know a target is ready to act on if the function of the pattern
  (reassurance-seeking vs. another driver) is confirmed rather than
  hypothesised.
- We will reconsider the "no action yet" position if the intensifying
  pattern coincides with any indication of increasing risk — in which case
  this routes outside this skill entirely (immediate safety pathway) rather
  than being decided here.
- Review by: next scheduled case review, or sooner if contact frequency or
  distress escalates further before then.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the pattern is genuinely OCD-type reassurance-seeking, a different
anxiety-driven mechanism, a response to some unaddressed concrete concern, or
something else entirely, is not known. The single clearest fact in hand —
relief is temporary and frequency is climbing — is enough to say "don't add
more of the same kind of contact," but not enough, by itself, to say what
should replace it. That gap is the reason this lands on destination 6 rather
than a named destination-3 addition.
