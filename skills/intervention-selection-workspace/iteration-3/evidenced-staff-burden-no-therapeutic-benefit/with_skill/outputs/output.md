INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
No clinical target can be stated for restricting this caller's access. There
is no established evidence that reducing this caller's contact would produce
any therapeutic benefit — and their PORF formulation indicates the opposite:
contact currently functions as one of their few stabilising factors. A
target of "reduce this caller's clinical need" cannot be written, because no
such need for reduced contact has been established.

Separately, an operational target does exist: reduce the impact of this
caller's contact volume on other callers' wait times. That target is real
and evidenced, but it is a service-delivery target, not a clinical one, and
must be named as such rather than folded into this caller's care plan.

Established basis
- Established (Contact Pattern Analysis, confirmed by call logs): this
  caller's contact volume is measurably associated with documented instances
  of extended wait times for other callers. This is operational evidence,
  not a clinical finding about this caller.
- Established (PORF formulation): no evidence that reduced contact would
  benefit this caller therapeutically; contact currently functions as a
  stabilising factor.
- Nothing in the material establishes a clinical contraindication to this
  caller's current contact pattern from their own perspective — the only
  contraindication identified is to restricting it.

Selected response
Do not restrict this caller's access on the basis presented. The evidence
supports an operational (service-delivery) concern, not a clinical basis for
changing this individual's care or access. Maintain current contact
arrangements for this caller pending any separate, explicitly operational
process.

The documented service impact is a legitimate matter for the service to
address — but as an access-arrangement question it is not this skill's
territory to decide. Whether, and how, a service-level response is
appropriate (e.g. capacity, rostering, or a formally reviewed access
arrangement with explicit purpose, expected effect, and review mechanism) is
CMP/Access Review's decision, not something to resolve inline here.

Mechanism
No mechanism sentence can be completed for restricting this caller's access,
because no target for change in this caller's presentation has been
established. "We are restricting access because we expect it to influence
[this caller's presentation] through [mechanism]" cannot be filled in — the
only established effect of reduced contact anticipated by the formulation is
removal of a stabilising factor, which is the opposite of a therapeutic
mechanism.

Proportionality
An access restriction is a significant change to an individual's care and,
per the formulation, risks removing a stabilising factor for no established
clinical gain to that person. Proportionality has to be judged against this
caller's established need, not against how disruptive their volume is to
service delivery. Evidenced service impact is a real operational fact, but
by itself it does not establish that restricting this caller is
proportionate to their clinical situation.

Risks / unintended effects
- Destabilising a caller whose contact is currently functioning as a
  stabilising factor, per PORF — a plausible competing safety problem
  created by the "solution" itself.
- Treating documented staff/service burden as if it were evidence of
  clinical need, which it is not — service-level considerations may be a
  legitimate operational factor but must not be treated as evidence of
  therapeutic benefit or as sufficient justification, by themselves, for
  changing an individual's care or access.
- Leaving the underlying operational problem (wait-time impact on other
  callers) unaddressed if it is dismissed rather than routed to the process
  that can properly weigh and act on it.

Ownership
- This caller's care: no change; current response maintained.
- The operational service-impact question: CMP/Access Review, if a formal
  access-arrangement change is to be considered at all — that skill's own
  discipline requires explicit purpose, expected effect, and review
  mechanism, and requires weighing the established contraindication
  identified here rather than treating operational impact as sufficient
  justification on its own.

Review
Not applicable to this caller's clinical care, since no intervention is
being changed. If CMP/Access Review takes up the operational question
separately, that review would need its own criteria — not specified here,
as that is outside this skill's remit.

Residual uncertainty
- Whether there is a way to address the service-delivery impact that does
  not involve changing this caller's individual access or care (e.g.
  operational/capacity measures) has not been explored here and isn't this
  skill's territory.
- Whether the stabilising function identified in PORF would hold under any
  future change in this caller's presentation is unknown and not assessed
  by this decision.
