INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not established as a clinical target. The stated rationale for this proposal is reducing the caller's contact with the helpline in order to reduce staff workload. Staff workload is a service-level/operational consideration, not a clinical intervention target in its own right (see Boundary rules: service-level considerations "must not be treated as evidence of therapeutic benefit or as sufficient justification, by themselves, for changing an individual's care or access"). No clinical target — e.g. reduced risk, improved regulation, increased use of an appropriate alternative coping resource — has been stated or established for this caller. If a genuine clinical target exists (for example, that current contact is reinforcing a counterproductive pattern, per an established Contact Pattern Analysis finding), it has not been supplied here and cannot be assumed.

Established basis
None supplied. What is given is an operational motive (reduce staff workload) plus two facts that cut against, rather than support, the proposed response: (1) the destination service has a long waiting list, and (2) the caller is currently using helpline contact as their main source of stability. Nothing in the material establishes that the caller's use of the helpline is itself clinically counterproductive, nor that the external service is presently able to deliver an equivalent or superior function. This is a case where the pressure to act ("reduce load") must not be read as evidence that this specific action is indicated.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an intervention. As proposed, this response is not ready to select. Before any change to this caller's access to helpline contact is considered, the following would need to be established: a clinical (not operational) target for reducing contact; the caller's current risk state and function of contact (from upstream Suicide Enquiry / PORF / Contact Pattern Analysis, if not already established); and confirmation that the receiving service can actually deliver support within a timeframe that does not leave a stability gap. None of this is stated as available.

If, separately, the organisation wants to address staff workload as an operational matter, that is a legitimate operational conversation — but it must be named as operational and pursued through service-level channels (e.g. staffing, triage capacity), not enacted as an individual clinical intervention for this caller.

Mechanism
Cannot be completed. The candidate sentence would be "We are reducing this caller's helpline contact because we expect it to influence [workload] through [referral to an external service]" — but workload is a service outcome, not a clinical outcome for the caller, so this does not satisfy the required "X influences Y through Z" test for an intervention aimed at the caller's needs. No mechanism by which reduced helpline contact is expected to improve this caller's stability, regulation, or risk has been offered; the available evidence points the other way (helpline contact is currently their main source of stability).

Proportionality
Cannot be assessed because no clinical target has been established. Proportionality is measured against an established need, not against staff burden — there is nothing here to match the response's intensity to.

Risks / unintended effects
Naming the trade-off explicitly, as required: this intervention would reduce staff workload while shifting risk onto the caller, specifically —
- Removing or reducing an established source of stability before an alternative is actually in place, given the receiving service's long waiting list — a stability gap during the interim period.
- No established equivalence between what the helpline is currently providing and what the external service would provide once (or if) contact begins.
- Risk that the referral functions as service containment (fewer calls, less staff strain) rather than as a clinically justified change in care — the two can look identical from the service's side while having opposite effects on the caller.
- Fragmented ownership during the gap: helpline steps back, external service hasn't yet stepped in, no one is clearly holding the caller's stability in the interim.
- If the caller was not consulted, this also risks overriding an active, apparently working coping strategy (helpline use) without their input.

Ownership
If a genuine clinical target for reducing contact exists, or the caller's current pattern of use needs assessment, that determination belongs upstream (Contact Pattern Analysis / current risk-state finding) — name the gap and route back to Clinical Workflow Router rather than proceeding on the operational rationale alone. Any actual referral pathway, once a target is established, would be designed by the appropriate care-pathway/access process, not specified here.

Review
Not applicable yet — no intervention has been selected. Once a clinical target and mechanism are established (or the operational workload question is pursued separately through proper channels), a review criterion should specify: what would indicate the caller's stability is maintained through any transition, what would indicate it isn't, and by when the receiving service's capacity/waiting-list status would be reconfirmed before any reduction in helpline contact takes effect.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the caller's current helpline use reflects a counterproductive pattern or an appropriate, working coping strategy is unknown. The external service's actual waiting-list length and interim support offer are unknown. The caller's own preference has not been stated. Current risk state during any transition period is unknown. All of these would need to be established before this proposal could be evaluated on clinical grounds rather than operational ones.
