INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Reduce reported hopelessness. (Taken as the established upstream target for
this intervention — this skill does not re-derive it; if the target was
never formally established upstream rather than just assumed, that is a
prior-skill gap, not something to backfill here.)

Established basis
Established observation, not hypothesis: weekly scheduled supportive calls
have been delivered exactly as designed for two months (perfect adherence),
and the person's reported hopelessness — the specific thing this
intervention was meant to move — has not reduced over that period. This is
a genuine outcome review of the stated target, not an absence of review. It
is different in kind from "effectiveness was never checked."

Selected response
Reduce/stop the current weekly supportive-call intervention as currently
configured — it should not simply continue unchanged. Do not select
"maintain current response": perfect adherence is a fidelity fact, not an
effectiveness fact, and this skill explicitly treats "continuing an
intervention with a perfect adherence record but no demonstrated effect,
simply because it's being followed correctly" as a reasoning shortcut to
avoid rather than a valid basis to continue.

This does not mean support is withdrawn or nothing is offered. Whether the
right next step is to stop this component and replace it with a different,
mechanistically justified intervention, or to modify its content/format
while keeping the target, cannot be decided here — that requires knowing
why it hasn't moved hopelessness (wrong mechanism, wrong dose, wrong
modality, or a target that needs a different kind of intervention
entirely), which is not established in what's given. Route that
determination rather than inventing it: destination 4 for this specific
intervention (stop as currently configured, on the basis below) combined
with destination 6 for what replaces or modifies it.

Mechanism
As designed, the rationale was: "We are doing weekly supportive calls
because we expect them to influence hopelessness through regular
supportive contact and connection." Two months of perfect delivery with no
change in the target means that causal chain has not been demonstrated to
hold in practice. Continuing to deliver the same mechanism unchanged, on
the same rationale, is no longer justified by that mechanism — the
mechanism itself is the thing now in question.

Proportionality
Stopping/reconsidering this specific intervention is proportionate to what
has actually been shown: a defined, deliverable intervention run with full
fidelity for a sustained period without moving its own stated target. This
is not escalation-driven or distress-driven — it's a direct response to an
outcome finding on the exact measure the intervention was chosen to
address. It is also not an argument for doing more of the same or for
withdrawing support altogether; both would be disproportionate in the
opposite direction.

Risks / unintended effects
- Stopping abruptly without a replacement could itself be destabilising or
  read by the person as withdrawal of support, independent of whether the
  calls were "working" — any change needs to be paired with what replaces
  it, not left as a gap.
- Concluding "ineffective" too strongly could obscure a dosing or format
  problem (e.g., the right target, wrong mechanism) rather than a target
  problem — worth distinguishing before assuming the target itself needs a
  different approach.
- Conversely, quietly continuing unchanged risks reinforcing a service
  routine ("we're doing something") without therapeutic benefit, and risks
  reading as service containment (case looks stable/quiet) rather than
  actual change in the person's hopelessness.

Ownership
Next step (what replaces or modifies the call schedule, and any safety-plan
implications) belongs to Clinical Workflow Router to determine what
upstream input is needed, and from there to whichever of Safety Planning /
CMP-Access Review / Contact Pattern Analysis is indicated once the missing
information is available. This skill names the destination only, not the
replacement's content.

Review
We will know a replacement/modified approach is helping if reported
hopelessness shows measurable movement within a defined follow-up window
(the length of that window is not established here and should be set
explicitly when the next intervention is chosen — do not assume two months
was itself a validated trial length; nothing in the material establishes an
expected time course for this intervention type).
We will reconsider again if the next approach also shows perfect delivery
with no change in the target.
Review by: date to be set when the replacement/modified intervention is
selected.

Residual uncertainty
- Whether the two-month window constitutes an adequate trial period for
  this type of intervention is not established — the "no effect" finding is
  real, but the field doesn't specify an expected time course, so this
  should not be read as proof the intervention could never have worked
  given more time, only that it hasn't worked so far.
- Why the mechanism didn't produce change (wrong modality, wrong dose,
  wrong target-for-this-person, or a target that isn't responsive to
  supportive contact at all) is unknown and needs upstream input before a
  replacement can be selected.
- Whether the person's own preference or experience of the calls (helpful,
  neutral, unhelpful) has been captured is not stated and is relevant to
  what comes next.
