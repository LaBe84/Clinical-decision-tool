INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. No target has been stated in terms of what needs to
change for the caller — no shift in Risk State, coping capacity, or
engagement pattern has been named. The only stated driver for this proposal
is a reduction in helpline contact volume, which is a service-load metric,
not a clinical target. Reducing staff workload is not a legitimate
intervention target under this skill.

Established basis
What is established: the caller is currently using helpline contact as
their main source of stability during this period, and the proposed
referral is to an external service with a long waiting list. No upstream
formulation (PORF/Suicide Enquiry/Contact Pattern Analysis/CRG Triage) has
been supplied indicating that current contact is reinforcing an unhelpful
pattern, that risk has changed, or that the caller's needs exceed what the
helpline can appropriately provide. Absent that material, there is no
established clinical basis for reducing contact now.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information / do not select this
intervention as proposed. This proposal should not proceed in its current
form. Before any change to contact arrangements is considered, the
following need to be established: (a) a current formulation of why the
existing contact pattern is being treated as a problem rather than as a
currently-functioning stabiliser, (b) confirmation of realistic wait-list
timeframe and interim cover from the external service, and (c) whether
referral is being proposed as a genuinely indicated care-pathway addition
or as a workload-reduction measure dressed as one.

As framed, this proposal fails the boundary rules before it reaches the six
domains: it treats reduced helpline contact as the goal rather than as a
possible side effect of a properly justified intervention, and it does so
during a period explicitly described as the caller relying on that contact
for stability. That combination — cutting the thing currently providing
stability, replacing it with a resource that will not be available for an
unspecified long period — is a service-containment move, not a
therapeutic one, and this skill treats that distinction as decisive.

Mechanism
No coherent mechanism can be completed. The candidate sentence would be:
"We are reducing helpline contact and referring to [external service]
because we expect it to influence [reduced staff workload] through
[transfer of caller's care elsewhere]." This does not resolve to a Y that
is a clinical outcome for the caller — Y is a staffing outcome. A
mechanism sentence with the caller's wellbeing as the target cannot
currently be written, because during the wait-list period the caller would
lose their current main source of stability with nothing established to
replace it. That gap is not a detail to fill in later; it is the reason
this intervention is not ready to propose.

Proportionality
Not assessable, because no target has been established to be proportionate
to. Separately: even treating "appropriate step-down to specialist
services" as a plausible eventual target, timing this against a waiting
list — during a period explicitly identified as the caller's main source
of stability — is disproportionate to any target that could be named,
since it front-loads the loss (of current support) before the caller has
the replacement.

Risks / unintended effects
- Removes or reduces the caller's current main source of stability before
  any replacement is actually in place, with a named risk of destabilising
  someone who is not currently in crisis about their support arrangement.
- Fragments ownership: the caller is nominally referred elsewhere but that
  service will not be able to see them for a long period, creating a gap in
  which no service is actively holding the case.
- Service containment masquerading as clinical benefit: the proposal is
  explicitly staff-workload-motivated. Even if the caller's presentation
  looks quieter after handoff, that would reflect the helpline's exposure
  going down, not the caller's need going down — this is exactly the
  distinction the boundary rules require not to conflate.
- Risk of the caller experiencing this as rejection or withdrawal of
  support at a point of reliance, which could itself increase risk or
  distress, i.e. the intervention could produce the opposite of its
  unstated implicit aim.
- If contact is reduced and no interim safety/stability arrangement is
  specified, this could constitute an access change made without the
  purpose, expected effect, and review mechanism being explicit — which
  routes to CMP/Access Review, not this skill, and should not be decided
  here.

Ownership
Not assigned — no intervention is being selected. If the underlying
question ("should this caller's helpline access change, and should an
external referral be added") is to be pursued, it needs: (1) a formulation
input naming what, if anything, is currently going wrong with the existing
contact pattern (route: Contact Pattern Analysis / CRG Triage), and (2) if
an access change is still indicated once that's established, that specific
question routes to CMP/Access Review rather than being decided inline here.

Review
Not applicable — no intervention has been selected, so there is nothing to
review for effect. What should happen instead: revisit this proposal once
(a) an upstream formulation states an actual clinical or engagement-pattern
target, and (b) either the wait-list timeframe is short enough to bridge
safely, or an explicit interim stabilisation arrangement is specified for
the caller's care during the wait, with its own review point.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the current helpline contact pattern is itself clinically
indicated to continue, reduce, or change is genuinely unknown from what's
been provided — this assessment does not conclude "leave everything as is
forever," only that a workload-driven referral to a long wait-list, timed
against a stated reliance for stability, is not a justified intervention as
currently framed. Also unknown: what specifically would happen to the
caller's support during the wait-list gap, and whether the caller has
expressed any preference about this referral, which has not been reported
and should be sought before any further decision.
