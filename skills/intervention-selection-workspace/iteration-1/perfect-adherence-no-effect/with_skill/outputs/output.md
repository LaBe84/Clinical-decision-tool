INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Reduce reported hopelessness. This is the stated treatment target the weekly
supportive calls were designed against, and it is the only target in scope
here — this skill is not re-deriving it, just testing the intervention
against it.

Established basis
Two months of weekly scheduled supportive calls, delivered exactly as
designed, with perfect adherence. Over that period there is no demonstrated
change in the target measure (hopelessness has not reduced). That is the
entire evidentiary basis available: a completed, well-implemented trial of
the intervention that has not moved the outcome it exists to move.

What is explicitly *not* part of the basis: adherence quality is not
evidence of effectiveness. A stable, faithfully-executed pattern is not the
same as an effective intervention (carried from CMP/Access Review
discipline) — so "it was followed correctly" cannot be used here to justify
continuing it. Two months is also long enough to be a fair trial for a
supportive-contact intervention aimed at a subjective target like
hopelessness; this isn't a case of stopping prematurely before an effect
could plausibly show.

Selected response
Do not continue this intervention unchanged. The default answer implied by
"it's been followed perfectly" — keep going — is precisely the reasoning
shortcut this skill flags as unsafe: continuing an intervention with a
perfect adherence record but no demonstrated effect, simply because it's
being followed correctly.

However, the material as given doesn't distinguish between two different
next steps, and the skill draws a hard boundary against picking one just to
have an answer:

- Destination 4 (Reduce/stop) is correct if the mechanism itself was wrong —
  i.e., weekly supportive calls were never plausibly going to move
  hopelessness for this person, regardless of how well they were delivered.
- Destination 2 (Modify current response) is correct if the target is right
  but the implementation needs to change — e.g., the calls address contact
  and containment but not the cognitive/behavioural drivers of hopelessness
  specifically (agency, reinforcement of helplessness, absence of any active
  skills component), so the format needs to change even though the broad aim
  (address hopelessness through regular contact) stays.

Nothing in the facts given establishes which of these is true — that
requires going back to why the calls were expected to work in the first
place (the original mechanism statement, if one exists) and to any
formulation detail about what is maintaining the hopelessness now. Selecting
between "stop" and "modify" without that would be manufacturing precision
the evidence doesn't support. So the operative destination is:

**Destination 6 — seek additional information before finalising the
replacement response** — combined with an immediate, firm conclusion that
maintains nothing: continuing the intervention exactly as-is (Destination 1)
is not supportable on this evidence and should not be the default while that
information is gathered.

Mechanism
The original mechanism (whatever justified starting weekly supportive
calls) has not been confirmed by two months of adherent delivery — the
intervention was run as designed and did not produce the expected effect on
Y (hopelessness) through whatever Z (e.g., "reduces isolation, which reduces
hopelessness") was originally proposed. That failure to confirm the
mechanism is itself the key finding: it tells us the causal chain assumed at
the outset is not operating as expected, not that more of the same chain is
needed. No new mechanism can be stated yet for a replacement/modified
response — that has to come from re-examining why the original one didn't
hold, which is outside this skill's remit (it consumes formulation, it
doesn't redo it).

Proportionality
Proportionality cuts against maintaining the status quo: two months of a
resource-committing weekly contact intervention with a null result on its
own target is disproportionate to continue without re-justification.
Equally, escalating to something more intensive (Destination 5) is not
supported either — there's no evidence here that the required intervention
is undeliverable at the current level, only that the current intervention
choice hasn't worked. Absence of resolution is not evidence the response
needs to be bigger.

Risks / unintended effects
- Continuing unchanged risks reinforcing a contact pattern that has become
  ritual rather than therapeutic — the calls may now be serving a
  containment function (case looks stable, staff feel reassured) rather than
  a treatment function, which the skill explicitly warns against mistaking
  for benefit.
- Stopping abruptly without a modified alternative risks the person
  experiencing withdrawal of a relationship they may value, independent of
  whether it's moving the hopelessness measure — that's a live
  contraindication to weigh once more information is available, not a
  reason to keep the current format running by default.
- Modifying without first understanding why the original mechanism failed
  risks repeating the same non-result in a new format.

Ownership
Case holder/named worker to review the original formulation and PORF/prior
material behind the weekly-calls decision, and to bring back: (a) what
mechanism was originally proposed, (b) whether anything in the current
presentation suggests what is actually maintaining the hopelessness now,
and (c) the person's own view of whether the calls feel useful. This
information gathering — not a new intervention — is the immediate next
action.

Review
- We will know a next step is ready to select once the original mechanism
  has been checked against two months of null results and either confirmed
  as still plausible (→ modify format) or disconfirmed (→ stop and consider
  a differently-targeted response).
- We will reconsider maintaining the current calls immediately — they should
  not continue unexamined past this review point.
- We will complete this review within [timeframe to be set by case
  holder/team, not specified in the material given] and revisit via this
  skill once the additional information is in hand.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the calls have been mechanistically wrong from the start, or
directionally right but insufficient/wrongly formatted, cannot be
determined from "two months, perfect adherence, no change" alone. The
person's own experience of the calls (helpful-but-not-enough vs.
not-relevant-to-the-hopelessness) is unknown and materially affects which
destination applies. Whether anything else has changed for this person over
the two months (that might independently explain a stable hopelessness
score) is also unknown.
