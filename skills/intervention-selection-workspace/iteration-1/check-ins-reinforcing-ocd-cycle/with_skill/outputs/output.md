INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Reduce reinforcement of the unhelpful contact/checking pattern — i.e.,
reduce the frequency and reassurance-value of reassurance-seeking calls
without withdrawing appropriate support. This is the target implied by
Contact Pattern Analysis's finding (a reassurance-seeking pattern that is
intensifying), not "reduce distress" and not "increase contact."

Established basis
Contact Pattern Analysis has identified a pattern consistent with OCD-type
reassurance-seeking: repeated calls, each producing short-term anxiety
relief, with the checking pattern intensifying over weeks. This is the
textbook signature of a negative-reinforcement cycle — the call relieves
anxiety in the moment, which strengthens the urge to call again, which is
why the pattern is escalating rather than resolving despite (in fact
because of) repeated contact. That the caller remains distressed between
calls is not evidence the current level of contact is insufficient; in this
pattern it is more consistent with the response itself maintaining the
distress it's meant to relieve.

Selected response
The proposed intervention — adding more frequent scheduled check-in calls
"to get ahead of it" — is rejected as proposed. It fails the mechanism and
contraindication tests below and should not go forward in this form.

In its place: Destination 2, modify current response, combined with
Destination 5, escalate to an appropriate pathway. The current contact
arrangement should be restructured (not simply intensified or simply cut)
so that it stops functioning as a reassurance-delivery mechanism, and the
case should be routed to whoever holds clinical ownership of OCD-type
presentations (GP / community mental health / psychological therapies,
ERP-informed) for a specific plan on how contact should be structured,
since designing an exposure/response-prevention-consistent contact protocol
is clinical-pathway territory, not something this skill invents inline.
Destination 6 elements remain: the exact target frequency and scripted
response for in-between contact are not yet established here and should
come from that clinical input rather than being guessed at on the helpline
side.

Mechanism
The proposed response fails this test rather than passing it. "We would add
more scheduled check-ins because we expect it to influence anxiety through
increased reassurance" is mechanistically incoherent given the established
pattern — increased reassurance is the mechanism sustaining the problem,
not a mechanism for resolving it. More frequent contact would influence Y
(short-term anxiety) through Z (reassurance), which is precisely the loop
Contact Pattern Analysis flagged as intensifying.

The modified response instead: "We are restructuring contact (moving from
open-ended reassurance-on-demand toward a bounded, clinically-designed
contact protocol, with the specific parameters set by the OCD-appropriate
pathway) because we expect it to reduce reinforcement of the checking cycle
through decreasing the frequency and reassurance-content of each contact,
consistent with an ERP-informed approach." This is a placeholder for the
receiving pathway's plan, not a fully specified intervention this skill is
authoring itself — see Residual uncertainty.

Proportionality
The proposed escalation (more frequent check-ins) is disproportionate in
the wrong direction: it matches the intensity of the caller's felt urgency
and staff's wish to "get ahead of it," not the intensity actually indicated
by the formulation. Nothing in Contact Pattern Analysis establishes a
change in acute risk that would justify more contact: this is a contact-
pattern/maintenance problem, not a new risk finding. Proportionality here
means matching the response to what maintains the cycle (reinforcement
structure), not to how uncomfortable it is to leave the current call
volume unchanged while a clinical plan is sought.

Risks / unintended effects
- Reinforcing escalation-linked contact: more scheduled check-ins directly
  rewards the checking behaviour with more reassurance, which on this
  formulation would be expected to intensify the pattern further, not
  "get ahead of it."
- Increasing dependency: scheduled reassurance normalises the idea that
  anxiety requires an external check to be tolerated, undermining any
  future work on tolerating uncertainty.
- Undermining a future care plan: if this case moves toward ERP-informed
  treatment, a helpline-driven habit of frequent reassurance calls will
  actively work against that treatment's mechanism.
- Service containment mistaken for benefit: more check-ins may make the
  caller calmer and staff less anxious in the short term while making the
  underlying pattern worse — exactly the trap this skill's boundary rules
  warn against.
- Doing nothing until the clinical pathway responds also carries risk if
  distress is significant in the interim — this is why "modify current
  response" (bounded, non-reinforcing contact) is paired with escalation
  rather than simply withdrawing contact and waiting.

Ownership
- Helpline/service holding current contact: implement the bounded,
  non-open-ended contact structure once parameters are set; hold the line
  against ad hoc increases in check-in frequency in the meantime.
- Referral to GP / community mental health / psychological therapies
  (ERP-informed): own design of the specific contact protocol and any
  treatment plan for the reassurance-seeking pattern itself.
- Whoever proposed the "more frequent check-ins" plan: needs this
  formulation fed back before it is actioned, since it was proposed in
  good faith without visibility into the reinforcement mechanism.

Review
- We will know this is helping if the frequency and intensity of
  reassurance-seeking calls trend down over successive weeks (the same
  metric Contact Pattern Analysis used to identify the problem, now used
  in reverse).
- We will reconsider it if the pattern continues to intensify despite a
  restructured, non-reinforcing contact protocol, or if between-contact
  distress escalates to a level suggesting a genuine change in risk state
  (which would trigger Suicide Enquiry / CRG Triage re-assessment, not a
  bigger version of this intervention).
- We will review by the next scheduled Contact Pattern Analysis pass, and
  immediately if the referral pathway has not responded within a
  clinically reasonable window (service to define, not defaulted here).

Residual uncertainty
- The exact parameters of a non-reinforcing contact protocol (how much
  contact, what the caller is told during it, how "reassurance" is
  distinguished from legitimate check-in content) are not established by
  this skill and should not be guessed at — they belong to whoever holds
  the ERP-informed clinical pathway.
- Whether this caller has an existing OCD diagnosis or formal treatment
  relationship is not stated in the material provided; this affects who
  the referral actually goes to and how urgently.
- Whether any acute risk factors exist alongside the reassurance-seeking
  pattern is not addressed here — this skill consumes Contact Pattern
  Analysis's conclusion as given and has not re-run Suicide Enquiry or
  Risk Formulation; if those are not current, that gap should be closed
  separately, not folded into this intervention decision.
