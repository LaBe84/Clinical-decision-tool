INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. "Staff feel exhausted by a particular caller" names a
staff experience, not a change target. No target such as reduced
service-delivery impact, reduced reinforcement of an unhelpful contact
pattern, or reduced risk has been stated or evidenced.

Established basis
Only staff-reported fatigue toward this caller. No Contact Pattern Analysis
has been done, so there is no documented pattern (frequency, timing,
escalation-linked contact, reinforcement dynamics) to act on. No evidence has
been documented that other callers are being affected, that service delivery
is measurably degraded, or that this caller's contact is inappropriate
relative to need. Staff fatigue is a real and legitimate signal that
attention is needed, but on its own it is not evidence that access
restriction — or any specific intervention — is indicated.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek additional information before selecting an
intervention. Access should not be restricted on the basis of what's
currently documented. Specifically, before any intervention (restriction or
otherwise) can be proposed:
- Complete a Contact Pattern Analysis (frequency, timing, triggers, content,
  trend over time, relationship to risk state).
- Document whether there is actual, specific evidence of service-delivery
  impact — other callers unable to get through, response times affected,
  identifiable operational harm — rather than staff distress alone.
- Separately, staff fatigue is a legitimate welfare issue in its own right
  and can be addressed through supervision, debrief, or workload
  redistribution without it being reframed as a caller-access problem.

If, after that work, a pattern and a genuine service-delivery impact are
established, access restriction would then be CMP/Access Review's territory
to design (purpose, expected effect, review mechanism) — not decided here
and not decided now.

Mechanism
Not applicable — no intervention is being proposed. Restricting access
without an established pattern and evidenced impact would not have a
statable causal mechanism ("we restrict access because we expect it to
influence X through Y") beyond "it might make staff feel less exhausted,"
which is a containment effect on staff, not a demonstrated therapeutic or
service-protective effect tied to an established problem.

Proportionality
Not applicable at this stage. Proportionality can't be assessed against an
unestablished target. Acting now, on staff fatigue alone, would be a
response calibrated to staff distress rather than to the caller's need or
any evidenced service problem — exactly the kind of defensive escalation
this skill is designed to block.

Risks / unintended effects
Restricting access now, without evidence, risks: punishing the caller for
help-seeking rather than addressing an established problem; damaging the
therapeutic relationship and potentially increasing risk if the caller loses
a coping resource that was actually working; setting a precedent that staff
fatigue alone justifies access changes, which erodes the evidentiary bar for
future cases; and treating a staff-welfare issue as a caller-management
issue, leaving the actual staff-support need unaddressed. It also risks
disguising service containment (staff feel relief) as therapeutic or
operational benefit, which this skill's boundary rules explicitly warn
against.

Ownership
- Team lead/supervisor: commission Contact Pattern Analysis and gather any
  concrete evidence of service-delivery impact.
- Supervisor: open a separate staff-support conversation (debrief,
  supervision, workload distribution) addressing the fatigue directly,
  independent of any decision about the caller's access.
- Do not assign access-restriction ownership yet — there is nothing for
  CMP/Access Review to action until the above is established.

Review
Reconvene once Contact Pattern Analysis is complete and any service-delivery
evidence is documented (or confirmed absent). At that point, re-run this
skill with the new material to determine whether a target and mechanism can
be stated. No fixed date is set for an access decision because none is yet
warranted; the staff-support conversation should happen promptly (within the
next few days) regardless of the outcome of the pattern analysis.

Residual uncertainty
Whether this caller's contact pattern is actually unusual or reinforcing is
unknown. Whether other callers are in fact being affected is unknown. Whether
the caller's needs are being adequately met by current contact is unknown.
Whether staff fatigue reflects a genuine, disproportionate demand from this
caller or a broader team capacity/support issue is unknown. All of these
remain open until the named information-gathering steps are completed.
