INTERVENTION SELECTION

Target
Not yet established. The intervention target for the fortnightly check-in
calls was never stated at set-up eighteen months ago and has not been
articulated since, so there is nothing here to test the current response
against.

Established basis
PORF has established (and is not being re-litigated here) that presentation
is stable relative to baseline, with no material change in Risk State and no
new plan/intent. That finding supports only one conclusion directly: there is
no basis to intensify or escalate. It does not, by itself, establish that the
existing fortnightly check-in arrangement is proportionate, effective, or
should continue unchanged — those are separate questions the PORF finding
does not answer. Separately, the intervention has been running for eighteen
months with no target, mechanism, or review criterion ever set, and the
question actually being asked here — "should the current intervention
continue?" — is an explicit continuation question.

Selected response
Destination 6 — seek the missing review information before selecting an
intervention. This is not a stable-pattern "maintain" case: the threshold for
destination 6 is met on two independent grounds — (a) the intervention has
run for a prolonged period (18 months) with no established target,
mechanism, or review basis, and (b) continuation is explicitly under
question in the scenario as posed. Per the destination-6 threshold rule, when
either condition holds, the missing review is decision-critical rather than
a footnote, so destination 1 (maintain) is not available here even though no
one is proposing escalation and no failure signal has been identified.
Nothing is proposed yet in terms of add/modify/stop.

Mechanism
Not applicable — a mechanism can't be stated because the target was never
established. This is precisely the gap that has to be resolved before a
mechanism sentence ("we are doing X because we expect it to influence Y
through Z") could be completed honestly.

Proportionality
Not assessable yet. Proportionality is judged against a stated target; with
no target on record for this intervention, there is no basis to judge
whether fortnightly contact is too much, too little, or right-sized.

Risks / unintended effects
Continuing an 18-month-old contact arrangement by default, without ever
having named what it's for, risks: service containment being mistaken for
therapeutic benefit (the case looks quiet, which is not the same as the
intervention working); reinforcing a contact pattern that may or may not
still serve any purpose; and drift, where an arrangement persists purely
because stopping it feels riskier than continuing it, rather than because
continuation is justified. Note also the inverse risk: stopping or reducing
the check-ins now, on the basis that effectiveness was never established,
would be an equally unsupported move in the other direction — absence of a
review is a gap to fill, not evidence the intervention is ineffective or
should be withdrawn.

Ownership
This skill names the destination only. Obtaining the missing review
information (what the check-ins were intended to achieve, and whether that
has been assessed against any criterion) is a gap to flag to Clinical
Workflow Router so the appropriate upstream process can supply it — this
skill does not itself run or name a specific upstream skill to fill the gap.

Review
Not yet set — a review criterion cannot be established until a target and
mechanism exist for this intervention. Once target/mechanism information is
obtained, this skill (or a subsequent run of it) should set explicit
"we will know this is helping if / we will reconsider if / we will review
by" criteria before the check-ins continue on an undefined basis further.

Residual uncertainty
Whether the fortnightly check-ins were ever intended to address a specific,
nameable target; whether any informal review has occurred that isn't
reflected in the material provided; and what the person's own current view
of the check-ins is (helpful, neutral, burdensome) — none of this is
established by the facts given. The PORF stability finding tells us there is
no basis to escalate; it does not tell us whether this specific, long-running
intervention is working, and that question remains open pending the missing
review information.
