CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
Eight months of contact history. Usual frequency 4-5 calls per week, spread
across the week rather than clustered. Calls consistently occur in the
evening. Typical duration 15-25 minutes. Recurring content: ongoing
loneliness and low mood, supportive/talking-based contact. Calls typically
end in a planned, counsellor-agreed closure — caller thanks the counsellor
and agrees a plan for the night. No history described of emergency
escalation, pre-call disconnection, or abrupt/counsellor-initiated endings.
This is a well-established baseline, not an inference from a single window.

Observed change
This week the caller made 5 calls on 5 consecutive evenings, rather than the
usual spread of 4-5 calls across the week. This is the only observed change.
Duration (15-25 minutes), content (loneliness, low mood), and ending (calm,
planned, caller-thanked-counsellor-and-agreed-a-plan) are all reported as
unchanged from baseline.

Note on data available: this analysis is based on connected, substantive,
completed contact only (calls that reached a counsellor and ran their usual
course). No information is given about attempted-but-unconnected contact
(pre-call disconnections, abandoned queue attempts) this week or historically.
If such attempts exist and haven't been reviewed, that is a gap, not evidence
either way about the pattern as a whole.

Engagement pattern
Unchanged. Each call this week reportedly showed the same engagement,
content, and calm, planned ending as the established baseline. There is no
report of reduced engagement, rapid recontact after a call ends, distress at
disconnection, or any deviation in how contact concluded.

Possible function — hypotheses only
- Continuation of the caller's established use of the service for
  containment, regulation, and connection around chronic loneliness and low
  mood — the same function the baseline pattern already reflects.
- A short-term increase in need for evening support this week (e.g. a
  harder week, an anniversary, a change in circumstances) that hasn't
  otherwise surfaced in call content.
- Chance clustering — calling on consecutive evenings rather than a spread
  across the week may reflect scheduling/logistics rather than any change in
  underlying state.
These are not distinguished by the data available. Nothing in the material
supports selecting one over the others; the content and ending of the calls
themselves have not changed, which does not favour the "increased need"
hypothesis over the others.

What remains unknown
- Whether any contact attempts this week failed to connect or were
  abandoned before reaching a counsellor (attempted-contact data not
  provided).
- What, if anything, changed in the caller's circumstances this week that
  might explain consecutive-evening calling rather than a spread.
- Whether this is the first time this caller's contact has clustered this
  way in 8 months, or whether occasional clustering has occurred before
  without being flagged — the baseline as described states "usual spread"
  but does not confirm this is a first-time deviation.

Clinical significance
The only observed change is the distribution of otherwise-identical contacts
within a single week (spread across the week versus five consecutive
evenings), while frequency (5 calls, within the existing 4-5/week baseline),
duration, content, and ending all remain unchanged. This is a minor,
single-week variation in clustering, not a change in volume, engagement, or
outcome. On the information given, it does not indicate the existing
formulation or intervention has stopped working — the caller is still
reaching the same supportive endpoint each time. It has not yet been shown
to be a sustained or accelerating change, since it spans one week only.

Therefore
Helpline — the pattern is adequately understood and the current response
remains proportionate. Frequency this week sits within the caller's
established range, and duration, content, and ending are unchanged; a shift
in which evenings the calls fall on, alone, is not evidence of escalation.
This should not be labelled "escalating contact" on the basis of clustering
alone. If clustering persists or extends beyond this week, or if duration,
content, or ending change, that would warrant re-analysis rather than
carrying this label forward unexamined.
