CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
No established baseline is provided. We have no data on this person's contact
frequency, duration, or engagement prior to this 10-day window, so there is
nothing to compare the current window against. Whatever is described below
is a description of a single window, not a change from a known baseline.

Observed change
Over 10 days, 70 attempted contacts were made. Of these:

- Attempted contact: 70 total.
- Connected contact: 6 attempts reached a counsellor.
- Substantive engagement: all 6 connected calls are documented as calm,
  coherent, supportive conversations lasting 10–20 minutes with clear
  closure.
- Completed intervention: on the documented evidence, the 6 connected calls
  appear to have concluded as intended (supportive contact delivered, planned
  closure).
- Pre-call disconnection: 64 attempts (91% of total contact volume)
  terminated during the automated pre-call message, before reaching a
  counsellor. There is no record of what, if anything, the caller said or
  intended during these attempts. No one has reviewed these 64 attempts as a
  group before this analysis.

This is an observation of two very different volumes sitting side by side:
a small, well-documented set of connected calls, and a much larger,
undocumented set of attempts that never became calls.

Engagement pattern
For the 6 connected calls, engagement is known and appears settled: calm,
coherent, supportive, with clear closure each time. For the 64 pre-call
disconnections, engagement is entirely unknown — there is no record of
whether the person stayed on the line briefly, hung up immediately, was cut
off, redialled repeatedly in short succession, or anything else about how
these attempts unfolded. This is a genuine data gap, not evidence of low or
absent engagement.

Possible function — hypotheses only
What the 64 pre-call disconnections represent cannot be determined from the
available record. Plausible, unresolved hypotheses include:

- The person is finding the automated pre-call message itself a barrier
  (length, content, or wait time) and disengaging before a counsellor is
  reached.
- The attempts reflect fluctuating capacity or ambivalence about
  disclosure — reaching for contact, then withdrawing before connection.
- Some or all attempts are redials after being cut off or queued, rather
  than 64 independent decisions not to continue.
- The attempts reflect an escalating need for contact that the 6 connected
  calls alone do not capture.
- Some attempts may be unrelated to distress (misdials, service testing,
  technical issues) — this cannot be ruled out or confirmed.

None of these is better supported than the others on the current record. The
6 calm, connected calls do not resolve what the 64 disconnections mean; they
describe a different, smaller subset of contact.

What remains unknown
- What happened during any of the 64 pre-call disconnections — content,
  duration on the line, whether the person spoke at all.
- Whether the 64 attempts are 64 independent decisions or a smaller number
  of underlying attempts with repeated redialling.
- Any baseline for this person's usual contact pattern, so it is not
  possible to say whether 70 attempts in 10 days is itself a change.
- Clustering/timing detail on the 64 disconnected attempts (time of day,
  spacing) is not provided here and has not yet been reviewed.
- Whether the pre-call message itself is a contributing factor to the
  disconnection rate.

Clinical significance
The existing record and response account only for the 6 connected calls,
which on their own look like a manageable, well-understood pattern of
supportive contact. But those 6 calls are 9% of this person's total contact
volume over the period. The 64 pre-call disconnections — the large majority
of contact — have not been reviewed as a group before now and are entirely
unaccounted for by the current formulation. This is not evidence that
something is clinically wrong; it is evidence that the current picture is
built from a subset of the person's contact and that the remaining 91% is an
unexamined gap. That gap, on its own, is a coordination and visibility
problem regardless of what it eventually turns out to mean.

Therefore
TL/Safety Huddle — Contact is fragmenting across a large volume of
pre-call-disconnected attempts (64 of 70, over 10 days) that no one has
reviewed as a group, alongside a small set of connected calls that looks
reassuring in isolation but does not represent the caller's overall contact
pattern. This is a continuity and visibility issue that needs joining up:
someone needs a full picture of all 70 attempts, and consideration of
whether the pre-call message or connection process itself is a factor,
before any conclusion is drawn about what this pattern means. The data does
not yet establish a sustained, unexplained change of the kind that would
warrant CRG, nor does it establish that the pattern is adequately understood
and managed, which would warrant no further action.
