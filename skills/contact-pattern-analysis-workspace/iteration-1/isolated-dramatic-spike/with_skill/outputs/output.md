CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
No established baseline. This is a first contact — there is no prior call history for this person, so nothing about frequency, typical duration, usual engagement, recurring themes, or typical mode of ending can be said to be "usual" for them. A single data point cannot be used to infer a baseline.

Observed change
Not applicable as a "change," since there is no baseline to change from. What can be stated as observation only:
- One attempted contact, which connected to a counsellor (connected contact).
- Duration 45 minutes.
- The caller was highly distressed at the start of the call, discussing a specific, acute, dateable stressor (job loss and eviction notice received the same day).
- The call involved substantive engagement — the caller stayed with the conversation rather than disconnecting or disengaging.
- It reached the level of completed intervention — the call ended with the caller reporting feeling calmer and having identified concrete next steps, and the caller agreed to call back if needed.
- No further contact (attempted or connected) in the two weeks since.

Data limitation: this analysis is based only on what is described as one connected, completed call. There is no information here about pre-call disconnections, abandoned queue attempts, or other attempted-but-unconnected contact in this window — if such data exists, it has not been reviewed here, and its absence should not be read as evidence that no other attempts occurred.

Engagement pattern
Within the single call, engagement moved from high distress to a calmer state, with the caller able to identify and articulate concrete next steps by the end. This is a within-call trajectory, not a cross-call pattern — there is only one call to observe it in.

Possible function — hypotheses only
The call is consistent with several functions, and the data does not distinguish between them:
- Containment and regulation in response to a genuinely acute, time-limited crisis (the job loss/eviction notice), with the call serving its evident purpose.
- Testing or establishing the service as a viable resource, which could be relevant to whether or how the person recontacts in future.
- Problem-solving support specific to the practical crisis, now at least partially addressed by the identified next steps.
- A first disclosure of broader distress that this particular crisis gave the person an opening to raise, which may or may not represent the whole picture.

None of these is preferred by the material available; the call content and outcome are consistent with the first and third being most directly evidenced, but that should not be read as ruling out the others.

What remains unknown
- Whether this is truly the person's first-ever contact with the service or simply the first recorded contact in the data reviewed.
- Whether any attempted-but-unconnected contacts (pre-call disconnections, abandoned attempts) have occurred in the two weeks since, since only connected/completed contact has been described here.
- Whether the practical crisis (job loss, eviction) has actually resolved, worsened, or remains unaddressed since the call — the call ending with "identified next steps" is not evidence those steps were taken or worked.
- Whether the absence of further contact reflects the crisis resolving, the person coping independently, the person seeking help elsewhere, or reduced willingness/capacity to re-contact.
- Any relevant history predating this service's records (other services, prior crises) that isn't visible here.

Clinical significance
A single connected call, with clear precipitant, high initial distress, substantive engagement, a completed intervention (de-escalation plus identification of concrete next steps), and an explicit agreement to call back if needed, is an adequately explained and adequately managed presentation on the information available. There is no persisting or accelerating pattern here to be explained — there is one data point, which is itself the entire "pattern" so far. The two weeks of non-contact since is not, on its own, either a reassuring or a concerning finding: absence of contact is not evidence of improvement, but nor does it constitute an unexplained or escalating contact pattern requiring oversight. Isolated variation cannot be distinguished from a sustained pattern here because there is no second data point — this is expected and appropriate at this stage, not a gap that needs to be filled by inference.

Therefore
Helpline — the single contact is well understood on its own terms (clear precipitant, substantive engagement, completed intervention, explicit re-contact agreement), and the current response (closure with an open invitation to call back) remains proportionate. There is no persisting or unexplained pattern of contact to justify TL/Safety Huddle or CRG involvement at this time. This conclusion covers the contact pattern only — it says nothing about whether the underlying situation (job loss, eviction) still carries risk that would be picked up in a PORF if further contact or other information becomes available. If a second contact occurs, particularly one that doesn't resolve as cleanly, or if any attempted-but-unconnected contacts come to light, this pattern should be re-assessed rather than assumed to still be "one clean call."
