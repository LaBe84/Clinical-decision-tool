CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
Over the six-week period on record, the caller's contact has been highly
regular: daily, at approximately the same time each day, occurring just after
his usual medication dose. Each call is brief (5-8 minutes). This regularity
is itself the established pattern — there is no earlier, pre-six-week
baseline in the material to compare it against, so it cannot be said whether
this represents a change from some prior mode of contact or is the full
extent of the recorded relationship with the service. Treat the six weeks as
the baseline currently available, not as a deviation from a known one.

Observed change
No change within the six-week window is described in the material — frequency,
timing, and duration have stayed constant call to call. All contact described
is connected contact (calls that reached a counsellor); no data is provided
on attempted-but-unconnected contact (pre-call disconnections, abandoned
queue attempts), so the full attempted-contact picture is unknown and should
not be assumed to match the connected-contact picture.

Engagement pattern
Once connected, engagement is brief and narrowly focused: the caller asks the
counsellor to confirm he "did the right thing" taking his medication and has
not overdosed, receives that reassurance, thanks the counsellor, and ends the
call. This sequence has repeated consistently across calls and across at
least two different counsellors. No suicidal ideation, self-harm, or distress
content beyond this specific checking behaviour has been recorded in any
call.

Possible function — hypotheses only
This is one of the rarer cases where function is directly evidenced rather
than hypothesised: the caller has himself stated, in the same terms, to two
different counsellors, what the calls are for — confirming to himself that
he took his medication correctly and has not overdosed. This should be
described as the observed function at the behavioural level, not as one
item in a list of speculative alternatives. It is not, however, a complete
clinical explanation for why this checking need exists (e.g. whether it
reflects an underlying anxiety or checking pattern, medication-related
confusion risk, cognitive factors, or something else) — that remains
unestablished and is outside what a contact-pattern review can determine.

What remains unknown
- Whether any pre-call disconnections or unconnected attempts exist alongside
  the six weeks of connected calls — no attempted-contact data is provided.
- Whether this six-week window itself represents a change from an earlier
  pattern of contact (or non-contact) with the service, since no prior
  baseline is available.
- The underlying reason the checking need exists (e.g. anxiety about dosing
  accuracy, confusion about the regimen, or another driver) — the caller has
  evidenced what the calls are for, not why the need itself persists.
- Whether this pattern has been reviewed against his broader
  medication/clinical context (e.g. by a prescriber or GP) outside of
  Lifeline contact.

Clinical significance
The pattern is well explained at the behavioural level: consistent timing,
consistent short duration, consistent content, and a directly and
consistently evidenced reason from the caller himself across separate
counsellors. There is no indication in the material of escalation,
broadening content, increasing frequency, or distress beyond the checking
behaviour.

The second question is separate and remains open. Six weeks of the same
reassurance-and-closure cycle resolving cleanly every time shows the pattern
is stable — it does not by itself show that daily Lifeline contact is the
right or most effective way of meeting this need, nor that it is achieving
anything beyond repeating itself. The unchanged, unshortening, unreducing
cycle raises a credible question for formulation — whether the daily
reassurance may be maintaining reliance on Lifeline for a checking need that
might otherwise be met differently (e.g. through his prescriber, a support
plan, or another structured approach) — but the contact-pattern data alone
cannot establish that the response is causing the pattern to continue.
Temporal association (stable pattern coexisting with stable response) is not
the same claim as causal maintenance, and this analysis does not make that
claim.

Therefore
TL/Safety Huddle.

The pattern and its immediate behavioural function are adequately explained,
so this is not an unresolved-pattern case in the CRG sense. But two things
sit outside what a single counsellor call-by-call can resolve: (1) the
attempted-contact picture is unknown, so it isn't yet confirmed that the
full six-week picture is even complete; and (2) a well-evidenced function
that has repeated unchanged for six weeks, with the open question of whether
daily Lifeline contact is the appropriate long-term mechanism for meeting it
versus other supports, is a coordination question — whether anyone holds the
full picture across counsellors and whether the current response is the
right one going forward — not a single-call clinical decision. This is
raised as a question for coordinated review to consider (including whether
referral toward his prescriber or another structured support is appropriate),
not as a finding that the current response is causing or worsening anything.
