CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
Over the six-week window under review, the caller has established a consistent pattern: daily contact, occurring at almost exactly the same time each day (shortly after his usual medication dose), with each call brief (5–8 minutes). This is now the baseline for this person within the period covered — there is no data here on his contact pattern prior to these six weeks, so it is not known whether this daily-checking pattern is itself a change from an earlier baseline or has been present longer than six weeks. That earlier history, if it exists, is a gap rather than a finding either way.

Observed change
No change is evidenced within the six-week window itself. Frequency, timing, duration, and content have remained consistent call to call: contact once daily, close to a fixed time tied to medication dosing, each call short, each call structured around the same request (confirmation that he "did the right thing" taking his medication and has not overdosed), each call ending in a planned closure once he is reassured. This is reported as observation only. Available data covers connected, substantive contact — calls that reached a counsellor and involved actual exchange. There is no information in the material provided about attempted-but-unconnected contact (pre-call disconnections, abandoned queue attempts) in this period; if such attempts exist and haven't been reviewed, the picture below is limited to the connected subset, not the full contact pattern.

Engagement pattern
Engagement is consistent and complete within each call: the caller connects, states his specific request, receives reassurance from the counsellor, and ends the call once reassured. No call has terminated in distress, disconnection, or unresolved content. No suicidal ideation, self-harm, or distress content beyond the checking behaviour itself has been recorded in any call.

Possible function — hypotheses only
Function is directly evidenced here, not merely hypothesised. The caller has explicitly and consistently told two different counsellors what the calls are for: confirming to himself, via external reassurance, that he took his medication correctly and has not overdosed. This meets the bar for evidenced function at the behavioural level — reassurance-seeking tied specifically to medication-dosing uncertainty — rather than being one item on a list of speculative alternatives. This is not, however, a complete clinical explanation. It does not establish why he needs external reassurance rather than being able to self-verify (e.g. whether this reflects a checking/OCD-type process, cognitive or memory difficulty affecting dosing certainty, anxiety specific to the medication, or something else) — that underlying question is a formulation matter, not something contact-pattern data can resolve, and should not be assumed from the pattern alone.

What remains unknown
- Whether this pattern existed before the six-week window, and if so for how long, or whether it began at some identifiable point (e.g. a medication change, a dosing error, a health scare).
- Attempted-but-unconnected contact volume in this period — not available in the material reviewed.
- Why he cannot self-verify that he has taken the correct dose (the underlying driver behind the reassurance-seeking), which is a formulation question rather than a contact-pattern one.
- Whether this pattern has been reviewed by the same counsellor across multiple contacts or by a wider group, and whether any counsellor has responded differently from another — no evidence of inconsistency has been reported, but continuity of review has not been explicitly confirmed either.

Clinical significance
The pattern itself is well explained: two independent counsellors have received the same account of what the calls are for, in the same terms, and the calls have consistently resolved the same way with no distress or risk content beyond the checking behaviour. There is no indication of conflicting responses across staff and no operational decision currently pending. On the second question — whether the existing response is achieving something, rather than simply running alongside the behaviour it addresses — six weeks of the same reassurance cycle resolving cleanly every time is evidence the pattern is stable, not evidence the response is resolving the underlying need. Whether repeated reassurance is helping him tolerate uncertainty over time, or whether it is maintaining reliance on external checking rather than reducing it, cannot be determined from contact-pattern data alone. This is a genuine open question worth recording for formulation — it is not, by itself, evidence that the current response is failing or that anything is currently going wrong.

Therefore
Helpline, with ongoing review. The pattern and its function are recorded and consistently understood across the counsellors who have taken these calls; there is no conflicting staff response and no operational decision requiring real-time coordination, so this does not meet the bar for TL/Safety Huddle — that would require an actual evidenced visibility or coordination gap (e.g. contact split across unreviewed channels, inconsistent staff handling, a decision genuinely pending), and none is present here. Nor does it meet the bar for CRG: the pattern is not unexplained, and there is no evidence the intervention is failing — only an unresolved formulation question about whether the reassurance response is optimal long-term, which is worth noting and monitoring rather than acting on today. Continue routine documentation of these calls, keep the record available to any counsellor who takes one so the pattern stays visible and consistently handled, and flag for formulation review if the pattern changes materially (frequency, distress content, or resolution), if staff responses become inconsistent, or if the reassurance-seeking appears to intensify rather than settle over time.
