CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
No established baseline is described for this caller prior to the current
10-day window. What is available is only a description of the 6 connected
calls within that window (calm, coherent, supportive conversations lasting
10-20 minutes each with clear closure) — this is not a pre-existing baseline
to compare against, it is part of the current window itself. Whether this
caller's usual pattern differs from what is seen here is not established from
the material provided.

Observed change
Over the past 10 days, the caller made 70 total attempted contacts.
- Connected contact: 6 of 70 attempts (9%) reached a counsellor. These are
  documented as calm, coherent, supportive conversations, 10-20 minutes each,
  with clear closure.
- Attempted-but-unconnected contact: 64 of 70 attempts (91%) terminated
  during the automated pre-call message, before reaching a counsellor. There
  is no record of what, if anything, was said or intended during any of these
  64 attempts.
- No one has reviewed the 64 disconnected attempts as a group before now. The
  existing clinical picture of this caller has been built entirely from the
  6 connected calls — 9% of total contact activity.

This is an observation about what the record contains and does not contain,
not a characterisation of what the 64 disconnected attempts mean.

Engagement pattern
For the 6 connected calls, engagement is documented as good: calm, coherent,
supportive conversation with clear closure each time. For the 64 disconnected
attempts, there is no engagement data at all — no record of whether the
caller stayed on the line briefly, hung up immediately, was cut off, or
anything else about what happened during the pre-call message window. This is
a genuine gap, not evidence of any particular engagement level.

Possible function — hypotheses only
Function is not established from available information. There is no record
of what, if anything, the caller intended or communicated during the 64
disconnected attempts, and the 6 connected calls do not themselves speak to
why the other 64 attempts occurred or ended where they did. Generating a list
of plausible-sounding explanations for the 64 attempts (e.g. testing

What remains unknown
- What happened during any of the 64 pre-call disconnections — content,
  duration on the line, or reason for termination — is entirely unknown.
- Whether the 64 disconnected attempts represent one unfolding presentation,
  several different contact purposes, or a mix, is unknown; they have not yet

- Whether the 64 attempts cluster in particular time windows, follow a

- Whether this represents a change from the caller's usual attempt-to-connect

Clinical significance
The existing formulation, such as it is, has been built entirely from the 6
connected calls — the calm, coherent, well-closed 9% of total contact
activity. It does not account for the 64 disconnected attempts because no one
has looked at them as a group before now. This means the current picture
cannot be said to adequately explain the caller's overall contact pattern:
the visible evidence of good engagement in 6 calls says nothing reliable
about what is happening in the 91% of attempts that never reached a
counsellor. This is not evidence that something is clinically wrong with the
caller — it is evidence that the picture held by the service is incomplete,
and incomplete in a way that has not previously been identified.

Therefore
TL/Safety Huddle.

This is a visibility and coordination problem, not yet a question of whether
the underlying pattern is concerning: the recorded clinical picture for this
caller is based on only 9% of total attempts (6 of 70), with the remaining
91% (64 of 70) never reviewed as a group and containing no record of content
or intent. That gap needs to be joined up — the 64 disconnected attempts
reviewed together for clustering, timing, and any other structure the raw
log can show — before the pattern can be properly understood, regardless of
whether the underlying function turns out to be benign once that review is
done. This is not, on the current information, a CRG trigger: nothing yet
shows the pattern or its function remains unexplained after adequate review,
or that the existing intervention is failing to achieve its purpose, because
that review has not yet happened. It also is not simply Helpline-only,
because a formulation built on 9% of contact activity is not one that can be
said to adequately account for the pattern as a whole.
