CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
Over the past 8 months this caller has contacted Lifeline 4-5 times per week,
consistently in the evening, in calls lasting 15-25 minutes. Content has
consistently concerned ongoing loneliness and low mood. Calls have
consistently ended the same way: the caller thanking the counsellor and
agreeing a plan for the night. No history of pre-call disconnection, abrupt
endings, or emergency escalation is reported. This is a well-established
baseline.

Observed change
This week the caller contacted on 5 consecutive evenings, rather than her
usual spread of 4-5 contacts across the week. Duration, content, and ending
were the same as usual on each of the 5 calls. No data is provided on
attempted-but-unconnected contact (e.g. queue abandonment, pre-call
disconnection) this week; the analysis below is based on connected,
substantive contact only, and that is a limitation, not an assumption that no
other attempts occurred.

Engagement pattern
Unchanged from baseline: the caller engaged substantively on each call this
week (15-25 minutes, consistent with usual), and each call reached the same
planned, calm closure as before. There is no indication of reduced
engagement, abrupt endings, or deteriorating tone on any of the 5 calls.

Possible function
Function is not established from the available information. The recurring
content (ongoing loneliness and low mood) and the consistent evening timing
and calm closure are observed, repeated features of her contact — but nothing
in the material shows the caller has directly and consistently stated why she
is calling, so this remains a description of behaviour, not an evidenced
function.

What remains unknown
- Whether any contact attempts this week did not connect (queue abandonment,
  pre-call disconnection) — not reported either way.
- Why the caller's contact clustered into 5 consecutive evenings rather than
  her usual spread this particular week (no stated reason on record).
- Whether this clustering has occurred before during the 8-month history, or
  is a first occurrence.
- Whether anything changed in the caller's circumstances this week that is
  not reflected in call content.

Clinical significance
Total weekly volume (5 calls) sits within, not above, her established range
of 4-5 calls per week — the change is in clustering (consecutive evenings
vs spread across the week), not in frequency, duration, content, or
engagement. This is a single week of altered spacing against an 8-month
baseline that has otherwise repeated unchanged; it is not yet evidence of a
sustained or accelerating change, and there isn't enough history beyond this
one week to say whether the clustering will persist. The existing
formulation (ongoing loneliness and low mood, managed through supportive
evening contact ending in a agreed plan) continues to account for what was
said and how each call ended. Nothing in this week's pattern indicates the
current response has stopped achieving planned, calm closure — each call
this week closed exactly as calls have closed for 8 months.

Therefore
Helpline. The pattern remains adequately explained by the existing
formulation, and the response continues to reach its usual planned closure on
each contact. The change observed (clustering into consecutive evenings
within an unchanged weekly volume) is an isolated variation, not a sustained
or unexplained shift, and does not on its own indicate a coordination gap or
an unaccounted-for change in presentation. If this clustering persists beyond
this week, or if duration, content, or closure begin to differ from
baseline, the pattern should be reassessed at that point.
