CONTACT PATTERN ANALYSIS

Established pattern
No established baseline. This is the caller's first recorded contact with the service — there is no prior history against which to judge frequency, typical duration, timing, or usual engagement level.

Observed change
Not applicable in the usual sense, since there is no baseline to compare against. What can be stated is the single observation available: one attempted contact, which connected, and which became substantive engagement — a 45-minute call in which the caller discussed a specific acute stressor (job loss and an eviction notice received the same day). No data exists on any pre-call disconnections or other attempts, since none are reported; this is not a limitation flagged from missing records, it is simply the full extent of contact to date.

Engagement pattern
The caller was highly distressed at the outset. Over the course of the call they engaged substantively with the counsellor, and by the end reported feeling calmer and had identified concrete next steps. The call reached a planned closure rather than an abrupt or counsellor-initiated ending, and the caller agreed to call back if needed. No recontact has occurred in the two weeks since.

Possible function — hypotheses only
Not a hypothesis in this instance: the caller directly described what prompted the call — an acute, dateable crisis (same-day job loss and eviction notice) — and this was consistent with how the call was used (working through the immediate crisis to identified next steps). This can be described as the observed function of this single contact at the behavioural level. It should not, however, be treated as a complete account of the caller's underlying circumstances or of what future contact, if any, might be for — with only one contact, there is no basis to generalise it into a pattern or a diagnosis of ongoing need.

What remains unknown
Everything about how this caller uses the service outside this one contact: whether the absence of recontact reflects resolution, avoidance, use of another support, reduced capacity to reach out, or something else entirely. Two weeks of non-contact after a single first call is a short window and, per this skill's invariants, non-contact is not itself evidence of improvement — it is simply the current state of the record. There is also no information on any attempted-but-unconnected contact, since none has been reported.

Clinical significance
The single available contact is well explained: an acute, specific precipitant, a substantive connected call, and a closure with concrete next steps identified — this is not an unexplained or fragmented picture. Within this one contact, the response also appears to have achieved something concrete (de-escalation and a next-steps plan), rather than merely running alongside the distress without effect. There is no persisting or unexplained pattern to assess, because there is only one data point; persistence and magnitude cannot yet be evaluated for a caller with a single contact.

Therefore
Helpline. The one contact on record is adequately explained, was substantively engaged, and closed with an identified plan rather than an unresolved or ambiguous ending. There is no visibility gap (a single call, fully connected, is not split across multiple people or disconnected attempts) and no unexplained or accelerating pattern to justify TL/Safety Huddle or CRG. Two weeks of subsequent non-contact does not, on its own, change this — non-contact is not evidence of either improvement or deterioration, and with only one prior contact there is not yet a "pattern" whose absence of continuation would itself be meaningful. If the caller does make further contact, that contact should be reviewed alongside this one to see whether a pattern is starting to form, rather than assuming continuity or a resolved case either way.
