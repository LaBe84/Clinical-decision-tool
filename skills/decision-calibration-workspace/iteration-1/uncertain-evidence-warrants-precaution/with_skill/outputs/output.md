DECISION CALIBRATION

Belief under examination
The caller may be concealing current suicidal ideation. Basis: a flat tone and a quick denial when asked directly, with no baseline communication style on file to compare against. Inference Control has calibrated this as speculative, not established — flat tone and a quick denial are each consistent with concealment but equally consistent with fatigue, discomfort with the question, a communication style that is simply flat, wanting to end the call, or genuine absence of current ideation. There is no baseline to say this response is atypical for this caller, and nothing here rises to consistent-with or well-supported.

Consequence if true and unaddressed
If concealment is real and the contact ends without further enquiry, a caller with current suicidal ideation leaves the contact without that ideation being identified, without a safety plan or referral being offered, and without the chance for a differently-phrased question to open space they didn't take the first time. This is a serious potential consequence — it is also not certain, since it depends on the speculative belief being true, and even a further question is not guaranteed to surface concealed ideation if the caller is determined not to disclose.

Consequence if false and acted upon
If the concern is unfounded and one further, differently-phrased direct safety question is asked anyway: the cost is a few extra seconds on a call, mild discomfort for a caller who has already answered honestly and is asked again, and a small risk of the caller feeling doubted or not believed. This is not a restrictive or punitive action — it does not withhold anything, deny access, escalate to another party, or change the caller's relationship with the service. The cost-if-wrong is low and does not compound.

Reversibility and cost of candidate actions
- Asking one further, differently-phrased direct safety question before ending contact: reversible, information-gathering, low-cost. It changes nothing structurally about the contact and can be followed immediately by normal closure if the answer is unremarkable.
- (For contrast, not proposed here) Escalating to emergency services, initiating a welfare check, or refusing to end the call without a named safety plan would be consequential, harder to reverse, and would carry real cost to the caller and the relationship if the concern turned out to be unfounded — those actions require a materially stronger belief than what is present here.

Threshold analysis
The belief is speculative — the weakest calibrated tier — but the candidate action is a single reversible, low-cost, information-gathering question, not a consequential intervention. Under this skill's framing, a low-confidence hypothesis can justify a precautionary, low-cost, reversible action when the cost of being wrong is severe and the cost of the precaution is small. That is exactly this configuration: severe potential consequence if true and unaddressed, low cost if false and acted upon. The action clears the threshold. It would not clear the threshold for anything beyond itself — the speculative belief does not stretch to justify escalation, refusal to close the call, or treating the concealment as established.

Proportionate action(s)
- Ask one further, differently-phrased direct question about current safety before ending the contact. This is justified and proportionate to a speculative belief given the asymmetry in consequence.
- Alternatively or additionally: name the observation plainly to the caller (e.g., noting the flat tone) and give them an explicit, low-pressure opening to say more, rather than only repeating a direct question in different words — this is the same class of action (reversible, information-gathering) and clears the same threshold.
- Providing standard closing safety information (crisis line details, invitation to call back) regardless of the answer to the further question is also proportionate at this belief strength — it is offered routinely and does not require any elevated suspicion to justify.

What is not justified
- Treating the denial as confirmed concealment and escalating to emergency services, welfare check, or mandatory follow-up contact — this would require an established or well-supported finding, not a speculative one, and the belief has not earned that weight.
- Refusing to end the contact until the caller "admits" something, or repeating the question more than the one further attempt in a way that becomes pressuring rather than information-gathering — at that point the action's own weight and cost start to shift and would need re-examination under this same calibration, not be waved through because the underlying concern feels urgent.
- Declining to ask any further question on the grounds that the belief is "only speculative" — this collapses action threshold into belief strength and ignores that the candidate action here is low-cost and reversible.

Residual judgement
Whether to phrase the further question as a repeated direct check versus an observation-plus-opening (naming the flat tone and inviting more) is a genuine judgement call the calibration doesn't resolve — both clear the threshold, and the choice between them is better made on the specifics of this caller's responses so far than dictated in advance.
