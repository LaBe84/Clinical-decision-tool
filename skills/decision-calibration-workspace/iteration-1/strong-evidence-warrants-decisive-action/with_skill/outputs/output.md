DECISION CALIBRATION

Belief under examination
Active suicidal ideation with a specific plan and a stated timeframe, disclosed this contact. The caller was directly asked and directly answered in their own words. Inference Control has calibrated this as **established**, on direct comparison against a documented prior baseline of passive ideation with no plan. This is not an inferred or reconstructed belief — it is a direct first-person disclosure of plan and timeframe.

Consequence if true and unaddressed
If the disclosure is accurate and no safety action is taken, the specific, foreseeable consequence is that the caller acts on a plan they have already formed, within a timeframe they have already named, with no safety net in place. This is not a worst-case hypothetical layered on top of an uncertain belief — plan and timeframe are the caller's own stated words, so "if true" is not really the live uncertainty here; the belief is established, not speculative. The residual uncertainty is narrower: exactly when within the stated timeframe, and how reliably intent translates to action — not whether the ideation and plan exist.

Consequence if false and acted upon
Because the belief is established rather than inferred, "false" is not the primary risk to weigh — the caller said this directly. The more relevant cost-if-wrong is: taking a safety action when it turns out not to be strictly necessary (e.g., the caller was testing the response, has since de-escalated, or the plan is less immediately actionable than stated). That cost is real — a rupture of trust with the caller, a more guarded future contact, or a caller who feels penalized for disclosing honestly — but it is a cost attached to a low-probability error (the disclosure being unreliable), not to the belief itself being weak.

Reversibility and cost of candidate actions
- Immediate safety action per organisational protocol (the candidate action): consequential and, in important respects, not reversible in the moment it is taken — engaging emergency services or a safety intervention changes the caller's situation in real time and cannot be "undone" once initiated, even if it later proves to have been the right or an overly cautious call.
- This is distinct from lower-cost actions (continuing to engage, direct safety questions, collaborative safety planning) which remain reversible and information-gathering — but those are only proportionate substitutes when belief strength is lower than what is present here.

Threshold analysis
An established finding of imminent risk — first-person, specific plan, specific timeframe, directly confirmed — is precisely the category of belief that a consequential, hard-to-reverse safety action is built to respond to. The threshold for immediate safety action is not "any concern," it is established imminent risk with severe and irreversible consequence if untreated (loss of life) against a bounded, recoverable cost if the concern turns out to be overcautious (a difficult but repairable interaction). That asymmetry, combined with belief strength at the top of the scale, clears the bar.

This is also the case the skill explicitly defers on: where danger is already directly established, action is not gated behind a calibration exercise, and organisational protocol governs. The analysis above confirms rather than substitutes for that protocol.

Proportionate action(s)
- Immediate safety action per organisational protocol, as mandated, is justified and should proceed without delay or further calibration.
- Alongside it, not instead of it: continue direct, collaborative engagement with the caller through the process (maintaining rapport, transparency about what is happening and why, involving the caller in the safety response where possible) — this does not replace the mandatory action but reduces the relational cost identified above.
- Which specific mechanism (emergency services, warm handoff, other protocol-specified pathway) is a domain selection made by Intervention Selection / CRG Triage, not by this calibration.

What is not justified
- Delaying the mandated action to gather further confirmation: the belief is already established, not speculative or consistent-with, so further evidence-gathering before acting would be treating an established finding as if it still needed to earn its weight — an inversion of proportionate response, not caution.
- Treating this as a case requiring the two-error analysis this skill exists to police (calibration between low-confidence belief and action): that analysis matters when belief strength is uncertain. Here it is not; the caller stated plan and timeframe directly. Applying a hedge-and-verify posture to an established, immediate-risk disclosure would be Error 2's mirror — treating a strong claim as if it needed to be strengthened further before acting.

Residual judgement
The judgement that remains open is not whether to act — protocol and the belief strength both settle that — but how the mandated action is carried out: pacing, what is said to the caller, and how much the caller is kept informed and involved as the action unfolds, which affects the relational cost identified above without changing whether the action itself is warranted. That is a matter for the specific intervention/triage skill and for the responder's judgement in the moment, not for this calibration.
