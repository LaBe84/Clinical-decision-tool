DECISION CALIBRATION

Belief under examination
Established: this caller has disclosed active suicidal ideation with a specific plan and a stated timeframe, this contact, in their own words, directly asked and directly answered. This has been calibrated as established by Inference Control against a documented prior baseline of passive ideation with no plan. Not established in the material so far: current access to means, capability, or how imminent the stated timeframe actually is — these have not yet been assessed in this contact.

Consequence if true and unaddressed
If the disclosure is accurate and nothing is done, the specific consequence is that a caller who has told the service they have a plan and a timeframe for suicide is left without any real-time safety response — the service would have direct, self-reported evidence of acute risk and would not have acted on it. The severity of that consequence does not depend on resolving imminence or means first: even without knowing exactly how soon or how the caller could act, "I have a plan and a timeframe" is itself the kind of disclosure the service's real-time safety response exists to receive. What remains genuinely uncertain is how close to acting the caller is right now — that uncertainty affects how the response is calibrated within the process, not whether the process is entered.

Consequence if false and acted upon
The belief itself is established (the caller's own words, directly asked and answered) — so the residual uncertainty is not "did they say this," it's "how imminent/capable is this in fact." If the response process is entered and closer assessment finds the timeframe is more distant or means are not currently accessible than first appeared, the cost is that the caller has moved through a real-time safety conversation that a calmer read might have handled differently in pacing. This is a modest cost: it is a conversational and time cost, not a restrictive or coercive one at this stage, and real-time safety responses are generally built to scale their intensity to what the ongoing conversation reveals rather than being all-or-nothing.

Reversibility and cost of candidate actions
- Moving into the real-time safety response process: reversible and adjustable — entering the process does not itself lock in a specific disposition; it opens a structured conversation in which capability, means, and imminence get assessed as part of the process itself, and the response can be scaled up or down as that information emerges.
- Continuing generic history-gathering before moving into the process: also reversible, but it is not a lower-cost way of addressing the same consequence — it delays the point at which imminence and means are actually assessed, and the consequence identified above (an unaddressed disclosed plan and timeframe) accrues for as long as that delay lasts.

Lower-cost alternatives and governance requirements
There is no lower-cost action available here that resolves the same uncertainty faster than the real-time safety response process itself — assessing imminence, means, and capability is what that process is for; deferring to further generic history-taking would not gather that information any sooner, and would gather it more slowly. Separately, from what's described, an established disclosure of active ideation with plan and timeframe is very likely to sit at or above the organisation's own mandatory real-time safety response threshold — if such a policy threshold applies, it governs independent of this calibration and should be checked and named explicitly by the practitioner, not inferred here.

Threshold analysis
The belief strength (established, this contact, direct disclosure) is high. The consequence of omission (a self-reported plan-and-timeframe disclosure left unaddressed) is serious and time-sensitive even though its exact imminence is unassessed. The consequence of commission (entering a structured, adjustable safety conversation that may turn out not to have needed its highest intensity) is comparatively low and does not foreclose recalibrating pace or intensity as more is learned. The candidate action is reversible and adjustable, not a hard-to-reverse restriction. No lower-cost alternative would resolve the open questions (imminence, means, capability) faster than the process itself would. Together, these factors — not belief strength alone — support entering the process without delaying for further generic history-gathering. This is not because "established disclosure automatically requires real-time response" as a fixed rule; it is because, in this specific combination of consequence, reversibility, and absence of a faster alternative, the process is the way the still-open questions get resolved, and delay has a real cost while entering it does not foreclose anything.

Proportionate action(s)
- Moving into the organisation's real-time safety response process now, rather than continuing generic history-gathering first — this is warranted by the analysis above.
- Within that process, treating imminence, means access, and capability as the specific open questions still to be resolved through the process's own assessment steps (e.g. its equivalent of Suicide Enquiry / PORF or direct real-time observation) — not as already established, and not as a precondition that must be satisfied before the process can begin.

What is not justified
- Treating the disclosure as though it already establishes "imminent risk," "confirmed means access," or "confirmed capability" — those haven't been assessed yet, and the fact that the process is being entered doesn't retroactively establish them. What licenses entering the process is the established disclosure itself and the consequence/reversibility analysis above, not an assumption that those further facts are already known.
- Any action that would go beyond the real-time safety response process itself into a specific, harder-to-reverse disposition (e.g. a specific restrictive or coercive outcome) before the process's own assessment steps have actually resolved imminence, means, and capability — that still requires information the process, not this calibration, will surface.

Residual judgement
The pacing and intensity of the real-time safety response — how quickly it escalates within itself once entered, and which of its internal steps take priority — depends on what the process's own assessment (imminence, means, capability) turns up, and that is a judgement for the process and its owning skill(s) to make as the conversation unfolds, not one this calibration can resolve in advance.
