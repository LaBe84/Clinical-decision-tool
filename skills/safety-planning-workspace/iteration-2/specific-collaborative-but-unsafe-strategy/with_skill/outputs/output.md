SAFETY PLAN REVIEW

Verdict: Not yet usable as written. Most of the plan is genuinely strong — specific, person-tested, collaboratively built with named supports who've confirmed their roles. But one step (the frozen phone) actively undermines the plan's own escalation step, and that's not a minor wording issue — it's a structural conflict that needs to be resolved before this plan should be signed off as complete.

Crisis state this plan addresses
Established late-night suicidal crises, with access to prescription medication. This is carried from existing formulation/enquiry material as the pattern being planned for. Not established in the material provided: what specifically triggers or escalates these episodes (e.g. isolation, a particular thought pattern, a specific event) — the plan opens with "when I start feeling like this," which names a state but not the recognisable early signs of it. That gap sits between questions 1 and 2 below.

Early warning signs
Not specific in the material as given. "When I start feeling like this" is the person's own shorthand, but the plan doesn't capture what "this" actually looks like early — thoughts, physical sensations, or behaviours the person or Jade would notice before the crisis is already underway. This is worth going back to the person for directly: what do you notice first, before it gets to the point of needing to text Jade? Without that, the plan's entry point is only legible to the person retrospectively, which risks it being recognised too late to use the earlier steps.

Independent coping steps
The freezer strategy is genuinely person-specific and was arrived at collaboratively — that's real strength, not a generic placeholder. It's plausible it does break the urge to check messages or spiral, which is a concrete, testable claim rather than vague reassurance.

However, it fails the "doesn't create a competing safety problem" check. The plan's own final step is "if things are still bad, I call the crisis line" — but the phone that would be used to make that call has just been physically sealed in a bag and put in the freezer. If the freezer step doesn't resolve the crisis (which is exactly the scenario the escalation step exists for), the person now has to retrieve, unwrap, and dry off a phone before they can act on it — at exactly the point they're least likely to have the coordination, patience, or motivation to do that. A friction technique that also cuts off the ability to call for help if the friction doesn't work is a net-negative trade, even though it's genuinely person-specific and was arrived at together.

This needs to go back to the person as a design problem, not be dropped unilaterally: what would give the same "I physically can't keep checking/spiralling" effect without also blocking the escalation route? Options worth raising with them (not prescribing): a physical barrier that doesn't disable the device itself (phone left in another room, given to whoever's in the house, silenced/face-down rather than frozen), or a clear amendment to the plan that a specific, always-accessible backup way to reach Jade or the crisis line exists that doesn't depend on the frozen phone (e.g. a landline, a housemate's phone, a doorbell/knock arrangement with whoever holds it). Whatever's chosen needs the same collaborative confirmation the freezer idea got.

Interpersonal / environmental support
Jade: specific, named, confirmed she always replies and has confirmed this role directly — meets the bar of an actually available and willing resource, not just a relationship that exists. Availability at the specific late-night times these crises occur isn't explicitly confirmed (does Jade see late-night texts promptly, or reply the next morning?) — worth confirming that timing detail given the crisis window is specifically late-night.

Means / environmental safety
Mum holding the medication during risk weeks: this is a concrete, feasible, clinically appropriate means-restriction step, aimed at the actual established risk (access to prescription medication), with a specific person confirmed as willing and able to implement it. This is a genuine strength of the plan. Worth clarifying operationally (not for this skill to specify how, but for the person/service to confirm): how "risk weeks" get identified and communicated to Mum in practice, so the handover actually happens before it's needed rather than being agreed in principle only.

If this isn't enough
"I call the crisis line" is present and specific rather than being the plan's only content, which is good — but its position and reliability are compromised by the phone-in-freezer step immediately before it in the sequence. As currently written, the plan's own structure blocks its own final safety net at the exact moment it's most needed. This needs to be resolved (see coping-steps note above) before the escalation step can be relied on as written.

Collaboration status
Collaboratively developed. The person, Jade, and the caller's mother were all directly involved in building the plan and have each confirmed their specific roles. This is a genuine strength — not just agreement in a calm moment, but named people confirming concrete commitments (Jade replying, Mum holding medication). This status should be recorded as-is; it doesn't need to be downgraded by the phone-freezer issue, which is a design flaw in one step, not evidence the collaboration wasn't real.

Feasibility check
Tested/plausible: the freezer trick as a break-the-spiral technique (person's own account it works), Jade's willingness and reliability, Mum's willingness and access to implement medication removal during risk weeks.
Untested/unknown: early warning signs specific enough to trigger step 1 in time; Jade's availability specifically at late night; how "risk weeks" get flagged to Mum operationally; and — the core issue — no confirmed way to reach Jade or the crisis line if the freezer step doesn't work and the phone is the only route to both.

What needs to happen before this is usable
Go back to the person (and ideally Jade) and resolve the phone-access conflict directly: either the friction method changes to something that doesn't disable the escalation channel, or a backup escalation route independent of the frozen phone is agreed and confirmed. This is a small, concrete fix to an otherwise strong, genuinely collaborative plan — not a reason to discard the plan or start over.
