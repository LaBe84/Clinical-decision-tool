SAFETY PLAN REVIEW

Verdict: Not usable as written. Three of the four core elements are strong,
person-specific, and feasible — but one step (the freezer) creates a
competing safety problem that directly disables the plan's own escalation
step. This is a structural flaw, not a wording issue, and needs to be fixed
before this plan is signed off.

---

Crisis state this plan addresses
Late-night intoxication (3+ drinks) with impulsive contact toward an ex-partner,
in the context of prescribed medication being accessible at home. Consistent
with the established pattern for this caller.

Early warning signs
"3+ drinks and starting to text my ex" — specific, behavioural, and tied to
this person's actual pattern rather than a generic mood cue. This is
plausibly early enough to act on: it names the onset of the risk behaviour
itself rather than a point already deep into crisis. One caveat: by the time
someone is intoxicated enough to be texting an ex, self-monitoring and
follow-through on a planned step can already be degraded — the plan doesn't
address whether this person can reliably execute step 3 below in that state
(see feasibility check).

Independent coping steps
"Phone in the freezer, wrapped in a bag" is specific, memorable, and clearly
designed with this person's actual failure mode in mind (compulsive texting).
As a pure friction technique against contacting the ex, it's well-designed —
concrete, not generic, and plausibly executable even somewhat intoxicated
(a physical action, not one requiring insight or planning).

However, this step cannot stand as written: it disables the only access
point the plan gives this person to their own crisis-line escalation and to
their "CALL FIRST" contact. See "If this isn't enough" below — this is the
plan's central problem, not a minor caveat.

Interpersonal / environmental support
Sister — agreed to hold the medication bottle during weeks flagged in advance
via a Sunday-night check-in text. This is a genuine collaborative
arrangement (not just a named relationship): a specific action, a specific
trigger for it (the Sunday text), and stated willingness. Feasibility
depends on two things not established in the material — whether the sister
reliably receives/acts on the Sunday text every risk week, and what happens
if a risk week arises without warning (impulsive breakup contact doesn't
always give a week's notice). Worth confirming whether there's a fallback if
a risk week is identified only after Sunday has passed.

Means / environmental safety
Medication held by the sister during identified risk weeks is a concrete,
person-specific means-restriction step, implemented by someone who has
agreed to it — this is close to operationally complete, contingent on the
Sunday-text mechanism actually running every week it's needed (see above).

If this isn't enough
This is the plan's critical failure point. The stated escalation is:
"if I still feel unsafe after [the freezer], I call the crisis line, not a
friend." But the crisis line is saved on the phone that the plan has just
instructed the person to seal in a bag and put in the freezer. The plan
gives no alternate route to the crisis line, a landline, a neighbour, or any
other way to reach help once the phone is frozen. As written, the person's
own escalation step is inaccessible for exactly as long as the coping step
is doing its job.

This is not a case where a highly specific, person-tailored technique should
be waved through because it's clearly better than a generic placeholder. A
friction technique that also cuts off the person's ability to call for help
if the friction doesn't work is a net-negative trade, not a positive one,
even though the freezer idea is genuinely well-matched to this person. The
plan needs one of the following before it's usable:
- A means of reaching the crisis line that doesn't depend on the frozen
  phone (e.g., a second device, a landline, a written number kept somewhere
  else, a neighbour/housemate who can be woken), or
- Resequencing so the crisis-line contact happens (or the option to make it
  is preserved) before the phone goes in the freezer — e.g., call/text the
  crisis line first, then freeze the phone as the technique for stopping
  further contact with the ex, rather than freezing the phone as the first
  and only response.

Collaboration status
Reads as person-generated and specific throughout (first-person, concrete
detail, an actual agreement with the sister) — appears collaboratively
developed, not something written up on the person's behalf. This document
does not establish whether the freezer/escalation conflict was noticed and
discussed with the person, or whether it was written and agreed before this
review surfaced it.

Feasibility check
Tested and reasonable: early warning sign is specific and pattern-matched;
sister arrangement is a real agreement, not just an available relationship;
crisis line as the stated escalation target instead of an unreliable friend
is a sound judgment call.

Untested/unknown:
- Whether the person can reliably execute the freezer step, and follow
  through afterward, once already intoxicated and mid-episode.
- What happens if a risk week isn't flagged by Sunday (impulsive contact
  after an unanticipated breakup event).
- Not established in the material provided: whether this person has a phone
  alternative or any other way to reach the crisis line once the primary
  phone is deliberately made inaccessible.

Not usable in current form. The fix needed is narrow and specific: give the
plan a path to the crisis line that survives the freezer step, or reorder
the steps so escalation access is preserved before it's used. Everything
else in the plan can stay as written.
