## Why this doesn't belong in a safety plan

The proposed line — "caller will only contact Lifeline between 9am-5pm on weekdays" — is a restriction on when and how the caller can access the service. It is not:

- a crisis state, early warning sign, coping step, support contact, means-safety action, or escalation step (the six things a safety plan is built from), or
- a risk-reduction intervention aimed at the caller's safety in a specific crisis state.

It is a limit on service access, dressed in safety-plan language. The skill's invariants are explicit on this point:

> Safety planning should reduce reliance on the service where appropriate, but must not become disguised access restriction. A plan that quietly limits when/how someone can contact the service is a CMP question, not a safety-planning outcome — if that's what's actually being proposed, name it and route to CMP/Access Review rather than letting it pass as a safety plan.

And it appears again as a named reasoning shortcut to avoid:

> Quietly building a service-access restriction into a "safety plan" (e.g. "only contact Lifeline between 9-5") without naming it as a CMP question and routing it there.

This is the exact scenario the skill was written to catch.

## The distinction that matters

There's a legitimate concept nearby — a safety plan can reduce reliance on the service by building out the caller's independent coping steps and other support options so the service isn't their only or first resort. That's a genuine safety-planning outcome, built from questions 3 and 4 (what can they do independently; who else can they access).

But "only contact Lifeline between 9am-5pm on weekdays" is a different thing entirely:

- It's a blanket time-boxed restriction on contacting the crisis line itself, not a plan for what the caller does instead.
- It isn't tied to a specific crisis state, feasibility check, or the caller's actual pattern — it's an administrative limit.
- It directly contradicts what a crisis line is for: a caller in crisis outside 9-5 on a weekday would, under this "plan," be instructed not to call the service designed for exactly that moment. A plan that tells someone not to reach the crisis line during a genuine crisis is a safety risk, not a safety intervention.
- Frequent contact is a workload/access management question (capacity, appropriate use, alternative pathways), not a clinical safety question. Reframing it as "safety planning" makes an access-restriction decision without it going through the scrutiny an access restriction requires — that is what "disguised" means here.

## What should happen instead

1. Do not include this content in the safety plan.
2. Name it explicitly: this is a proposed access restriction, not a safety plan element.
3. Route it to CMP/Access Review, where restricting contact is properly evaluated — with its own criteria, review process, and safeguards — separate from clinical safety planning.
4. If frequent contact is itself a signal worth addressing in the safety plan, that's a different, legitimate thread: understand what's driving the frequent contact (per the existing PORF/Suicide Enquiry material) and build person-specific independent coping steps and alternative supports (questions 3 and 4) so the caller has real options besides Lifeline — not a rule about when they're allowed to call it.

## Bottom line

Reject the suggestion as safety-plan content. Name it as a CMP/Access Review question and route it there. If there's a genuine safety-planning need underneath the request (reducing over-reliance on the service), address that separately through person-specific coping steps and support options, not through a contact-hours restriction.
