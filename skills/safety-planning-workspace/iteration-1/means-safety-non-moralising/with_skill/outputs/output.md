Means / environmental safety

PORF material establishes that the caller has access to a firearm at home,
and that this is clinically relevant to their current risk. That finding
converts directly into plan content — not into a test of how seriously the
caller means it, and not into an instruction framed around what they "should"
do to prove commitment to living. The aim is to change what's physically
reachable in a moment of acute risk, independent of the caller's intent at
any given moment.

Before finalising this section, the following needs to be confirmed with the
caller (currently unknown from the material as summarised) — mark each as
resolved or still open in the record:

- Whether the firearm is the caller's own or belongs to someone else in the
  household.
- Whether it is currently stored loaded or unloaded, and where (on person,
  accessible location, locked).
- Whether ammunition is stored with the firearm or separately.
- Who else lives in or has regular access to the home, and whether any of
  them are available, willing, and safe to involve (i.e. not themselves part
  of the crisis pattern, e.g. not the person a conflict is escalating with).
- Whether the caller currently has sole means of removing/securing the
  firearm, or needs someone else to act (this determines whether the plan
  step is something the caller does themselves or something that requires a
  second person).

Plan content (to be finalised once the above is confirmed with the caller —
drafted here as the concrete options this reduces to, not as a generic list):

1. Preferred option — temporary removal from the home.
   "When [recognisable crisis cue — pending Early Warning Signs section] is
   present, I will ask [specific named person, once confirmed available and
   willing] to take the firearm and ammunition out of the house and store
   them somewhere I do not have access to, because removing it from the
   physical environment is more reliable than relying on my own judgement or
   willpower in that state. If no one can do this immediately, [next
   feasible step below] happens first."

2. If removal from the home isn't immediately possible — separation and
   locking, as an interim step, not a substitute.
   Where a lockable storage option exists (gun safe, lockbox) and someone
   other than the caller holds the key/combination: store the firearm
   unloaded, with ammunition locked separately from the firearm and held by
   that other person. This is weaker than removal from the premises and
   should be named as an interim measure in the record, not presented as
   equivalent to it — the plan should note the gap and what would need to
   change (e.g. someone becoming available to take it off-site) to close it.

3. If no other person is currently available or willing (unknown — needs
   confirming), that is itself a gap to record, not to paper over. Do not
   write "family member will secure the firearm" unless a specific person
   has actually agreed to it. If unresolved at the time of this plan, record
   it as an open safety gap and route it explicitly to whoever is
   coordinating follow-up (case manager / next contact), rather than letting
   the plan read as complete when this step has no actual owner yet.

4. Feasibility check specific to this step: confirm whether the caller
   themselves would be the one physically moving/securing the firearm in any
   scenario. If the crisis state involves impulsivity, intoxication, or
   acute distress, a step that requires the caller to act alone at the point
   of risk is weaker than one that has already been actioned in advance
   (i.e. removal completed now, in a calmer state, rather than planned as
   something to do "when it gets bad").

5. This section addresses the firearm specifically because that is what the
   material establishes as clinically relevant. It does not extend to other
   means unless separately established in the Suicide Enquiry/PORF material
   — do not pad this section with generic means-restriction content (e.g.
   medication, other household items) that isn't grounded in what's actually
   known about this person's access and pattern.
