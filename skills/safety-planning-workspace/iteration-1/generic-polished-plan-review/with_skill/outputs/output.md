SAFETY PLAN REVIEW — USABILITY ASSESSMENT

## Bottom line

No, this plan is not usable. It is five generic coping statements that would
apply to almost anyone in almost any crisis. None of it is tested against
the specific state this person actually enters — late at night, alone,
drinking, post-breakup, with prescribed medication in the home and an
established pattern of *impulsive* overdose risk. As written, it is a
document that exists, not a plan that works. Per the foundational rule of
this skill: "has a safety plan" is not "has a usable safety plan."

Walking it line by line against the six operational questions this skill
requires a plan to answer:

## 1. What crisis state are we planning for?

Not named anywhere in the plan. The plan reads as if it could have been
written before anyone knew this person's pattern. It should open by naming
the actual state: e.g. "escalating suicidal ideation late at night, after
drinking alone, in the period following a breakup, with impulsive urges to
take prescribed medication on hand." Nothing in the five items reflects
that this is a *nighttime, intoxicated, impulsive* pattern — which changes
what's feasible for every subsequent step.

## 2. Early warning signs — missing entirely

There is no recognition step. Given the pattern is impulsive, this is the
single most load-bearing gap: if the person can't recognise the state
early, none of the downstream coping steps get a chance to fire before
impulsive action does. The plan needs person-specific cues (e.g. "third
drink," "re-reading old messages," "the thought 'I could just take these'")
captured from Suicide Enquiry material — this review can flag the gap but
can't invent the cues.

## 3. "Practice self-care and mindfulness" / "Engage in a hobby you enjoy" — not feasible in the actual state

These are exactly the placeholder strategies this skill's discipline warns
against. They may be true in general — but has anyone tested whether this
person, late at night, alone, several drinks in, and in acute distress, can
actually access mindfulness or motivation for a hobby? Intoxication and
acute dysregulation typically degrade exactly the executive function these
strategies require. Untested, these read as filler, not content.

## 4. "Reach out to a supportive friend or family member" — unspecified and unverified

No name, no phone number, no note on whether that person is awake, reachable,
and willing at whatever hour this crisis tends to occur, and no check on
whether an ex-partner or breakup-adjacent relationship is itself part of
what's driving the crisis (interpersonal conflict is explicitly one of the
crisis-state factors to check). "A supportive friend or family member exists
in this person's life" is not the same as "a supportive friend or family
member is available and willing to be called at 1am, drunk, mid-crisis."
This needs a specific name, a specific number, and an explicit note on
availability — or a flag that availability is unknown.

## 5. Means / environmental safety — the most serious gap

This is the plan's critical omission. The referral material establishes a
specific, known lethal-means risk: prescribed medication at home, with a
pattern of *impulsive* overdose. The plan contains no means-restriction
content at all. For an impulsive-overdose pattern, reducing access to the
means during the risk window is one of the highest-yield interventions
available — and it is entirely absent. This should be addressed directly
and practically: e.g. medication held by someone else during high-risk
periods, lock-box with delayed access, or reduced on-hand quantity —
whichever is actually feasible given who's around this person and willing.
This is risk reduction, not a test of intent, and it should not be skipped
because it's a harder conversation than "practice self-care."

## 6. "Call the crisis line if things get worse" — escalation content is inadequate on two counts

First, per this skill's explicit rule, a single crisis-line line is not a
complete escalation step, let alone a complete plan — it needs to specify
what "worse" means for this person, and what happens if the crisis line
isn't enough (emergency services, a specific person, going to an ED).
Second, position: this is a nighttime, intoxicated, impulsive pattern with
means at home — closer to a current/near-current risk state than a distant
one. Burying the only escalation instruction as line 5 of 5, after four
lines of generic coping content, doesn't match the urgency of the pattern
being planned for.

## Collaboration status

Not addressed by this review — no information was provided about whether
the person collaborated in producing this plan, partially engaged, or
whether it was written for them. That should be recorded accurately in any
revision rather than assumed.

## Feasibility check

Every element of the existing plan is untested against the actual crisis
state (nighttime, alone, intoxicated, impulsive, post-breakup). None of the
crisis-state stressors this skill requires checking — access, intoxication,
memory/recall in the moment, isolation, interpersonal conflict, shame — are
addressed anywhere in the document.

## What this needs before it can be rebuilt into a usable plan

This review is not a substitute for the missing person-specific detail. To
turn this into something usable, the following needs to come from Suicide
Enquiry / PORF material (or be gathered if it doesn't exist yet), not be
invented here:
- The specific early-warning cues this person notices before the state is
  fully in motion.
- A named, specific support contact with confirmed availability at the
  relevant hours — and clarity on whether an ex-partner is part of the
  crisis trigger rather than a support resource.
- What's actually feasible for this person to do alone, intoxicated, at
  night (current plan assumes capacities — mindfulness, hobby engagement —
  that are unverified and often unavailable in that state).
- A concrete, agreed means-restriction step for the prescribed medication,
  with whoever is willing and available to hold/limit access.
- A specific, appropriately-positioned escalation sequence beyond "call the
  crisis line" — what "not enough" looks like and what happens next.

Once that material exists, this plan should be rebuilt in full using the
skill's output format (crisis state / early warning signs / independent
coping steps / interpersonal-environmental support / means safety / if this
isn't enough / collaboration status / feasibility check) rather than patched
line-by-line — the current structure and sequencing don't match the urgency
of the pattern, not just the content.
