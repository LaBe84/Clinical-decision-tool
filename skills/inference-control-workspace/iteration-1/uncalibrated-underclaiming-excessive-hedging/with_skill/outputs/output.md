INFERENCE CONTROL ANALYSIS

Claim under examination
This caller's ideation has escalated compared to their documented baseline.

Underlying observations
- PORF on file, dated approximately six weeks ago: baseline formulation records passive ideation, no plan, no intent — directly asked about and denied at that time.
- Suicide Enquiry completed this contact: ideation, plan, and intent were asked about directly this contact. The caller disclosed active ideation, a specific plan, and a stated timeframe, in the caller's own words.
- Staff write-up this contact renders the above as: "there may possibly be some indication of a potential change, though this remains quite uncertain and would need further exploration."

Inferential step required
Minimal. This is not a case of inferring a mental state from behaviour, or inferring a pattern from repeated instances — it is a direct comparison of two documented data points, each obtained the same way (direct enquiry, direct disclosure), six weeks apart:
- Time 1 (PORF, six weeks ago): asked directly → denied plan/intent, passive ideation only.
- Time 2 (this contact, Suicide Enquiry): asked directly → disclosed active ideation, specific plan, stated timeframe.
The only step required to get from these two observations to "escalation has occurred" is a comparison along a single, well-defined axis (passive/no plan/no intent → active/plan/timeframe), using the same enquiry method at both points. No causal claim, no extrapolation beyond what was asked and answered, and no reliance on repeated documentation of a single account is involved.

Overreach check
The claim as stated by staff is at risk of pattern 5 — conclusion strength uncalibrated to evidence strength — but in the under-claiming direction: excessive hedging on a comparison that the record actually settles directly. "May possibly," "potential," "quite uncertain," and "would need further exploration" are speculative-tier hedges applied to two directly disclosed, directly comparable data points. This is not a case where the underlying evidence is thin — both time points rest on the caller's own direct answers to direct enquiry, obtained by the same method. Downgrading this to "possible" denies the record the confidence it has actually earned. None of the other four overreach patterns apply: there is no causal claim being made, no repetition-as-corroboration issue (each data point is a single, direct disclosure, not restated), no "consistent with" language drifting toward "demonstrates" (the write-up under-claims, it doesn't over-claim), and no competing explanations have been collapsed — a change in stated ideation, plan, and intent has only one plausible reading once both enquiries are taken at face value.

Competing explanations
None that the record supports naming as equally live. An alternative account would require doubting the reliability of one of the two direct disclosures (e.g., under-disclosure at the PORF baseline, or something specific to this contact prompting over-disclosure) — but no observation in the record raises either possibility. Absent a stated reason to doubt either enquiry, positing an alternative reading would itself be speculative and unsupported by anything documented.

Calibrated conclusion
Established. The caller's ideation this contact — active ideation with a specific plan and a stated timeframe, disclosed directly in the caller's own words — represents a change from the documented baseline of passive ideation with no plan and no intent, denied when asked six weeks ago. Both states were obtained the same way (direct enquiry, direct disclosure) and are directly comparable on the same axis. This does not require an inferential leap beyond reading the two records against each other; it is a direct comparison of two established facts, not a pattern inferred from ambiguous or indirect evidence. The staff write-up's hedged language ("may possibly," "quite uncertain," "would need further exploration") is not warranted by the evidence — it understates a comparison the record settles directly.

What this does not establish
This comparison establishes that a change in stated ideation, plan, and intent has occurred between the two time points. It does not establish why the change occurred, how long the current state has been present, or what level of imminent risk follows from it — those are separate questions for risk formulation and decision-making, not settled by this comparison alone. It also does not retrospectively call into question the accuracy of the six-week-old PORF baseline; it treats that baseline as an established fact as at the time it was taken.

Residual interpretive uncertainty
None on the narrow question of whether escalation occurred — the record settles that directly. Residual uncertainty exists only on downstream questions (imminence, drivers of the change, appropriate response), which are not within scope of this comparison and belong to whichever skill or clinician owns risk formulation and decision-making from here.
