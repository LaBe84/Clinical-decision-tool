INFERENCE CONTROL ANALYSIS

Claim under examination
Contact with the caller's sister is a trigger for deterioration in this caller's presentation.

Underlying observations
- Across four contacts over three months, the caller disclosed a call or visit from their sister in the hours before contacting the helpline.
- Each of these four contacts was documented by a different call-taker.
- On each of these four contacts, the caller's presentation was notably worse than baseline (increased distress, more specific language about hopelessness) compared to contacts not preceded by sister contact.
- No entry references or was influenced by any other entry (i.e., the four documentations are independent of one another, not one account echoed across records).

Inferential step required
To move from "sister contact preceded worse presentation on four independently observed occasions" to "sister contact is a trigger for deterioration," several inferential steps are required: (1) that the temporal sequence (sister contact, then worse presentation) reflects a causal relationship rather than mere association; (2) that sister contact is the operative factor, rather than something correlated with it (e.g., a shared underlying cause, or a pattern in when the caller happens to reach out); (3) that the direction of effect runs from sister contact to deterioration, rather than the reverse or a bidirectional relationship; (4) that four instances, however independently documented, constitute a pattern robust enough to license a causal-strength claim rather than an associative one.

Overreach check
The claim as stated ("is a trigger for") uses causal language for what the record establishes as a temporal, associative pattern — this is at risk of correlation presented as causation (pattern 1). It is not at risk of repetition presented as corroboration (pattern 2): the four instances are genuinely independent — different call-takers, different documented events, no entry referencing or shaped by another — so this is properly counted as four separate corroborating instances, not one account restated. It is at risk of competing explanations collapsed prematurely (pattern 4) if "trigger" is asserted without naming alternative accounts of the same pattern. It is at risk of pattern 5 (uncalibrated strength) if the causal word "trigger" is retained rather than being downgraded to the strength the evidence actually supports.

Competing explanations
- Direct trigger: sister contact itself causes or precipitates the deterioration in presentation (the claim as stated).
- Reverse or bidirectional pathway: the sister may be more likely to call or visit when the caller is already beginning to deteriorate (e.g., picking up on early warning signs through other channels), making sister contact a marker of an already-starting decline rather than its cause.
- Common cause: both sister contact and the caller's contact with the helpline could be driven by a shared underlying factor not captured in these observations (e.g., a recurring stressor, anniversary, or cyclical pattern) that independently increases both the likelihood of sister contact and the severity of presentation.
- Sampling/reporting artefact: the caller may be more likely to disclose a sister contact specifically on days when they are already more distressed and providing more detail generally (i.e., detail and disclosure both track distress level, inflating the apparent link to sister contact rather than reflecting a real trigger relationship).
None of these four accounts is ruled out by the observations as stated; the record does not include a stated mechanism, nor does it include instances that would distinguish direction of effect (e.g., cases where deterioration preceded sister contact, or where sister contact occurred without subsequent helpline contact at all).

Calibrated conclusion
Well-supported: an association between sister contact and worse presentation on that same contact. This rests on four independently documented instances, by different observers, with no cross-referencing between entries, and a consistent finding each time — this is a genuinely robust associative pattern, not a single restated data point.

Not established (at causal strength): that sister contact is a trigger for deterioration in the causal sense. The record supports "consistent with a triggering effect" but does not supply a stated mechanism, does not rule out reverse causation (sister responding to early signs of decline) or a common-cause explanation, and does not include contrast cases (sister contact without deterioration, or deterioration without sister contact) that would help arbitrate between these accounts. Causal language ("trigger") should not be used without qualification; the calibrated statement is that sister contact is well-supported as an associated, temporally-preceding factor, and consistent with (but not demonstrated to be) a causal trigger.

What this does not establish
This does not establish that removing or limiting sister contact would prevent deterioration (an intervention claim, which would require its own evidential basis beyond an observed association). It does not establish the mechanism by which sister contact and deterioration are linked. It does not establish that sister contact is the only or primary factor in the caller's deterioration episodes, since contacts not preceded by sister contact were not analysed here for other preceding factors. It does not establish anything about the sister's intent, behaviour, or role beyond the fact of contact occurring.

Residual interpretive uncertainty
Direction of effect and presence of a common cause remain genuinely open. What would help distinguish between the competing accounts: (a) instances of sister contact that were not followed by deterioration, and instances of deterioration not preceded by sister contact, to establish whether the association is specific and directional rather than reflecting reporting or detail-density effects; (b) some account, even informal, of what happens during these sister contacts (content, tone, nature of the exchange) that would support or undercut a plausible mechanism; (c) information on whether the sister's contact was itself prompted by something (e.g., the sister noticing distress through another channel), which would bear directly on the reverse-causation account. Absent this, the claim should be carried forward as a well-supported association, consistent with a triggering effect, rather than as an established trigger.
