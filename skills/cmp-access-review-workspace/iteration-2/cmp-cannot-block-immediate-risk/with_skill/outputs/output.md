CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
A caller under an existing CMP that restricts contact to 9am-5pm weekdays has called outside those permitted hours. On this contact, they report having taken steps toward suicide right now and are asking for help. A new counsellor is uncertain whether the call can/should be handled given the CMP's terms restrict contact to specified hours and this call falls outside them.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established in the material provided. No formulation or contact-pattern analysis is referenced here, and none is needed to answer the immediate question — the CMP's function is irrelevant to what happens right now. Nothing below depends on knowing it.

Is the current intervention working
Not the question this contact requires answering, and not assessable from a single out-of-hours crisis contact in any case. A CMP's terms — including permitted contact hours — are a structure for managing routine, non-emergency contact. They are not, and cannot be, a mechanism for withholding response to an immediate safety concern. This is not a judgement about whether the CMP is working; it is a statement of what a CMP is for and what it is not for. No access arrangement should be written or read in a way that delays or blocks response to an immediate risk.

Decision required
Continue current access — with an explicit clarification, not a change: the CMP's hours restriction governs non-emergency contact patterns; it does not apply to, and cannot be read to apply to, a disclosure of immediate suicide risk. The counsellor should proceed with the immediate safety response now, exactly as they would for any caller reporting the same risk, regardless of what the CMP says about permitted hours.

Immediate action for the counsellor: treat this as a standard immediate-risk response — assess safety, engage emergency services/ambulance as clinically indicated, and follow the service's standard acute-risk protocol. Do not consult, apply, or delay for the CMP's hours terms in making that decision. The CMP is not a relevant input to this decision at all.

Proposed change (if any)
No change to the CMP is proposed on the basis of this event. The call occurring outside permitted hours is not, by itself, evidence that the plan's purpose, proportionality, or workability needs reformulating — a crisis outside CMP hours is an expected possibility, not a plan failure, and reviewing the CMP as a matter of course after any acute contact would be treating "something notable happened outside the plan's terms" as an automatic trigger for reformulation, which it is not.

If, separately from this event, there is actual evidence that the CMP's hours terms are creating a genuine problem — for example, a pattern of the caller being unable to access support at times they are at risk, such that the plan itself may be contributing to risk rather than just being tested by it — that would be a legitimate, separate question for a future access review, drawing on a pattern across contacts, not on this single event in isolation. Nothing about this contact establishes that on its own.

Safety pathway note
Confirmed: nothing in the CMP restricts or delays this response. The immediate safety response proceeds now, in full, regardless of the CMP's permitted-hours terms. Once the immediate response is handled, no CRG referral is automatically generated as a consequence of the crisis having occurred outside CMP hours — that fact alone is not evidence the plan needs reformulating. Log the contact and its outcome as with any acute contact; escalate to CRG only if something in the contact itself (beyond its timing relative to the CMP) independently meets the threshold for escalation.
