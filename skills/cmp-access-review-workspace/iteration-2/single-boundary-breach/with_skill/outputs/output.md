CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
The caller is under an informal agreement (not a formal CMP) to contact the service only during evening hours. Over six months of this arrangement, contact has run within those terms with a single exception: one call at 2pm, during which the caller was clearly distressed about an acute daytime event. No other out-of-window contact has occurred in the six-month period.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established from the material provided. This is described as an informal agreement rather than a plan grounded in a documented formulation or contact-pattern analysis, and no such analysis has been supplied here. Rather than infer a rationale (e.g. "to manage volume" or "to structure risk-relevant contact") to make this review feel complete, function should be stated as unknown pending reference to any underlying formulation that may exist but wasn't provided.

Is the current intervention working
Six months of near-total adherence to the evening-only window is evidence that the arrangement is workable — the caller can and does follow it. That is a distinct finding from whether the arrangement is achieving a clinical purpose, and the two should not be collapsed. On the workability question specifically, the record is strong: one deviation in six months, and that deviation occurred under circumstances (an acute daytime event, clear distress) that plausibly warranted contact outside the window regardless of what the agreement says. A single out-of-hours contact prompted by an acute event is not, by itself, evidence that the arrangement is failing, unsafe, or being tested/eroded — it is also not, by itself, evidence that the underlying arrangement is clinically effective, since compliance and effectiveness are separate questions and this review has no independent evidence bearing on the latter (no data here on what the evening-only restriction is meant to achieve or whether it's achieving it).

Decision required
Continue current access — no CMP change indicated. The breach is a single, contextually explicable instance of out-of-window contact against six months of adherence; per the invariant that a boundary breach is not an automatic trigger for restriction, there is no basis here to tighten the arrangement. There is equally no basis in this material to formalise the informal agreement into a stricter CMP, since nothing in this event demonstrates the current informal approach is failing to achieve a defined purpose — it demonstrates the opposite, that the arrangement is being respected and that when a genuine acute need arose outside its terms, the caller made contact anyway rather than avoiding help-seeking. If there is a reason to suspect this informal agreement is inconsistently applied by staff, that would be a "clarify/standardise" question — but that would need its own evidence (e.g. staff applying the boundary differently), not this single breach.

Proposed change (if any)
No change proposed. This single instance does not meet the bar for a defined problem the way step 4/5 requires — there is no "because" that ties tightening to what actually happened here (a distressed person contacting once, appropriately, about an acute event, after months of adherence). Manufacturing a tightening in response to this event would be exactly the "one boundary breach therefore restrict further" error this skill is designed to catch, and would also risk penalising appropriate help-seeking during genuine distress.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to an immediate safety concern. The 2pm call was, correctly, responded to as an acute distress contact regardless of the evening-only terms of the informal agreement — that is how the arrangement should function and continue to function. No CRG referral is generated automatically from this out-of-window contact; the event has been reviewed against the arrangement as required, and because it doesn't raise a genuine question about the arrangement's purpose, proportionality, or workability, it is logged as a single explicable exception rather than escalated.
