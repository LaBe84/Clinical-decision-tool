CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
Several counsellors have raised with a manager that a particular caller is
"exhausting" to talk to and have requested a CMP be put in place to limit
contact. The Contact Pattern Analysis for this caller describes frequent
contact that is appropriate, on-topic, and risk-relevant. There is no
evidence in the material provided that the current approach is failing
clinically — no reported disengagement, no reports of missed risk content,
no evidence contacts are unproductive. The concern raised is specifically
about the emotional demand the calls place on staff, not about the pattern
of contact itself being clinically unproductive or unsafe. No existing CMP
is described as being in place for this caller.

Function of current access arrangement
Not formally established as "legitimate use of the service" by this review.
The Contact Pattern Analysis describes the contact as frequent, on-topic,
and risk-relevant — but per this skill's own rule, that description of
content and form is not itself evidence that the function is appropriate
use of the service; that would still be an inference from behaviour, just a
flattering one. Function should be treated as not formally established
unless it has been directly evidenced (e.g., the caller has said what the
contact is for) or genuinely formulated elsewhere. What can be said on
current evidence: there is no indication the pattern is dysfunctional, and
no evidence contradicting continued clinical purposefulness. That is a
different — and weaker — claim than "function is established as
legitimate," and this review does not overstate it into the latter.

Is the current intervention working
There is no current CMP to assess for effectiveness against a defined
clinical purpose, so this question is really "is the current unstructured
access arrangement working clinically?" On the evidence available: yes,
provisionally — the contact is on-topic and risk-relevant, and nothing in
the material indicates the person is being harmed, the risk pathway is
being missed, or the arrangement is failing the caller. What the material
does show is a service-delivery/staff-impact concern, but it is described
only as counsellors' subjective experience of the calls being exhausting —
staff emotional burden, not an evidenced, disproportionate service-delivery
effect (e.g., other callers demonstrably unable to get through, a specific
observed operational failure). Per this skill's invariants, staff emotional
burden is never, by itself, a restriction criterion — it is a supervision
matter. No evidence has been presented that clears the higher bar of
demonstrated service-delivery impact. On the dimensions this step requires
(clinical purpose, harm/impact to the person, harm/impact to staff via
evidenced service effect, impact on other callers, service delivery) there
is currently no basis to say the arrangement is failing.

Decision required
Continue current access — no CMP is clinically indicated on the evidence
presented. The staff difficulty raised is real and should be taken
seriously, but through a different channel: clinical supervision and
support for the counsellors involved, not a change to this caller's access.

Proposed change (if any)
No change proposed. Introducing or tightening a CMP here cannot be
supported: the "because / we expect / we will review by" sentence cannot be
completed with a clinical or safety rationale — the only driver offered is
that staff find the calls exhausting, which this skill treats as a
supervision matter, not a restriction criterion. If a genuine,
evidenced service-delivery impact emerges later (e.g., specific documented
instances of other callers unable to get through because of this caller's
contact volume), that would be a legitimate new input to re-run this review
against — but it would need to be evidenced, not inferred from staff
discomfort, and even then the resulting change would need its own
proportionate, purpose-linked design rather than defaulting to "restrict."

Recommended next step (outside the access decision): the manager should
arrange clinical supervision / debrief support for the counsellors who
raised this, addressing the emotional demand of the work directly, separate
from and without altering the caller's access arrangement.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to any immediate safety
concern. No CMP is being introduced or modified, so there is no new access
structure to reconcile with the safety pathway. If risk content arises in
future contact, it is responded to as it always would be, independent of
this review's outcome.
