CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
A CMP was introduced 3 months ago, limiting contact to two named counsellors and
structured 15-minute check-ins, in response to previously very high, distressed,
unstructured contact. Contact volume remains high — 5-6 contacts a week — under
the current plan. Since the CMP began, calls are consistently brief, calm, and
focused on a specific agreed coping plan, and each contact ends with clear
closure. No signs of unaddressed risk have been present in recent contacts.

Function of current access arrangement
Not separately re-derived here (this review draws on, rather than redoes,
formulation/pattern-analysis work). Based on the material available, the
plan's function is to provide structured, contained access to a small,
consistent set of counsellors while the caller works a defined coping plan —
consistent with the original purpose for which the CMP was introduced.

Is the current intervention working
Yes, on the evidence available. The relevant test is not whether volume has
dropped — it hasn't, and that alone is neither success nor failure — but
whether access, as currently structured, is achieving its clinical purpose
without disproportionate harm to the person, staff, other callers, or service
delivery:

- Clinical purpose: the CMP was introduced to address distressed, unstructured
  contact. Contacts are now brief, calm, focused on the agreed coping plan,
  and reach clear closure — this is a direct, on-target change in exactly the
  dimension the plan was designed to address, not just a superficially calmer
  pattern.
- Risk: no signs of unaddressed risk in recent contacts; the structure is not
  suppressing risk-relevant content from being reached.
- Impact on the person: contacts are purposeful and end with closure rather
  than the previous distressed, unresolved pattern — no evidence of the
  arrangement making the service feel unusable to them.
- Impact on staff/service: no evidenced, disproportionate service-delivery
  impact has been described (e.g. other callers demonstrably unable to get
  through, a specific observed operational failure). Volume remaining high is
  a fact about load, not, by itself, evidence of service-delivery harm — and
  staff finding sustained contact effortful, if that's part of what's prompting
  this question, is a supervision matter, not a restriction criterion on its
  own, unless there is actual evidence of disproportionate service impact.

Sustained compliance with the boundary (calls staying within the structured
format) tells us the arrangement is workable, but the finding of effectiveness
here rests on more than compliance alone: the content and outcome of the
calls (calm, focused, purposeful, closure, no unaddressed risk) directly match
the plan's intended clinical purpose. That's the basis for calling this
working, not the fact that the caller has adhered to it for three months.

Decision required
Continue current access. The CMP is achieving its intended clinical purpose
and no evidenced, disproportionate service-delivery impact has been
identified. High volume alone does not justify further restriction.

Proposed change (if any)
No change proposed. Tightening the CMP further is not supported: doing so
would be restricting on the basis of volume and/or staff burden rather than
on evidence that the current structure has stopped working, which the
"because / we expect / we will review by" test cannot honestly complete —
there is no specific problem for a tighter plan to fix. If staff experience
of sustained contact is a live concern, raise it through supervision
separately from this access decision. Recommend routine re-review of the CMP
on the service's normal review cycle (or sooner if the pattern changes — e.g.
calls lengthen, lose focus, lose closure, or risk content stops being
reached), rather than a reactive tightening now.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to any immediate safety
concern. The two-counsellor, structured-check-in arrangement continues to sit
alongside, not instead of, the standard immediate-risk response pathway,
which remains fully available regardless of CMP terms.
