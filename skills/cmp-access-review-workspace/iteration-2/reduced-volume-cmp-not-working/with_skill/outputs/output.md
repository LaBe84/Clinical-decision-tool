CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
A CMP was introduced 2 months ago after high contact volume was flagged as a
service concern, restricting the caller to one contact per day within a
specific time window. Contact volume has since dropped from daily to roughly
weekly. The caller has told a counsellor they "don't bother calling anymore
because it's too complicated." The last two contacts that did occur involved
the caller minimising distress and ending the call quickly, without the
counsellor able to explore how they were coping.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established. The original CMP was introduced in response to a service
concern (volume), not a documented formulation of what the caller's frequent
contact was for. No PORF Formulation or Contact Pattern Analysis output is
referenced as available here. The function of the caller's original contact
pattern — and therefore what the CMP is actually meant to be protecting or
enabling — remains unknown, and that gap should not be filled by inference at
this stage.

Is the current intervention working
No, on the evidence available. Volume has dropped, but a volume drop is not
itself success — the specific pattern here (reduced volume, disengagement,
and contact ending before risk or coping can be explored) matches the
failure case this skill is designed to catch: a caller whose contact volume
has dropped sharply after a restrictive CMP but who now disengages before
discussing risk and reports the service no longer feels usable. Two direct
indicators of harm outweigh the volume metric:
- The caller has explicitly stated the arrangement has made the service too
  complicated to use ("don't bother calling anymore").
- The remaining contacts show a changed and worse quality: minimisation of
  distress and early termination, preventing the counsellor from exploring
  coping — i.e., the restriction is associated with less risk-relevant
  content being reached, not more purposeful contact.

There is no evidence in this scenario that the reduced volume reflects the
caller's needs being better met elsewhere or contact becoming more purposeful;
the direct account from the caller and the pattern of the last two contacts
both point the other way. The original volume concern was a real operational
pressure at the time, but that operational reality does not retroactively
establish that the one-contact-per-day/time-window restriction had a valid
clinical rationale — it only explains why a restriction was introduced, not
whether this restriction is achieving anything now.

Decision required
Review/reduce restriction.

The current arrangement is more restrictive than the evidence supports. The
restriction appears to be impairing usability — the caller is disengaging and
minimising — while the original contact function was never established. Per
this skill's own guidance, the defensible move here is to reduce or loosen
the restriction and establish function, not to design a detailed replacement
structure on top of a function that is still unknown. Building a more
elaborate CMP now would repeat the same error that created this problem:
structuring access around a service concern rather than a clinical
understanding of what the caller needs from contact.

Proposed change (if any)
We are changing access from a fixed one-contact-per-day, restricted-time-
window limit to a loosened arrangement (e.g., an increased daily allowance
and/or a wider contact window, set collaboratively with the caller) because
the current restriction is associated with the caller reporting the service
feels too complicated to use and with a marked drop in the quality of contact
(minimisation, early termination, coping not explored) rather than any
established clinical purpose being achieved; we expect this to result in the
caller re-engaging in fuller, explorable contact and coping being
discussable again; we will review whether it has done so by 4 weeks from
implementation, using both contact-quality indicators (does the caller stay
on the call, is coping explored) and the caller's own report of whether the
service feels usable — not volume alone.

Alongside this change, function should be actively established (through
direct conversation with the caller and/or referral for PORF Formulation)
rather than assumed. If function remains unclear after the loosened
arrangement is tried, that is evidence for a formulation review, not a
reason to reintroduce or tighten restriction.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to any immediate safety
concern. The loosened access arrangement does not alter the caller's ability
to reach the service in a genuine crisis, and any immediate risk identified
during a contact — whenever it occurs — should be responded to through the
standard immediate-risk pathway regardless of CMP terms.
