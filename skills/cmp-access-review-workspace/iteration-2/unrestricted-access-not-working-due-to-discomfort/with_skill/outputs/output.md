CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
No CMP is in place; access is currently unrestricted. Over several months the
contact pattern has become extremely frequent, chaotic, and unstructured.
Multiple counsellors, independently, report that they are unable to complete
a meaningful conversation with this caller: the caller talks over them,
contacts shift topic rapidly, and no session — across contacts and across
staff — reaches closure or a coping plan. No CMP has been proposed to date;
the stated reason is not a clinical judgement that access is fine as-is, but
staff discomfort with suggesting a boundary might be needed.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established. No formulation or contact-pattern analysis is referenced
here to indicate why this caller contacts as frequently or chaotically as
they do. That absence is preserved as a genuine unknown rather than filled
in — it is not itself evidence for or against restriction.

Is the current intervention working
No. This is assessed against whether access, as currently structured, is
achieving a clinical purpose without disproportionate harm to the person,
staff, or service delivery — not against volume or calm alone. The evidence
here isn't a single missed proxy (e.g., one call not reaching a coping plan)
but a broad, repeated pattern: across months, multiple contacts, and
multiple independent counsellors, no session reaches closure or any usable
outcome, and sessions are structurally derailed by the caller talking over
staff and changing topic rapidly. That consistency across staff and time is
what makes this a genuine failure signal rather than a rigid hidden
standard being applied. Unrestricted access is not itself the problem — high
contact volume alone would not justify intervention — the problem is that
the current unstructured format is not enabling any contact, by anyone, to
reach a usable endpoint. This also is not simply a staff-comfort question:
the inability to complete a session is an observed, repeated operational
and clinical fact, not a feeling of difficulty.

Decision required
Introduce a CMP — with two caveats built into how it's framed. First, because
function is unknown, this CMP must be introduced explicitly as a limited,
testable structural intervention aimed at enabling calls to reach some usable
closure — not as a full formulation of what the caller needs or why they
contact this way. If it doesn't achieve that, the correct response is to
route to formulation/CRG review, not to tighten further. Second, the fact
that staff have avoided proposing this due to discomfort is not itself
evidence the unrestricted approach is right — discomfort about proposing a
boundary is not a clinical rationale for withholding one, any more than
staff difficulty alone would be a rationale for imposing one. The decision
rests on the repeated, cross-staff evidence above, not on the discomfort
either direction.

Proposed change (if any)
We are changing access from unrestricted, unstructured contact to
unrestricted contact conducted under a brief structural agreement (e.g., a
shared understanding at the start of each call about staying with one topic
at a time and allowing the counsellor to speak without being talked over,
with counsellors trained/supported to name and gently re-apply this
structure in-call), because repeated, cross-staff evidence over several
months shows the current unstructured format is preventing any session from
reaching closure or a workable outcome for the caller or the counsellor. We
expect this to achieve at least some sessions reaching closure or an
identifiable next step, and a reduction in counsellors reporting an inability
to complete the conversation; we will review whether it has done so in 4–6
weeks, drawing on counsellor feedback across multiple contacts (not a single
call) and, if function remains unclear at that point, escalate for shared
formulation rather than tightening access further.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to an immediate safety
concern. The structural agreement proposed is a conversational scaffold, not
an access limit — the caller retains full access, and any immediate risk
raised during a call is responded to as an immediate risk regardless of
where the call is in relation to the agreed structure.
