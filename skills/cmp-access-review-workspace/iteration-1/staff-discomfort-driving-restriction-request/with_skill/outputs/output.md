CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
Several counsellors have raised with a manager that a particular caller is
"exhausting" to talk to and have requested a CMP to limit contact. The
available contact pattern analysis describes the caller's contact as
frequent, but appropriate, on-topic, and risk-relevant, with no evidence
identified that the current (unrestricted/no-CMP) approach is failing
clinically. There is no existing CMP in place.

Function of current access arrangement
Not formally separated from the pattern analysis in the material provided,
but the pattern analysis's own characterisation — frequent, on-topic,
risk-relevant contact — indicates the caller is using the service for its
intended purpose: to bring risk-relevant material to the service when
needed. Nothing in the material suggests the contact serves a function other
than that, and nothing suggests the current unstructured approach is failing
to meet that function.

Is the current intervention working
There is no current CMP to assess as an intervention — the question here is
whether the current unrestricted access arrangement is achieving its
clinical purpose without disproportionate harm to the person, staff, other
callers, or service delivery.
- Clinical purpose (person): being met — contact is on-topic and
  risk-relevant, i.e., the caller is engaging productively with the
  service.
- Harm to the person: none identified; nothing suggests restriction is
  needed to protect the caller.
- Harm to other callers/service delivery: not evidenced in the material —
  no indication that this caller's volume is displacing access for others
  or compromising service delivery.
- Impact on staff: real and named — several counsellors independently
  describe the contact as emotionally exhausting. This is a genuine finding,
  but it is a staff-support/workload finding, not evidence that the contact
  pattern itself is clinically unproductive or that access needs to change.
  Per the skill's invariants, service/staff impact is not evidence of
  client intent, dysfunction, or a need for restriction on its own, and
  staff difficulty must not be folded into the access decision.
No boundary breach, disengagement before discussing risk, delayed
help-seeking, or other marker of an access problem has been identified.

Decision required
Continue current access — no CMP. The contact pattern analysis found
frequent, appropriate, on-topic, risk-relevant contact with no evidence the
current approach isn't working clinically. There is no defined clinical or
safety problem for a CMP to solve; introducing one here would be using a
clinical intervention to manage staff emotional demand, which the skill
explicitly rules out.

Proposed change (if any)
No change to access is proposed. Emotional demand on staff is a real and
separate problem and should be addressed directly through the appropriate
channel — case-specific and general supervision, workload/rostering
adjustments (e.g. rotating who takes this caller's contacts), and debriefing
support for the counsellors who raised it — rather than through restricting
the caller's access. This should be fed back to the manager and the
counsellors explicitly: their experience is being taken seriously and acted
on, just through staff support rather than a CMP, because the material does
not support a clinical rationale for restricting this caller's contact.

If, going forward, new evidence emerges that the contact pattern itself is
not working clinically (e.g., contacts stop being risk-relevant, the caller
begins disengaging before discussing risk, or genuine service-delivery harm
to other callers is identified), this should be brought back through a fresh
CMP/Access Review at that point, on the actual evidence available then —
not manufactured now to resolve staff discomfort.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to any immediate safety
concern. No CMP is being introduced, so there is no new access structure to
create a safety-pathway risk; the caller's existing full access to the
service, including for risk-relevant contact, is unchanged.
