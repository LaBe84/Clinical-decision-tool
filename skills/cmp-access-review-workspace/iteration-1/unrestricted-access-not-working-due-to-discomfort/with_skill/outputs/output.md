CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
No CMP or access restriction is currently in place; the caller has unrestricted access to the service. Over several months, contact has become extremely frequent, chaotic, and unstructured. Multiple counsellors independently report the same pattern: the caller talks over them, contacts shift topic rapidly, and no session — across any counsellor, across several months — has reached closure or resulted in a coping plan. No CMP has been proposed to date; the stated reason is not a clinical judgement that access is fine as it is, but staff discomfort with suggesting a boundary might be needed.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established. No PORF Formulation or Contact Pattern Analysis output has been provided for this caller, and none is referenced as having been done. It would be a mistake to infer a function here (e.g. "attention-seeking," "avoidance of closure," "the chaos is the point") to make this review feel more complete — per the foundational rule that unknown is not negative evidence and behaviour is not function. What can be said without inference: the pattern is consistent across multiple counsellors and has persisted for months, which suggests it is not counsellor-specific or a one-off, but this observation describes the pattern's stability, not its function.

Is the current intervention working
No — on the terms this review has to use (clinical purpose achieved, and impact on the person, staff, other callers, and service delivery), not on volume or calm alone.

- High contact volume is not itself the problem, and is not being treated as one here — high use of a crisis line can be exactly what it's for.
- The specific finding is that contact never reaches closure or a coping plan, over months, across multiple counsellors. That is a direct failure against the service's clinical purpose: crisis contact is meant to move toward some form of stabilisation, plan, or safe close, not to be structurally prevented from getting there.
- This also has a plausible cost to the caller, not just to staff: if no session ever reaches a coping plan, the caller is not getting the thing the service exists to provide, regardless of how much contact time they are having.
- The impact on staff (repeated inability to complete a meaningful conversation) and on the service (capacity consumed by contacts that structurally cannot conclude) are real and relevant to this test, but they are not, on their own, the basis for restricting access — they are one input among several, alongside the impact on the caller of never reaching a plan.
- The current unrestricted arrangement is therefore not a neutral or safe default. Leaving it in place is itself a decision with a consequence (no session ever closing) — it does not become the "least restrictive" option just because no one has proposed changing it.

Decision required
Introduce a CMP.

This is not being decided because volume is high, because staff find the calls difficult, or because a boundary would relieve staff discomfort — none of those are, on their own, a valid basis under this framework. It is decided because steps 1–3 establish a specific, defined problem: across a period of months and multiple counsellors, contact structurally does not reach closure or a coping plan. That is a concrete, checkable clinical gap a plan can be built to address, independent of whatever the caller's underlying function turns out to be.

Two things are explicitly not being decided here:
- This is not "Escalate for shared formulation" — the problem itself (no session reaching closure) is already clear enough to act on; formulation of the caller's underlying function is missing, but that gap should be pursued in parallel, not used as a reason to defer structuring the contact itself.
- This is not a volume-based or punitive restriction. Nothing in this review proposes reducing how often the caller may contact the service.

Proposed change
We are changing access from unstructured, unrestricted contact to a CMP that structures each contact around a brief opening check-in, an agreed focus for the call, and an explicit closing/coping-plan step (with counsellors given a consistent, shared approach to hold this structure, e.g. naming the structure at the start of the call and returning to it if the conversation fragments), because contact has gone months without reaching closure or a coping plan under the current unstructured approach and this has been consistent across multiple counsellors, not counsellor-specific; and we expect this to achieve sessions reaching an identifiable close or coping-plan step in a clear majority of contacts, and a reduction in counsellors reporting they were unable to complete a meaningful conversation; we will review whether it has done so in 4–6 weeks, using session notes across counsellors as the check.

This proposed structure is about how contact is organised, not about limiting frequency, method, or duration of contact — it is the least restrictive change that speaks to the actual problem identified. Alongside it, referral for a fuller formulation of the caller's contact pattern is recommended, since function remains unestablished and should inform any future revision of this plan. Staff discomfort with raising a boundary, and the difficulty of holding these calls, are real and should be named and supported through supervision — but they are not, themselves, why this CMP is being introduced, and no further restriction beyond this structural change is supported by what steps 1–3 establish.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this CMP restricts, delays, or gates the caller's ability to reach the service, and nothing in it should be read as requiring the structure to be completed before an immediate safety concern is responded to. If risk content emerges at any point in a contact, in or out of the agreed structure, it is responded to immediately and the structure is set aside for that purpose.
