CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
An existing CMP was introduced three months ago, limiting the caller's contact to two named counsellors and structured 15-minute check-ins, in response to previously very high, distressed, unstructured contact. Contact volume has remained high since — 5-6 contacts a week — but the pattern within those contacts has changed: calls are consistently brief, calm, and focused on a specific, agreed coping plan. Contacts end with clear closure. No signs of unaddressed risk have appeared in recent contacts.

Function of current access arrangement
The CMP was introduced to address distressed, unstructured contact by giving the caller consistent counsellors and a predictable, time-bounded structure. Per the reported pattern, this is directly evidenced as the arrangement in current use: contacts are now organised around the agreed coping plan and reliably reach closure, consistent with the plan's intended purpose rather than an inferred or assumed function.

Is the current intervention working
Yes, on the terms that matter. The question is not whether volume has dropped — it hasn't, and that is not itself a problem — but whether access as currently structured is achieving its clinical purpose without disproportionate harm to the person, staff, other callers, or service delivery. The evidence indicates it is: contacts are shorter, calmer, purposeful, tied to an agreed plan, and consistently reach clear closure, with no signs of unaddressed risk. This is a stable pattern, but it is not merely stable — the *quality* and *purpose* of contact has measurably changed from the pre-CMP state the plan was designed to address, and risk-relevant content is being reached and resolved within contacts rather than avoided. There is no indication in the material that the continued volume is creating disproportionate strain on the two named counsellors or the service that would point toward the arrangement failing on the "impact to staff/service" arm of this test; if that strain exists it has not been reported here and would need to be raised separately rather than assumed.

Decision required
Continue current access — no change to the CMP. High contact volume alone is not evidence that the intervention needs tightening; the plan is achieving the specific outcome it was introduced for (structured, calm, closure-reaching contact), and volume was never the target of the intervention.

Proposed change (if any)
No change proposed. Tightening the CMP further — e.g., reducing counsellor access or shortening check-ins — would not be tied to any identified problem from steps 1–3; it would be responding to volume alone, which this skill's invariants explicitly rule out as a rationale ("high contact ≠ dysfunctional contact," "do not increase restriction merely because the current arrangement is difficult for staff"). If ongoing volume is creating strain on the two named counsellors specifically, that is a staff-support/supervision matter to raise alongside this review, not a basis for restricting the caller's access.

Recommended next step: maintain the current CMP terms, and set a routine review point (e.g., 3 months from now, in line with the original review cadence) to confirm the pattern holds rather than reopening the question now.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to an immediate safety concern. The CMP's structured check-ins and named-counsellor terms remain subordinate to the immediate risk pathway, which stays fully open regardless of contact volume or plan terms.
