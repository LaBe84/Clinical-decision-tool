CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
A restrictive CMP was introduced 2 months ago, limiting the caller to one contact per day within a specific time window. The trigger was high contact volume, flagged as a service concern. Since introduction, contact frequency has dropped from daily to roughly weekly. Separately, the caller has told a counsellor they "don't bother calling anymore because it's too complicated." The last two contacts that did occur involved the caller minimising distress and ending the call quickly, without the counsellor being able to explore how the caller was coping.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established from the material provided. The original CMP appears to have been introduced in response to service/volume concern rather than a documented formulation of what function the caller's frequent contact was serving. No PORF Formulation or Contact Pattern Analysis output is referenced here to say why the caller was contacting daily beforehand, or what the restriction was clinically intended to achieve beyond reducing volume. This is a gap to name, not to fill by inference.

Is the current intervention working
No. Volume reduction alone is not evidence of success, and here the surrounding evidence points the other way:

- The caller's own account ("don't bother calling anymore because it's too complicated") indicates the drop in contact reflects reduced usability of the service under the new structure, not reduced need or improved coping.
- The last two actual contacts show a change in contact quality consistent with harm, not improvement: the caller is minimising distress and ending calls quickly, and the counsellor has been unable to explore coping — meaning risk-relevant content is very plausibly not being reached.
- There is no evidence the caller is coping better, being supported elsewhere, or that the underlying need that drove daily contact has resolved. The plausible alternative reading is that the caller has learned the arrangement doesn't accommodate them, and is compressing or suppressing what they raise to fit inside a single, time-windowed daily slot they no longer trust will be available or sufficient.
- Weighed against harm to the person: this pattern (delayed/reduced access to support, distress minimisation, unexplored coping) is a plausible-to-likely negative impact on the caller's safety and wellbeing. Weighed against service impact: the volume concern that prompted the CMP has technically been addressed, but at the apparent cost of the service no longer functioning as a usable point of contact for this caller.

This is the second pattern the skill is specifically built to catch: reduced utilisation is not treatment success here — it looks like the CMP achieving the opposite of its clinical purpose, by making the service harder to use in a way that suppresses exactly the content (coping, risk) staff need to reach.

Decision required
Modify existing CMP.

The plan has achieved its administrative aim (volume down) but is not achieving — and appears to be actively working against — a clinical purpose, since remaining contacts are shorter, more guarded, and not reaching risk/coping content. It is not a "continue" (the current structure is not working), and the evidence does not yet clearly show the whole restriction is unwarranted (an original volume concern was real), so "review/reduce restriction" is folded into how the plan should be modified rather than treated as a bare removal. It is not an escalation on its own, but if the modification below doesn't restore usability and access to risk-relevant content, this should go to CRG/shared formulation next, since function was never established here.

Proposed change (if any)
We are changing access from a rigid one-contact-per-day, fixed-time-window CMP to a modified CMP that preserves a defined contact structure (to keep the original volume concern addressed) but removes the elements the caller has identified as making the service too complicated to use — for example, widening or clarifying the time window, and explicitly building in that a contact reaching risk or distress content will not be cut short or treated as a breach of the plan — because the current rigid structure is associated with reduced usability, minimised distress, and unexplored coping in the caller's own words and in the last two contacts. We expect this to result in the caller re-engaging at a frequency closer to their actual need, and in contacts reaching and being able to explore coping/risk content rather than ending quickly; we will review whether it has done so within 4 weeks, using both contact frequency and a qualitative check (via the counsellor(s) involved) of whether risk/coping content is now being reached in contacts.

This modification should also include obtaining or building the missing function analysis (via PORF Formulation or Contact Pattern Analysis) that was never established for the original CMP, so any further change is grounded in why the caller was contacting frequently in the first place, not only in managing volume.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays a response to an immediate safety concern. Any contact in which risk is raised must be responded to on its own terms regardless of CMP terms, time window, or contact count — the CMP structures routine access, it does not gate the safety pathway. This is being made explicit in the proposed modification itself, given evidence that the current rigid structure may already be discouraging the caller from raising distress.
