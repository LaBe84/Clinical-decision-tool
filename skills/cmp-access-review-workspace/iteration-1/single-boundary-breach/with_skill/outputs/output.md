CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
The caller is under an informal agreement (not a formal CMP) to contact the service only during evening hours. Over a six-month period under this arrangement, the caller has contacted outside the agreed window on one occasion: a single daytime call at 2pm, during which they were clearly distressed about an acute event occurring that day. This is the only contact outside the agreed window in six months; all other contact in that period has fallen within the agreed evening hours.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established in the material provided. No formulation or contact-pattern analysis is referenced here to indicate why evening-only contact was agreed (e.g. daytime unavailability of the caller, alignment with a support network's availability, structuring contact around a daytime commitment, or a clinical rationale specific to this person). This review does not infer a function to fill that gap.

Is the current intervention working
Judged against adherence and purpose rather than the single breach in isolation: the caller has maintained the agreed pattern in all but one contact across six months, indicating the informal arrangement is being respected and is workable for the caller. The one exception was not a pattern of drift, pressure-testing, or escalating disregard for the agreement — it was a single instance of contacting during an acute, unplanned daytime event, which is a plausible and appropriate reason to depart from a routine-hours agreement. There is no evidence in this material that the breach caused disproportionate harm to the person, staff, other callers, or service delivery, and no evidence the underlying arrangement has stopped serving its (unstated) purpose. A single breach under acute circumstances, against an otherwise unbroken six-month pattern, is information about the arrangement's resilience, not evidence that it has failed.

Decision required
Continue current access — the informal arrangement is working as evidenced by six months of near-total adherence, and the single breach does not on its own indicate the arrangement is no longer clinically purposeful, proportionate, or workable.

Proposed change (if any)
No change proposed. Note for consideration outside the access decision itself: because the arrangement is informal, it may be worth briefly confirming with the caller (and documenting) that contact during a genuine acute daytime crisis is understood as acceptable and will not be treated as a breach requiring consequence — this is a clarity/communication point, not a tightening of access, and is optional rather than required by this review.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in this review restricts or delays response to an immediate safety concern. The evening-hours agreement is a routine-contact convention, not a barrier to crisis contact; the caller's daytime call was responded to appropriately as an acute presentation, and this review does not recommend anything that would discourage or block similar contact in future if genuine acute need arises outside the agreed hours.
