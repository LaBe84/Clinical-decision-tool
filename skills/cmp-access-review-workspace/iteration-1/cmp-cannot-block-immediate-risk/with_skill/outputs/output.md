CMP / ACCESS REVIEW

What is happening
A caller with an existing CMP restricting contact to 9am–5pm weekdays has
called outside those permitted hours. On this contact, they report having
taken steps toward suicide and state they need help right now. A new
counsellor is uncertain whether the call can be answered given the CMP's
hours-of-contact terms.

Function of current access arrangement
Not established in the material provided — no formulation or contact
pattern analysis is referenced here. This review does not infer a function
to fill that gap. Regardless of what that function turns out to be, no CMP
function can extend to withholding response to an active, immediate safety
concern; that limit does not depend on knowing the plan's purpose.

Is the current intervention working
Not the question this contact raises, and not answerable from an
out-of-hours safety disclosure alone — a single contact of this kind is not
evidence about whether the CMP's structure is achieving its clinical
purpose over time. That evaluation is a separate, later piece of work (see
Decision required) and must not be conflated with, or allowed to delay,
the immediate response.

Decision required
Immediate risk response required now, outside and prior to any CMP
decision. The CMP question — whether the plan needs review, modification,
or clarification in light of this out-of-hours crisis contact — should be
escalated for shared formulation (CRG) after the immediate safety response
is complete, since a caller reaching crisis outside permitted contact
hours may itself be information about whether the current arrangement is
still workable. That review is not for this counsellor to resolve alone or
in the moment, and it must not be resolved by reading the CMP as a bar to
responding tonight.

Proposed change (if any)
No change to access is proposed or decided at this point in time. If, once
the immediate risk is addressed, review of the CMP is warranted (e.g.
because out-of-hours crisis contact is a recurring pattern, or because the
9am–5pm restriction is itself contributing to delayed help-seeking), that
review should go through the full five-question process above, including
the "we are changing access from X to Y because... we expect... we will
review by..." statement — not be decided as a byproduct of this incident.

Safety pathway note
Nothing in the CMP restricts or delays response to an immediate safety
concern, and nothing in this review does either. A CMP structures how
access is normally provided; it is not, and cannot be read as, a condition
on responding to an active risk-to-life disclosure. The counsellor should
proceed immediately with standard immediate-risk/safety response procedure
exactly as they would for any caller in this situation, treating the
CMP's hours-of-contact term as inapplicable to this contact. The
uncertainty a new counsellor felt here is a supervision and induction
issue — CMP terms and their limits should be clarified in supervision
after the call — not a reason to hesitate on the call itself.
