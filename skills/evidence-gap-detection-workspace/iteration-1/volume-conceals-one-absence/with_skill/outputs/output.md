EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller's access to means been adequately assessed for safety
planning purposes?

Established evidence
The case file contains eleven pages of contact history spanning two years,
covering: mood over time, relationship difficulties, work stress, coping
strategies the caller has tried, and prior contacts with other services.
These are genuinely established — they are documented, repeated, and
cross-referenced across multiple entries. What is established, in other
words, is a detailed picture of the caller's psychosocial context and
help-seeking history.

Evidence gaps
Access to means — no entry across any of the eleven pages, at any point in
the two-year history, records whether the caller currently has access to
any specific means. This is not a case of the question being asked and
answered negatively (which would be absent evidence), and it is not a case
of different entries disagreeing (which would be contradictory evidence).
It is missing evidence: the domain does not appear to have been assessed
at all. Volume of documentation on adjacent topics (mood, relationships,
work, coping, service contacts) does not substitute for this — none of
that material addresses means access, however thorough it looks in
aggregate.

Gap classification
Access to means: Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Apply the counterfactual test: if this were resolved in either plausible
direction, would it change the decision of whether means access has been
adequately assessed for safety planning?

- If resolved as "no current access to any specific means" — this would
  directly inform what a safety plan needs to address (or does not need to
  address) and would materially change the content and confidence of the
  plan.
- If resolved as "caller currently has access to [specific means]" — this
  would materially change the safety plan itself (means restriction
  becomes a live element) and would change the urgency and shape of the
  response.

Both directions produce a materially different safety plan. Because means
access is not assessed anywhere in the record, safety planning cannot
currently rely on either direction — the plan would be proceeding on an
assumption rather than on an established fact. This is what makes the gap
safety-critical rather than merely decision-critical: it is not just that
the plan's specifics would change, but that no safety plan reliant on
means status can currently be said to rest on established information.
This is an epistemic limit on what can be concluded, not a claim that the
caller is at elevated risk — the absence of the assessment is what is
being flagged, not a risk level.

What remains supportable
The psychosocial picture — mood pattern, relationship and work stressors,
coping strategies attempted, and history of service contact — is well
established and can continue to inform formulation, rapport, and other
elements of the case that do not depend on means status.

What is not supportable
A safety plan, or any claim that safety planning has been adequately
completed, cannot be supported on the current record with respect to
means. The eleven pages of history, however thorough on other domains,
cannot be read as reassurance that means access is low-risk or not
applicable — that would convert an unassessed domain into a reassuring
finding, which the record does not support.

Information requirement
A direct assessment of current access to specific means is needed. Naming
this as a requirement is as far as this analysis goes — how and when that
assessment is obtained, and what happens to the safety-planning process in
the meantime, is a matter for whichever process owns safety planning, not
for this audit.

Residual uncertainty
None of the other domains in the file (mood, relationship difficulties,
work stress, coping strategies, prior service contacts) present a
counterfactual-test failure for this specific decision — they are relevant
context but their further elaboration would not change whether means
access has been adequately assessed. Only the means-access gap is
decision-relevant here; it has been classified above as safety-critical
rather than residual.
