Materiality analysis
Counterfactual test applied to each gap: if employment history were resolved
in either plausible direction (e.g. stable job vs. recent job loss), would it
change whether immediate safety action is needed right now? No — the decision
under examination is already answered by directly established evidence
(current intent, specific plan, access to means). Learning about employment
history could add context for later formulation work, but it cannot undo or
outweigh an already-established combination of intent, plan, and means. The
same reasoning applies to family-of-origin relationships: whatever that
history contains, it does not bear on whether the caller is in immediate
danger right now, because immediacy has already been established directly,
not inferred from absence of these domains.

Neither gap is safety-critical either: a safety-critical gap is one whose
absence prevents safe reliance on the decision. Here, the decision does not
rely on employment or family-of-origin information at all — it relies on the
intent/plan/means disclosure, which is present and direct. There is no
epistemic reliance on the missing domains for this specific decision, so
"cannot safely conclude" does not apply to them.

What remains supportable
The conclusion that immediate safety action is warranted is fully supportable
on the evidence already established (active intent, specific plan, access to
means, disclosed directly). This conclusion does not depend on, and is not
weakened by, the unexplored employment and family-of-origin domains.

What is not supportable
A broader psychosocial formulation of this caller (e.g. precipitating
stressors, relational support network, occupational risk factors) is not yet
supportable — those domains remain unexplored. But that limitation applies to
formulation, not to the immediate-safety-action decision under examination.

Information requirement
None required to support the immediate-safety-action decision itself.
Employment history and family-of-origin relationships would be relevant
information for later formulation, safety planning, or ongoing-risk work, if
and when those processes proceed — named here as the type of information
that domain would need, not as a requirement blocking the current decision.

Residual uncertainty
Employment history and family-of-origin relationships can remain unresolved
for this contact without blocking or qualifying the immediate-safety-action
decision. They should be named for the record as unexplored, not treated as
reassuring or as evidence of anything, and not allowed to delay action that
the already-established evidence already supports.
