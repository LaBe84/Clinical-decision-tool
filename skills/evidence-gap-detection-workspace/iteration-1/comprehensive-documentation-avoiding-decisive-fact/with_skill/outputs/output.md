EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller ever made a previous suicide attempt?

Established evidence
The lifetime-history section contains detailed, well-organised entries on:
family history, previous service contacts, mental health diagnoses,
medication history, and social history. These entries are genuinely
established — they document specific, named domains with apparent
thoroughness. None of them, individually or collectively, records whether
the caller has or has not made a previous suicide attempt. At no point does
any entry state the answer to that specific question, directly or as a
documented negative.

Evidence gaps
- Previous suicide attempt history — missing (never asked/assessed, as far
  as the record shows). This is not "asked, denied" and it is not
  "sources disagree." The record simply does not contain an entry that
  addresses the question at all. It is missing evidence, not absent
  evidence — there is no basis here for concluding either "yes" or "no."

No contradictory evidence was found on this question, because the question
is never engaged with anywhere in the section — contradiction requires two
answers, and there is zero.

Gap classification
Previous suicide attempt history: Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Counterfactual test: if this question were resolved in either plausible
direction, would it change the decision under examination?

- If resolved as "yes, prior attempt(s)" — this is one of the most
  established individual risk factors for future attempts and directly
  changes what can responsibly be concluded about this caller's risk
  profile and what response is proportionate.
- If resolved as "no prior attempts" — that is also a materially different
  evidentiary position from not knowing, and it too changes what can be
  concluded (absence of this factor, rather than absence of information
  about this factor).

Both directions plausibly change the decision. The gap therefore passes the
counterfactual test as decision-critical at minimum. Because the specific
question under examination — has this caller ever made a previous
attempt — cannot be answered at all from the material presented, and that
question is the one the decision is defined by, the record cannot currently
support a safe conclusion either way. This is not evidence of risk (an
unknown is not negative evidence, and it is not positive evidence either);
it is an epistemic limit on what can be said. That makes it
safety-critical, not merely decision-critical: the decision "has this
caller ever made a previous attempt" cannot be relied upon as answered
by what's in front of us, regardless of how the rest of the section reads.

The volume and organisation of the surrounding material (family history,
service contacts, diagnoses, medication, social history) does not bear on
this materiality analysis. Thoroughness on adjacent domains is not evidence
that the decisive question was addressed, and it should not be read as
reassurance that the answer is implicitly "no" or "probably not documented
because there's nothing to document."

What remains supportable
- The record can be treated as containing genuine, established detail on
  family history, prior service contacts, diagnoses, medication, and social
  history — those domains are not in question.
- It is accurate to state, as a finding, that the lifetime-history section
  does not address prior attempt history anywhere within it.

What is not supportable
- Any conclusion that the caller has made a previous attempt.
- Any conclusion that the caller has not made a previous attempt.
- Any reading of the section's thoroughness elsewhere as implying an answer
  to this question in either direction (e.g., "if there had been an
  attempt, surely it would appear somewhere in this much detail" is an
  inference from a plausible pattern, not an established fact, and is
  exactly the kind of gap-filling this analysis must not perform).
- Proceeding with the decision under examination as though it has been
  answered.

Information requirement
A direct assessment of prior suicide-attempt history is needed: whether the
caller has ever made a previous attempt, and if so, when, how, and any
related detail the owning process requires. This is a distinct enquiry
question from the domains already covered (family history, service
contacts, diagnoses, medication, social history) and is not satisfied by
any of them.

Residual uncertainty
None of the surrounding, already-established domains need further
resolution for this decision — the family history, service-contact,
diagnostic, medication, and social-history entries can stand as they are.
The only outstanding item is the one named above, and it is not residual:
it is safety-critical to the decision under examination.
