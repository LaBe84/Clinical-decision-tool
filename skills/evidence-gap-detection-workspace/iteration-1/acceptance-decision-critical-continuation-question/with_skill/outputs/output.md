EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Should the current intervention — fortnightly check-in calls, in place for
eighteen months — continue? No escalation is being proposed; the question is
whether continuation of the status quo is supportable.

Established evidence
- PORF has found the caller's presentation stable relative to baseline: no
  material change in Risk State, no new plan or intent. This is a direct,
  documented assessment finding — assessed, not absent.
- The intervention (fortnightly check-in calls) has been running for eighteen
  months. This duration is established.
- The intervention has never been reviewed against a target since it was set
  up. This is established as a fact about the record, not an inference.
- No one is proposing escalation. Established as the current stance of those
  involved.

Evidence gaps
1. Whether the intervention has a defined target or objective it was set up
   to achieve — missing (never established in the material presented; no
   indication a target was ever articulated at set-up or since).
2. Whether the intervention is still achieving whatever function it originally
   served (as opposed to merely coinciding with a stable presentation) —
   missing. Stability of presentation has been assessed; whether that
   stability is attributable to, or independent of, the fortnightly calls has
   not been assessed.
3. Whether continuing at the current frequency, a different frequency, or
   stepping down has been considered against any criteria — missing. No
   review has occurred, so no comparative judgement exists in the record.
4. The caller's own view of the calls (burden, helpfulness, preference) —
   missing. Not mentioned in the material presented.

Gap classification
1. Absence of a defined target — Decision-critical gap.
2. Whether stability is attributable to the intervention — Residual
   uncertainty.
3. Absence of any comparative review of frequency/format — Residual
   uncertainty (for the continue/not-continue decision as framed); would be
   decision-critical only if a change in intervention were being proposed,
   which it is not.
4. Caller's own view of the calls — Residual uncertainty.

Materiality analysis

Gap 1 — absence of a defined target
Counterfactual: if a target were established and the presentation is found to
meet it, that supports continuation on defined terms. If a target were
established and the presentation already exceeds or has moved past what the
target required (e.g., the target was stabilisation, and stabilisation has
now held for eighteen months), that could support stepping the intervention
down rather than continuing it unchanged. Either resolution plausibly changes
what "continue" means in practice — continue unchanged, continue with a
defined endpoint, or continue at reduced intensity. Because the direction of
resolution isn't fixed and both directions are plausible outcomes that
alter the shape of the decision, this is decision-critical — not to whether
contact continues, but to whether "continue exactly as configured" versus
"continue under a defined, reviewable target" is the supportable decision.

Gap 2 — attribution of stability to the intervention
Counterfactual: if the fortnightly calls were shown to be the operative
factor in stability, that would support continuation. If stability were shown
to be independent of the calls (e.g., attributable to other supports), that
would support reduced intensity rather than no contact at all — it does not
support stopping, since stability could still be protected by less frequent
contact rather than none. In neither direction does resolving this change
the immediate decision (continue vs. discontinue) — it only affects a
follow-on question about intensity, which is not under examination here.
Residual uncertainty.

Gap 3 — no comparative review of frequency/format
Counterfactual: no one is proposing to change frequency or format, and no
change is under examination. Resolving this either way would inform a future
decision about frequency, not the present decision about whether continuation
is supportable now. Residual uncertainty for this decision.

Gap 4 — caller's own view
Counterfactual: if the caller found the calls burdensome or unwanted, that
could argue against continuation as currently configured; if the caller finds
them valued, that supports continuation. This is plausibly relevant, but
nothing in the presented material indicates the caller has raised concern,
and PORF stability findings do not by themselves establish caller
dissatisfaction. Absent a specific signal, this remains residual uncertainty
rather than decision-critical for the present continue/discontinue question,
but it is closely adjacent to Gap 1 (informs what "review against a target"
should include if undertaken).

What remains supportable
- Continuation of the fortnightly check-in calls in the near term is
  supportable: presentation is stable relative to baseline, no material
  change in Risk State, no new plan or intent, and no escalation is
  indicated by anything in the material.
- The absence of a prior review does not itself argue for discontinuing or
  escalating the intervention — an unreviewed intervention with a stable
  presentation is not evidence of harm or of ineffectiveness.

What is not supportable
- That the intervention is continuing because it is meeting a defined,
  agreed target — no such target has ever been established, so this
  cannot be claimed as the basis for continuation.
- That the current configuration (fortnightly, indefinite, no defined
  target) has been actively judged as the right one — it has not been
  judged at all; it has simply persisted.
- Treating eighteen months of unreviewed continuation as itself evidence
  that the intervention is working — duration and stability are not the
  same claim as effectiveness against a target that was never set.

Information requirement
- A defined target or review criterion for the intervention, so that
  "continue" can be assessed against something rather than by default.
- If undertaken, the caller's own view of the intervention's frequency and
  usefulness, to inform what the review considers.

Residual uncertainty
- Whether the fortnightly calls are the operative cause of the stable
  presentation, as opposed to incidental to it.
- Whether a different frequency or format would serve equally well.
- The caller's specific view of the calls, absent any indication it has been
  raised as a concern.
These may legitimately remain unresolved without blocking the decision to
continue in the near term.
