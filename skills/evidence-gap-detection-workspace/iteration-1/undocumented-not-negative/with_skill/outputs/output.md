EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Is it safe to conclude this caller has no current suicide plan?

Established evidence
The Suicide Enquiry notes document mood, recent stressors, and general coping. These are direct assessments in their own right — mood was assessed, stressors were assessed, coping was assessed. Nothing in the material states that plan or intent were directly asked about at any point in this contact.

Evidence gaps
Whether the caller has a current suicide plan — missing (never asked/assessed). The record covers adjacent domains (mood, stressors, coping) but contains no documentation that plan or intent were raised, explored, or that the caller volunteered anything about either. This is not a case of "asked, denied" — there is no record of the question being put to the caller at all, so this is missing evidence, not absent evidence.

Gap classification
Whether a current plan exists: Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Apply the counterfactual test: if this gap were resolved in either plausible direction, would the decision change?
- If directly asked and the caller denies a plan, that is absent evidence — a specific, documented finding that could support (though not by itself guarantee) a conclusion of no current plan.
- If directly asked and the caller discloses a plan, that materially changes the decision — a plan being present is the central fact the decision "is it safe to conclude no plan exists" is asking about.
Both directions plainly change what can be concluded, and the gap concerns the exact fact the decision turns on — not an adjacent or peripheral one. That makes it not merely decision-critical but safety-critical: the absence of any record that plan or intent were asked about means the material cannot be relied on to support a "no plan" conclusion, regardless of how thorough the surrounding mood/stressors/coping documentation is. Thoroughness on adjacent questions does not stand in for the decisive question having been asked.

What remains supportable
The record supports documented conclusions about the caller's mood, recent stressors, and general coping, as assessed. It does not support any conclusion — positive or negative — about plan or intent, because that domain was not addressed in what's presented.

What is not supportable
"No current suicide plan" is not supportable from this material. This is not because there is evidence of a plan, and it should not be read as evidence of risk — it is simply that the record does not establish the answer either way. Equally unsupportable is treating the absence of plan/intent documentation as reassuring; unknown is not negative evidence, and the volume of documentation on mood, stressors, and coping does not substitute for the decisive question having been asked.

Information requirement
A direct enquiry into current suicide plan and intent, conducted and documented as part of this contact (or a documented account of why it could not be conducted), is needed before a conclusion about presence or absence of a plan can be supported.

Residual uncertainty
None of the gap identified here is residual — it sits squarely on the decision under examination. Other unknowns not raised in the material presented (e.g., aspects of history outside mood/stressors/coping) are outside the scope of this specific decision and are not addressed by this analysis.
