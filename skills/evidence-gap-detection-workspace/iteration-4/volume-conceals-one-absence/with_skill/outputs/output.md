EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller's access to means been adequately assessed for safety
planning purposes?

Established evidence
The case file contains eleven pages of contact history spanning two years,
covering: mood, relationship difficulties, work stress, coping strategies
tried, and prior service contacts. These are documented in detail and
repeatedly, across multiple entries and contacts over time.

Evidence gaps
- Access to means: missing evidence. No entry across the two-year record —
  including the most recent contacts — records whether the caller currently
  has access to any specific means. This was never asked or assessed at any
  point in the material presented; it is not that the question was put and
  answered negatively (which would be absent evidence), and it is not that
  different entries disagree (which would be contradictory evidence). It is
  simply not addressed anywhere in the eleven pages.

Gap classification
- Access to means: Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Decision under examination, restated precisely: whether the record supports
concluding that means access has been adequately assessed for safety
planning purposes.

Counterfactual test: if this gap were resolved in either plausible
direction —
- the caller is found to have current access to specific means, or
- the caller is found not to have current access to specific means —

either answer changes what can be relied on for the decision under
examination, because the decision is specifically about whether this
assessment has occurred and to what it can be safely anchored. Since the
record contains no entry addressing means access at all, there is no basis
in the material to conclude the assessment has been done in either
direction. The absence of any signal pointing toward concern in the existing
eleven pages does not resolve this — mood, relationship, work-stress, coping,
and prior-contact information do not stand in for a means-access finding,
however extensively they are documented. This is safety-critical rather than
merely decision-critical because the decision under examination is itself a
safety-planning-adequacy question, and the unresolved gap directly prevents
safe reliance on that specific determination right now.

What remains supportable
The record supports conclusions about the caller's documented mood history,
relationship and work-related stressors, coping strategies attempted, and
pattern of prior service contact over two years. These are established and
can be relied on for whatever they individually bear on.

What is not supportable
The record does not support a conclusion that access to means has been
assessed, in either direction. It cannot be concluded that means access is
known to be present, known to be absent, or has been ruled in or out by any
prior contact. Volume and apparent thoroughness of the surrounding material
does not substitute for this.

Information requirement
A direct assessment of the caller's current access to specific means,
undertaken and recorded as its own item, distinct from mood, relationship,
work, coping, or prior-contact history.

Residual uncertainty
None identified beyond the gap named above; no other candidate gap in this
material was tested as bearing on the decision under examination.
