EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Is it safe to conclude this caller has no current suicide plan?

Established evidence
The Suicide Enquiry notes document mood, recent stressors, and general
coping. Nothing in the material records that plan was directly asked about
at any point in this contact — there is no documented question and no
documented answer, positive or negative, on plan status specifically.

Evidence gaps

1. Plan status — missing evidence. The record does not show that plan was
   asked about or assessed at all in this contact. This is not "asked,
   denied" (absent evidence) — it is "never asked" (missing evidence), and
   the two must not be flattened together. Nothing in the mood, stressor,
   or coping content stands in for a direct plan enquiry; those are adjacent
   topics, not a plan assessment by another name.

Note on intent (named, not folded into the plan gap)
Intent is a closely related variable to plan, and it is also not documented
as having been directly asked about in this contact. However, the decision
under examination here is stated precisely as plan status — whether a plan
exists. Resolving intent, in either direction, does not establish whether a
plan exists, and resolving plan does not establish intent. Per the rule
against bundling adjacent clinically-related variables into one gap, intent
is therefore named here as a separate, adjacent unknown rather than merged
into the plan gap above. It is not classified against the plan decision in
this analysis. If the decision actually needing to be made is wider (e.g.,
"is it safe to conclude no current plan or intent"), that is a different,
wider decision and would need its own evidence-gap analysis stating that
proposition explicitly.

Gap classification
1. Plan status — Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Gap 1 (plan status): Apply the counterfactual test at the decision's actual
stated granularity — "is it safe to conclude this caller has no current
plan?"
- If plan were resolved as "no plan present" — the decision under
  examination would be supported.
- If plan were resolved as "plan present" — the decision under examination
  would be false, and reliant on this conclusion would be unsafe.
Both plausible resolutions change the decision, so the gap passes the
counterfactual test and is at least decision-critical. It rises to
safety-critical because the decision under examination is itself a direct
safety conclusion ("no current plan"), and the absence of any direct
enquiry into plan means that conclusion cannot currently be relied upon one
way or the other — not because a plan is suspected, but because the one
piece of evidence the decision turns on was never gathered. The
thoroughness of the mood, stressor, and coping content does not substitute
for this: those are adjacent domains, and documentation volume elsewhere is
not evidence that the decisive question was addressed. No signal in the
record currently points toward a plan being present — that absence of
signal does not downgrade the classification, since materiality (not
likelihood) governs whether a gap is safety-critical.

What remains supportable
The material supports statements about the caller's mood, recent stressors,
and general coping as documented in this contact. It does not support any
conclusion — positive or negative — about plan status.

What is not supportable
The conclusion "this caller has no current suicide plan" cannot be
supported from the material presented. This is not because anything in the
record points toward a plan being present; it is because plan was not
established either way, and an absence of documentation on plan cannot be
read as a negative finding on plan.

Information requirement
Direct enquiry into current plan (whether a plan exists, and if so, its
elements) is needed before the decision under examination can be concluded
safely one way or the other. Whether and how to seek that information is a
matter for whichever skill owns enquiry or risk formulation, not this
analysis.

Residual uncertainty
None identified for the plan decision as stated — the single gap
identified is safety-critical, not residual, so nothing here can be treated
as safely deferrable for this specific decision. (Intent, as noted above,
remains an unresolved adjacent variable but is not part of this decision's
residual-uncertainty accounting; it would need its own gap analysis against
its own stated decision.)
