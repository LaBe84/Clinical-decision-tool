EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Can a safety plan currently be built and relied on for this caller — i.e.,
can we proceed to construct and depend on a written or phone-based safety
plan now, on the basis that the caller has agreed in principle to one?

Established evidence
- The caller has agreed in principle to having a safety plan. This is a
  disclosed preference/agreement, directly stated in the material.
- Nothing further about the caller's literacy, housing situation, or phone
  access is present in the material.

Evidence gaps

1. Literacy (ability to read/use a written plan) — missing. Never asked or
   assessed anywhere in the material.
2. Housing stability — missing. Never asked or assessed anywhere in the
   material.
3. Phone access — missing. Never asked or assessed anywhere in the
   material, including whether the caller has any phone access at all.

None of these are absent-evidence findings (nothing was asked and answered
negatively) and none are contradictory — all three are simple silences in
the record.

Gap classification

1. Literacy — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on any written
   component of the plan.
2. Housing stability — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on any
   plan component that depends on a stable location (e.g. where written or
   physical materials would be kept or accessed, or where location-dependent
   steps would be carried out).
3. Phone access — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on any
   phone-based component of the plan.

Materiality analysis

Literacy: If resolved as "caller can read and use written material," a
written plan component can be relied on. If resolved as "caller cannot read
or has significant reading difficulty," a written plan component cannot be
relied on and would need to be built around a different modality. Both
plausible answers change what can safely be concluded about whether a
written component of the plan is usable — this passes the counterfactual
test. The absence of any signal suggesting a literacy problem does not
demote this: materiality, not likelihood, governs. This gap prevents safe
reliance on written components specifically, not on the decision of whether
a plan can exist in some form.

Housing stability: If resolved as "caller has stable, consistent housing,"
location-dependent components of a plan can be relied on to function as
intended. If resolved as "caller's housing is unstable or absent," those
same components cannot be relied on in the same way. Both answers change
what can safely be concluded about location-dependent components. This
passes the counterfactual test independently of the literacy gap — it is
not derivative of it, and does not need housing resolved to test literacy or
vice versa.

Phone access: If resolved as "caller has reliable phone access," a
phone-based component can be relied on. If resolved as "caller has no phone
access, intermittent access, or shared/unsafe access," a phone-based
component cannot be relied on as configured. Both answers change what can
safely be concluded about phone-dependent components. This passes the
counterfactual test independently of the other two gaps.

These three gaps are independent of one another, not dependencies of a
single parent gap: resolving housing tells us nothing about literacy or
phone access, and vice versa. Each blocks reliance on a different, specific
category of plan component; none of them blocks reliance on every possible
plan format, and none needs to be resolved before the others can be tested
or answered.

What remains supportable
The caller's agreement in principle to having a safety plan is established
and can be treated as such — proceeding to discuss the existence and
acceptability of a safety plan with the caller is supportable. That
agreement itself is not undermined by any of the three gaps.

What is not supportable
That any specific written, phone-based, or location-dependent component of
a safety plan can currently be relied on to function as intended for this
caller. The material does not establish whether the caller could read a
written plan, access a phone to use a phone-based component, or rely on a
stable location for location-dependent components. Building or endorsing
those specific components now, on the unstated assumption that these are
not problems, is not supportable from what is in the material.

Information requirement
- Whether the caller can read/use written material, to the degree a written
  plan component would require.
- The caller's current housing situation and its stability.
- Whether the caller has phone access, and if so, its nature and
  reliability.

Residual uncertainty
None of these three are residual uncertainty for this decision — each
independently passes the counterfactual test against a specific class of
plan component. No other gap was identified in the material that meets the
threshold for nomination (i.e., implied by the decision, ordinarily expected
content, contradicted in the record, or directly relevant to a specific
element the decision depends on) beyond these three.
