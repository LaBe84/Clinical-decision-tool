EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller's access to means been adequately assessed for safety
planning purposes?

Established evidence
The eleven pages of contact history document, across two years: mood,
relationship difficulties, work stress, coping strategies tried, and prior
service contacts. These are genuinely established by direct contact-record
entries. Nothing in this material addresses whether the caller currently has
access to any specific means. No entry records that means access was asked
about and answered (in either direction), and no entry records it being
explicitly declined or deferred.

Evidence gaps
- Means access (current, specific): missing (never asked/assessed). The
  record does not show this question being put to the caller at any point
  across two years of contacts, nor any incidental disclosure that would
  establish it either way.

Gap classification
- Means access (current, specific): Safety-critical gap.

Materiality analysis
Decision as stated: whether means access has been adequately assessed for
safety-planning purposes — not whether the caller is at risk, and not
whether a safety plan should exist at all.

Counterfactual test: if means-access status were resolved in either
plausible direction —
- If resolved as "no access to means of concern" — a safety plan could
  reasonably proceed without means-restriction components, or with those
  components framed as reinforcing an already-favourable position.
- If resolved as "has access to specific means" — a safety plan would need
  to account for that access directly; proceeding without that knowledge
  means any means-restriction element of the plan would be built on an
  assumption rather than an established fact.

Both directions produce a materially different, defensible safety plan with
respect to means. Because the current decision under examination is
specifically whether means access has been adequately assessed for
safety-planning purposes, and the answer is that it has not been assessed at
all, this gap directly prevents safe reliance on that specific determination
now. This is not a claim that the caller is at elevated risk — it is that
the record does not permit a conclusion either way, and a safety plan's
means-related content cannot currently be said to rest on an assessed fact.

Volume note: the eleven pages of detailed, adjacent material (mood,
relationship strain, work stress, coping strategies, prior contacts) do not
bear on this question. Thoroughness on those domains is not evidence that
means access was addressed — it is a separate line of enquiry that this
record happens not to contain, regardless of how much surrounding detail
exists.

What remains supportable
The mood, relationship, work-stress, coping-strategy, and prior-contact
history are established and can inform whichever parts of a safety plan
depend on those domains. Non-means-dependent elements of a safety plan (e.g.
those resting on coping strategies or contact history) are not blocked by
this particular gap.

What is not supportable
A conclusion that access to means has been adequately assessed. Any
means-restriction element of a safety plan cannot currently be said to rest
on an established fact about this caller's actual access.

Information requirement
Direct assessment of the caller's current access to specific means, as a
distinct enquiry — not inferable from mood, relationship, work-stress, or
coping-strategy content already in the record.

Residual uncertainty
None identified beyond the means-access gap itself; the two years of
adjacent material do not raise further unresolved questions that bear on
this specific decision.
