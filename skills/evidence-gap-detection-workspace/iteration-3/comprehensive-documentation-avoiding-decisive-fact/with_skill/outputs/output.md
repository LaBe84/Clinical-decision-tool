EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller ever made a previous suicide attempt — does the enquiry
record, as it stands, establish an answer to that specific question (yes,
no, or genuinely unknown)?

Established evidence
The lifetime-history section documents, in detail and well organised:
family history, previous service contacts, mental health diagnoses,
medication history, and social history. These are genuinely established —
they are recorded findings, not gaps, and each answers the question it
was collecting for. What is not among them, at any point in that section
or elsewhere in the material presented, is a direct statement addressing
prior suicide attempt: no entry says an attempt occurred, no entry says
one was asked about and denied, and no entry states the question was
considered and left open. The decisive fact the section reads as though
it is building toward is never actually recorded.

Evidence gaps
1. Previous suicide attempt status — missing (never asked/assessed, as
   far as the material shows). This is not "absent evidence" (asked,
   denied) — nothing in the record indicates the question was ever put to
   the caller or checked against prior service contacts. It is not
   contradictory — no two entries disagree. It is a missing entry: the
   surrounding record covers adjacent domains (diagnoses, contacts,
   medication, family history, social history) at length without ever
   closing on this one.

Gap classification
1. Previous suicide attempt status — Decision-critical gap, with respect
   to the decision as stated (does the record establish a prior attempt).
   This gap would become safety-critical only if a current safety
   decision (e.g., a safety plan or risk formulation) were being built or
   endorsed on the assumption of no prior attempt — that reliance has not
   been stated as part of the decision under examination here, so it is
   classified on the factual question actually posed, not upgraded on the
   assumption of how it might be used downstream.

Materiality analysis
Previous suicide attempt status: apply the counterfactual test at the
stated decision's granularity ("has this caller ever made a previous
attempt — does the record establish an answer"). If resolved as "yes, one
or more previous attempts" — the answer to the decision under examination
is yes, and that fact was absent from a section that otherwise looks
exhaustive. If resolved as "no, no previous attempt" — the answer is no,
and it means the section's apparent thoroughness happened to be
accurate on this point, but only by coincidence of outcome, not because
the record actually said so. Either resolution changes what can currently
be stated in answer to the decision (which is presently: not
established, not: no attempt). The gap therefore passes the counterfactual
test and is decision-critical. This holds regardless of how the rest of
the section reads — the volume and organisation of adjacent history
(family, contacts, diagnoses, medication, social) is not evidence toward
either branch; thoroughness on those questions says nothing about which
way this one resolves, and should not be read as a signal in either
direction.

What remains supportable
The lifetime-history section can be relied on for what it actually
documents: family history, previous service contact pattern, diagnoses,
medication history, and social history, as recorded. Those conclusions
stand on their own evidence and are not touched by this gap.

What is not supportable
Any statement that the caller has, or has not, made a previous suicide
attempt. Neither branch is currently supportable from this record. It is
also not supportable to treat the section's overall thoroughness as
implying an answer in either direction (e.g., "this level of detail would
surely have mentioned an attempt if there'd been one" is exactly the
kind of inference this analysis excludes) — thoroughness elsewhere is not
evidence about the one question the section never states.

Information requirement
Direct confirmation, from the caller or from prior service records, of
whether a previous suicide attempt has occurred and, if so, when and in
what form the record holds that information (disclosure, service contact
note, or other documented source). This is a factual confirmation, not a
new assessment domain — the surrounding history-taking already covers
the ordinary domains; this is the one specific question that was not
closed out.

Residual uncertainty
None identified beyond the named gap. This analysis does not extend to
whether other undocumented details exist in the case (specific dates,
methods, or circumstances of any past contact) — those become relevant
only once the primary question (attempt: yes/no/unknown) is itself
resolved, and are not tested here as they were not presented as
implied by, or necessary to, the decision under examination.
