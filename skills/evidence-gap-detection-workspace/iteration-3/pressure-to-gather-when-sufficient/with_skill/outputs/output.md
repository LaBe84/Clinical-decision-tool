EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Is there enough established to formulate current risk state for this caller,
based on this contact? (Not: is the full history complete; not: what
intervention follows; not: is the case file thorough overall — the decision
in front of us is specifically whether current risk state can be
formulated.)

Established evidence

recorded for each:
- Current thoughts
- Plan
- Timeframe
- Means
- Intent

These are the direct components a current risk state formulation depends on.
Each was directly assessed (not inferred, not assumed), and each is
supported by a direct quote rather than a paraphrase or summary judgement.

Evidence gaps
The supervisor has requested "more history" but has not identified any
specific fact, domain, or question this refers to. Under this skill's
no-speculative-gap-generation rule, a request to gather more information is
only a valid candidate gap if it names something that is implied by the
decision, ordinarily expected content for it, contradicted in the record, or
directly relevant to a specific element the decision depends on. "Let's get
more history, I want to be thorough" does none of these — it does not name
a fact, does not point at a domain the current material is silent on in a
way that bears on this decision, and does not identify a contradiction.

There is therefore no nominated gap to classify against the decision under
examination. Historical information in general (e.g. prior attempts,
psychiatric history, past contacts) is not established in this record, but
it has not been shown to bear on formulating current risk state specifically
— current thoughts, plan, timeframe, means, and intent are the direct
components of that formulation, and all five are established with direct
quotes.

Gap classification
No material gap — for the decision "is there enough established to
formulate current risk state." The request for more history, as stated,
does not meet this skill's threshold to be treated as a nominated gap at
all; it is thoroughness-seeking rather than a specific evidentiary
question.

If the supervisor can name what the history is expected to establish (e.g.
"has this person made a prior attempt, because that would change how we
weight the current plan," or a specific contradiction they've noticed), that
named item would need to be tested against the current-risk-state decision
in its own right, separately from this analysis.

Materiality analysis
Not applicable in the strict sense, since no specific gap has been
identified to run the counterfactual test against. To make the boundary
explicit: general social, psychiatric, or contact history is the kind of
information that is often relevant to a broader clinical picture or to
future planning, but its absence does not change what can currently be said
about current thoughts, plan, timeframe, means, or intent — each of those
was asked and answered directly this contact. Resolving "history" in either
direction (extensive prior history vs. none) would not alter the fact that
current thoughts, plan, timeframe, means, and intent are directly
established for this contact. Continuing to gather information at this
point is not diligence — it is gathering past the point of
decision-sufficiency for the decision actually in front of us.

What remains supportable
Current risk state can be formulated from what has been directly
established this contact: current thoughts, plan, timeframe, means, and
intent, each with a direct quote.

What is not supportable
A claim that the caller's full history is known, or that nothing else is
ever relevant to this caller's care, is not supportable — but that is a
different and broader claim than the one the decision under examination
asks. This analysis does not extend to decisions such as long-term
formulation, intervention selection, or triage destination, which may have
their own information requirements.

Information requirement
None identified for the decision under examination. If a specific historical

Residual uncertainty

legitimately stay that way for purposes of formulating current risk state
