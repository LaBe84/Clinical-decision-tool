EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Should the current intervention — fortnightly check-in calls, as configured,
in place for eighteen months — continue. This is the decision as stated: it
concerns the current configuration (frequency and format), not merely
"contact versus no contact." No one is proposing escalation, and this
analysis does not treat escalation as live.

Established evidence
- PORF (Prior Occurrence / Risk-Factor review, or equivalent risk-state
  reassessment) has directly assessed the caller's current presentation
  against baseline: stable, no material change in Risk State, no new plan
  or intent. This is a direct, current assessment, not an inference from
  absence of contact.
- The intervention has been running at fortnightly frequency for eighteen
  months (established fact of duration and configuration).
- No party involved is currently proposing escalation (established
  position of those reviewing the case, not itself evidence about the
  intervention's adequacy).

Evidence gaps

1. Target-and-review gap. The intervention has never been reviewed against
   a target or goal since it was set up eighteen months ago. Whether a
   target was ever defined, and whether the intervention has been
   evaluated against it at any point, is missing evidence — the question
   has not been asked or assessed in the material.

2. Configuration-adequacy gap. Whether fortnightly frequency (as opposed to
   a different frequency or format) remains the appropriate configuration
   for this caller's current level of need is missing evidence. The
   material establishes that the caller's presentation is stable; it does
   not establish anything about whether the current configuration is the
   one that produces or matches that stability, or whether another
   configuration would serve equally well or better.

3. Caller's own view gap. The caller's current view on the acceptability
   or burden of fortnightly contact is not established anywhere in the
   material presented.

Evidence noted but not treated as an independent gap: whether the observed
stability is causally attributable to the intervention, or would hold
independent of it, is also unestablished. This is addressed under residual
uncertainty below rather than listed as a fourth gap, since it does not
independently drive the classification once gaps 1–3 are accounted for.

Gap classification

1. Target-and-review gap — Decision-critical.
2. Configuration-adequacy gap — Decision-critical.
3. Caller's own view gap — Decision-critical.

No gap identified here rises to safety-critical: nothing in the material
indicates that reliance on the current arrangement is currently unsafe:
PORF has directly established stability and no new plan/intent, which is
itself a safety-relevant finding, not an absence.

Materiality analysis

1. Target-and-review gap. If a target was defined and the intervention is
   shown to meet it, that supports continuation on its current terms. If a
   target was defined and the intervention is not meeting it — or no
   target was ever defined at all — that changes what "continue" can
   defensibly mean: continuing an intervention that has never been
   measured against any stated purpose is a different and weaker claim
   than continuing one that has been reviewed and found to be working.
   Either plausible resolution changes what can be said about the
   intervention's standing, so this passes the counterfactual test:
   decision-critical.

2. Configuration-adequacy gap. If fortnightly frequency is confirmed as
   matched to current need, that supports continuing unchanged. If it
   is found to be more or less frequent than current need requires, that
   would support a different configuration under the same broad decision
   of "what ongoing contact arrangement is warranted." Per the fixed
   decision granularity, this is material to the decision under
   examination as stated (continuation of the intervention as configured),
   not a separate follow-on question — a different plausible answer
   changes what "continue" would mean in practice. Decision-critical.

3. Caller's own view gap. The decision concerns continuation of a specific
   configuration that lands on the caller directly (frequency, format,
   burden of fortnightly contact over eighteen months). Per the explicit
   preference threshold, where a decision is about acceptability,
   configuration, or burden, the person's own view is often the fact the
   counterfactual test turns on. If the caller finds fortnightly contact
   acceptable and sustainable, that supports continuing as configured. If
   the caller finds it burdensome, or would prefer a different
   arrangement, that would count against continuing unchanged. Either
   plausible answer moves the decision: decision-critical.

Note on absence of a "signal": there is no indication in the material that
the target was missed, that frequency is mismatched, or that the caller is
dissatisfied. That absence of a signal toward the negative branch does not
demote any of the three gaps above — materiality, not likelihood, governs
classification, and each gap above passes the counterfactual test
independent of which answer seems more probable.

What remains supportable
- That the caller's presentation is currently stable, with no material
  change in Risk State and no new plan or intent, is supportable and
  directly evidenced.
- That there is no basis in the material for escalating the intervention
  is supportable.
- That continuing contact with this caller in some form is consistent with
  what's currently known is supportable — nothing in the material
  indicates that stopping or reducing contact is currently required by
  safety considerations, nor does anything indicate withdrawal of contact
  is safe to do unreviewed.

What is not supportable
- A conclusion that fortnightly calls, specifically, are the correct
  ongoing configuration is not supportable on the material presented. The
  intervention's stability finding establishes that the caller is doing no
  worse than baseline; it does not establish that the current
  configuration is the reason, or that it is the right one, since it has
  never been reviewed against a target and the caller's own view has not
  been established.
- Treating eighteen months of unreviewed continuity as itself evidence
  that the configuration is working is not supportable — stable pattern is
  not the same as effective, reviewed intervention.

Information requirement
- Whether a target or goal was set for this intervention, and if so,
  assessment of the intervention against it.
- Assessment of whether the current contact frequency/format matches the
  caller's current level of need, as distinct from whether the caller's
  presentation is stable.
- The caller's own current view on the acceptability and burden of the
  present contact arrangement.

Residual uncertainty
- Whether the observed stability is causally attributable to the
  intervention itself, as opposed to being independent of it, can
  legitimately remain unresolved without blocking the decision under
  examination. Resolving it either way does not itself change what continuing,
  reviewing, or reconfiguring the intervention would require — that turns
  on gaps 1–3 above, not on establishing causal attribution.
