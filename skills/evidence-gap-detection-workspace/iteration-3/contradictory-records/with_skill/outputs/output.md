EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
What is this caller's current living situation, and does that living
situation affect safety planning?

Established evidence
- Monday's contact note (call-taker A) documents the caller as living alone.
- Thursday's contact note (a different call-taker) documents the caller as
  living with a partner.
- Neither entry references or resolves the other; there is no indication in
  either record that the discrepancy was noticed, queried, or reconciled at
  the time.
- Both entries appear to be direct documentation from a contact with the
  caller (not inference by staff), but which one reflects the caller's
  current situation is not established.

Evidence gaps
1. Current living situation (contradictory). Two sources within the same
   record give inconsistent answers to the same question, and neither
   entry addresses the discrepancy. It is not established whether the
   caller currently lives alone, currently lives with a partner, or whether
   the situation genuinely changed between Monday and Thursday.
2. Reason for the discrepancy (missing). Whether the inconsistency reflects
   a genuine change in living arrangement, a recording error by one
   call-taker, or ambiguous phrasing on the call is not addressed anywhere
   in the material.

Gap classification
1. Current living situation — Decision-critical, and safety-critical for
   any safety-plan components that depend on household composition.
2. Reason for the discrepancy — No material gap for the decision under
   examination (see Materiality analysis).

Materiality analysis
1. Current living situation. Counterfactual test: if this were resolved as
   "lives alone," versus resolved as "lives with a partner," would the
   decision change? Yes — household composition bears directly on which
   safety-plan components can be relied on (for example, components that
   depend on another person being present in the home versus components
   that don't). Because plausible resolution in either direction changes
   what can be assumed about the caller's environment, this passes the
   counterfactual test independent of which answer seems more likely from
   either note. This is decision-critical to the factual question of what
   the current living situation is.
   Taking the safety-planning half of the decision separately: for any
   safety-plan element that depends on who is present in the household, the
   unresolved contradiction currently prevents safe reliance on that
   element in either direction — a plan cannot be safely built or endorsed
   on the unstated assumption that one of the two conflicting notes is
   correct. That makes this gap safety-critical specifically for
   household-composition-dependent components. For plan elements that do
   not depend on household composition, this gap does not by itself block
   reliance.
2. Reason for the discrepancy. Counterfactual test: if it were established
   that the discrepancy arose from a genuine change, a recording error, or
   ambiguous phrasing, would that change the current-decision answer of
   "what is the caller's living situation now"? No — direct confirmation of
   the current living situation resolves the operative fact regardless of
   why the record became inconsistent. Knowing the cause of the
   discrepancy might matter for record-quality purposes, but it is not
   necessary to resolve the decision under examination, and does not need
   to be answered for that decision to proceed. This is not a dependency of
   gap 1 — it is a separate, non-material question.

What remains supportable
- That the record contains two directly conflicting statements about the
  caller's living situation, made on different days by different
  call-takers, with neither referencing the other.
- That household composition is relevant to safety planning in this case in
  a way that makes the current unresolved contradiction consequential.

What is not supportable
- Any safety-planning decision, or component of one, that assumes the
  caller currently lives alone.
- Any safety-planning decision, or component of one, that assumes the
  caller currently lives with a partner.
- Any conclusion about why the two notes disagree.

Information requirement
- Direct, current confirmation of the caller's living situation, obtained
  from the caller (or otherwise verified), specific to today's date rather
  than inferred from either prior note.

Residual uncertainty
- The reason for the discrepancy between the Monday and Thursday notes
  (genuine change, recording error, or ambiguous phrasing) can remain
  unresolved without blocking the decision under examination, provided the
  current living situation itself is directly confirmed.
