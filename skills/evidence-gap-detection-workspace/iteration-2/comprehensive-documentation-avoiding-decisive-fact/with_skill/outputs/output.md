EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Has this caller ever made a previous suicide attempt? — the factual question
itself, at the resolution it was asked: whether the record establishes a
prior attempt (yes), establishes no prior attempt (no), or establishes
neither.

Established evidence
The lifetime-history section documents, in organised form: family history,
previous service contacts, mental health diagnoses, medication history, and
social history. These are genuinely established entries — they describe what
was assessed and recorded in each of those domains. None of them, individually
or in combination, states whether the caller has or has not made a previous
suicide attempt. Thoroughness in adjacent domains is evidence that those
domains were addressed; it is not evidence that the attempt-history question
was addressed, and the two must not be conflated.

Evidence gaps
- Prior suicide attempt history: missing. Nothing in the section states that
  the question was asked and answered in either direction ("asked, denied" or
  "asked, confirmed"). It also is not contradicted anywhere — no entry
  asserts a prior attempt and no entry asserts its absence. This is missing
  evidence, not absent evidence: the record does not show the question was
  put and answered; it shows the question was never actually recorded as
  addressed, despite adjacent domains being covered in detail.

Gap classification
- Prior suicide attempt history: Decision-critical gap, with respect to the
  decision under examination as stated (does the record establish a prior
  attempt). Whether it is also safety-critical depends on a separate
  question not established by the material provided: whether the unresolved
  answer is currently being relied on for an active safety decision (e.g. a
  safety plan or risk formulation proceeding on an assumption of no prior
  attempt). That reliance is not described in the material presented, so this
  gap is classified as decision-critical to the factual question, not
  automatically safety-critical. If this specific gap is in fact being relied
  on right now to support a safety decision, that reliance would need to be
  named explicitly for the classification to move to safety-critical — the
  clinical sensitivity of the topic itself is not sufficient grounds for that
  upgrade.

Materiality analysis
Counterfactual test applied to the decision "does the record establish a
prior suicide attempt":
- If resolved as "yes, prior attempt confirmed" — the decision under
  examination is answered affirmatively, and this is a fact that materially
  changes the evidentiary basis for anything downstream that treats attempt
  history as a factor (formulation, risk consideration, planning) — those are
  not decided here, but the fact itself is central to the decision under
  examination.
- If resolved as "no, no prior attempt" — the decision under examination is
  answered negatively, which is a different and equally consequential
  outcome to have on record, since "asked and denied" is a different finding
  from "never established" and would close the gap rather than leave it
  open.
Both plausible resolutions change what can be said in answer to the decision
under examination — from "unknown" to a definite answer in either direction.
The gap therefore passes the counterfactual test and is decision-critical.
This holds regardless of the fact that nothing else in the record points
toward a prior attempt — absence of a signal toward "yes" does not, by the
materiality-over-salience rule, demote this gap to residual uncertainty.

What remains supportable
The lifetime-history section can be relied on for what it actually documents:
family history, previous service contact history, diagnostic history,
medication history, and social history, each as recorded. These stand
independently of the attempt-history gap.

What is not supportable
Any statement that the record establishes — in either direction — whether
this caller has previously attempted suicide. It is equally unsupportable to
read the absence of a stated answer as reassuring ("nothing recorded, so
probably no prior attempt") or as concerning ("nothing recorded, so a prior
attempt is likely being concealed"). Both readings convert an unresolved
question into a conclusion the record does not support.

Information requirement
Direct confirmation of the caller's prior suicide attempt history — a
recorded answer to the specific question, distinct from the surrounding
history domains already documented. Named by type of information needed:
attempt-history status (yes / no / caller unable or unwilling to say), not a
further elaboration of any of the domains already covered in the section.

Residual uncertainty
None identified beyond the gap already named above. The other domains in the
lifetime-history section (family history, service contacts, diagnoses,
medication, social history) do not, on the material presented, contain
further unresolved questions that bear on the decision under examination.
