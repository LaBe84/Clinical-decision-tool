EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
What is this caller's current living situation, and does it affect safety
planning? Two sub-questions, held separately: (a) the factual question of
who the caller currently lives with; (b) whether that fact bears on the
safety plan currently being relied on.

Established evidence
- Monday's contact notes (call-taker A): caller lives alone.
- Thursday's contact notes (call-taker B, different from Monday's): caller
  lives with a partner.
- Neither entry references or resolves the other — there is no note
  indicating a change of circumstance, a correction, or an acknowledgement
  of the earlier entry.

Evidence gaps
1. Current living situation — contradictory. Two sources in the record
   directly disagree on the same question (alone vs. with partner), and
   neither addresses or reconciles the other.
2. Reason for the discrepancy (recording error, ambiguous phrasing at
   intake, or a genuine change in circumstance between Monday and Thursday)
   — missing. Never asked or addressed in either entry.

Gap classification
1. Current living situation: Safety-critical gap, specifically with respect
   to any safety plan currently being built or relied on that assumes one
   answer over the other. On the narrower factual question alone (without
   reference to a safety plan being relied on right now), it is
   decision-critical.
2. Reason for the discrepancy: Not a dependency of gap 1, and not
   independently material — see Materiality analysis below.

Materiality analysis
Gap 1 — current living situation:
Counterfactual test: if resolved as "lives alone," a safety plan that
assumes a partner is present to monitor, remove means, or intervene in a
crisis would be relying on a person who is not there. If resolved as "lives
with partner," a plan built around solitary status (e.g. treating the
caller as having no one on-site, or treating a partner as a means-access or
risk factor requiring separate assessment) would be built on a wrong
premise. Both plausible resolutions change what a safety plan can
responsibly assume about who is present, who can act as a support or
monitoring presence, and who may have access to means. This passes the
counterfactual test in both directions, so it is not residual uncertainty.
Because the stated decision includes "does it affect safety planning" —
i.e., reliance on this fact for a current safety plan — unresolved status
prevents safely concluding the plan is sound as constructed. That makes it
safety-critical for the safety-planning element of the decision, and
decision-critical for the plain factual question of living situation taken
on its own.

Gap 2 — reason for the discrepancy:
This is explanatory provenance, not a dependency of gap 1. The operative
fact the decision needs — who the caller currently lives with — can be
established directly (e.g., asking the caller in the next contact) without
first establishing why Monday's and Thursday's notes disagree. Resolving
the discrepancy's cause is not necessary to resolve the living-situation
gap, so it does not inherit gap 1's classification. On its own counterfactual
test against the stated decision: whether the discrepancy arose from
recording error, ambiguous phrasing, or a genuine change does not itself
change what safety planning should assume once the current fact is
confirmed directly. This is residual uncertainty — worth noting for record
quality, not for the safety-planning decision.

What remains supportable
That the record contains two unreconciled, conflicting statements about the
caller's living situation, made by different call-takers on different days,
is itself an established fact and can be documented as such.

What is not supportable
Any safety plan or planning decision that proceeds on the assumption of
either "lives alone" or "lives with partner" without direct confirmation of
the caller's current situation. Neither entry can be treated as
superseding the other on the record as it stands — there is no indication
of which is current, or whether both were accurate at different times.

Information requirement
Direct confirmation, from the caller, of their current living situation at
the point safety planning is undertaken. No further information about why
the two prior entries disagree is required to resolve this.

Residual uncertainty
Why the Monday and Thursday entries disagree (recording error, ambiguous
phrasing, or an actual change in circumstance between the two calls) can
remain unresolved without blocking the safety-planning decision, provided
the current living situation is confirmed directly before the plan relies
on it.
