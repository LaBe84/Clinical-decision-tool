EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Can a safety plan currently be built and relied on for this caller? The
caller has agreed in principle to a safety plan; the question is whether the
material establishes enough to build and rely on the specific plan
components (written elements, phone-based elements, and elements assuming a
stable place to act on) — not whether "some safety plan, of unspecified
form" is agreeable in the abstract.

Established evidence
- The caller has agreed in principle to a safety plan (documented
  agreement/willingness).
No other information in the material bears on the caller's capacity to
receive, retain, or act on any specific plan format.

Evidence gaps

1. Literacy — missing. Never asked or assessed anywhere in the material.
   Nothing indicates whether the caller can read.
2. Housing stability — missing. Never asked or assessed. Nothing indicates
   whether the caller has a stable place to keep a written plan, be reached
   at, or act on plan steps that assume a consistent location.
3. Phone access — missing. Never asked or assessed, including whether the
   caller has any phone access at all. Nothing indicates a phone number,
   device, or reachability was ever established.

Note on evidence type: all three are missing evidence, not absent evidence.
Nothing in the material suggests these are problems — that absence of a
negative signal is not itself a finding; the domains were simply never
assessed. Absence of a signal toward difficulty does not resolve the gap or
demote it (see materiality analysis below).

Gap classification

Applying the v0.2 rule to test each element of the decision separately,
because a safety plan's usability depends on several independent variables
(literacy for written elements, phone access for phone-based elements,
housing stability for elements assuming a stable location) that do not
imply one another:

1. Literacy — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on any written
   plan component (e.g., a written safety plan document, written coping
   steps, written contact list). Not established as safety-critical for
   phone-based or verbally-delivered components.
2. Phone access — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on any
   phone-based plan component (e.g., calling a crisis line, phone check-ins,
   phone-based contact with supports). Not established as safety-critical
   for written or in-person components.
3. Housing stability — Safety-critical, specifically for reliance on plan
   components that assume a consistent, safe location (e.g., keeping/
   retrieving a written plan, means-restriction steps tied to a fixed
   environment, being reached at a stable address). Not established as
   safety-critical for components that don't depend on a fixed location
   (e.g., steps the caller carries out regardless of where they are).

None of these three gaps is, on its own, evidence that the whole decision
("can any safety plan be built and relied on") is unsafe — each blocks
reliance on the specific components that depend on it, not on every
possible plan format. Whether the overall decision is blocked depends on
which components the plan being built actually relies on, which is not
established in the material either (see "What is not supportable" below).

Materiality analysis

Literacy: If resolved as "cannot read," a written plan cannot be relied on
— the caller cannot use it as constructed, and this changes what plan
content is safe to build. If resolved as "can read," a written plan remains
viable. Both plausible answers change what can safely be relied on for the
written component specifically, so this passes the counterfactual test:
safety-critical for that component.

Phone access: If resolved as "no phone access," any plan step depending on
calling a number (crisis line, support contact, check-in call) cannot be
relied on. If resolved as "has phone access," those steps remain viable.
Both answers change what can safely be relied on for phone-based
components, so this passes the counterfactual test: safety-critical for
those components.

Housing stability: If resolved as "unstable/no fixed housing," plan
elements assuming a consistent location (safe storage of a written plan,
contactability at an address, environment-based steps) cannot be relied on
as constructed. If resolved as "stable housing," those elements remain
viable. Both answers change what can safely be relied on for
location-dependent components, so this passes the counterfactual test:
safety-critical for those components.

Absence of any signal that these are problems does not change this
analysis — materiality, not likelihood, governs classification. "Nothing
suggests literacy/housing/phone access are problems" describes what hasn't
been said, not what has been established; it does not resolve any of the
three gaps or downgrade them from safety-critical.

What remains supportable
That the caller has agreed in principle to the general idea of a safety
plan. This agreement, on its own, is a legitimate starting point and is not
undermined by the gaps above.

What is not supportable
That a written safety plan, a phone-based safety plan, or a plan whose
components assume stable housing can currently be relied on for this
caller. The material does not establish the caller can read, has phone
access, or has stable housing, and each of those unknowns blocks reliance
on the specific plan components that depend on it. Nor is it supportable to
say which components a "safety plan" for this caller should consist of,
since the underlying capacities to receive and act on those components have
not been assessed.

Information requirement
Whether the caller can read (for any written component); whether the
caller has phone access and, if so, of what kind (for any phone-based
component); and the caller's current housing/location stability (for any
component assuming a consistent, safe location). Each is a distinct piece
of information tied to a distinct plan component, not a single combined
"capacity" question.

Residual uncertainty
None of these three questions can be treated as residual uncertainty for
this decision — each passes the counterfactual test against a specific,
identifiable plan component. There is no unresolved question in the
material that is safe to leave unaddressed without qualifying what kind of
plan can currently be relied on.
