EVIDENCE-GAP ANALYSIS

Decision under examination
Should the current intervention — fortnightly check-in calls, as currently
configured, in place eighteen months — continue? Held at the granularity
actually stated: this is not narrowed to "some contact versus none." Evidence
bearing on whether this specific configuration (fortnightly frequency, call
format) remains the right one, versus a different configuration serving the
caller better, is treated as material to this decision, not set aside as a
separate follow-on question.

Established evidence
- PORF has assessed the caller's current presentation against baseline and
  found it stable: no material change in Risk State, no new plan/intent.
  This is a direct, documented finding (assessed, not found) — not an
  absence of assessment.
- The intervention has been in place for eighteen months.
- No party is currently proposing escalation.

Evidence gaps

1. Whether the intervention has ever been reviewed against a target or
   objective since it was set up. Missing evidence — stated directly in the
   scenario as never having occurred. Nothing in the material indicates what
   the intervention was meant to achieve or whether it has done so.

2. Whether fortnightly frequency and call format remain the right
   configuration, versus an alternative that might serve the caller equally
   well or better. Missing evidence — this has never been assessed, because
   assessing it depends on the review described in gap 1.

Gap classification
1. Whether the intervention has been reviewed against a target — Decision-
   critical gap.
2. Whether the current configuration (frequency/format) is still the right
   one — dependency of gap 1: it cannot be assessed without the review
   described in gap 1 having occurred, so it is not independently tested
   against the decision; it inherits its operational status from gap 1
   rather than being separately classified as decision-critical in its own
   right.

Materiality analysis
Gap 1 — counterfactual test, applied at the decision's actual stated
granularity ("should this intervention, as configured, continue"):
- If resolved one way (a review would show the intervention has been
  meeting whatever target was set, in a configuration that suits the
  caller) — continuation as currently configured is supported.
- If resolved the other way (a review would show the intervention has not
  been meeting its target, or that no target was ever fit for purpose, or
  that a different configuration would serve the caller better) — continuing
  unchanged is not clearly supported, even though the caller's risk
  presentation is stable.
Both branches are plausible on the material presented, and they lead to
different conclusions about whether continuation "as configured" is
justified. This passes the counterfactual test independent of PORF's
finding: PORF's stability finding answers a different question (has the
caller's risk state changed) than this gap does (is the intervention itself
achieving anything, and is it the right configuration). A stable pattern is
not the same as an effective, still-fit-for-purpose intervention — the two
claims do not stand in for each other, and the absence of any signal that
the intervention is failing does not resolve this gap in either direction;
it has passed the counterfactual test and stays decision-critical regardless
of which answer seems more likely.

This gap is not safety-critical. Nothing in the material indicates the
absence of a review has left the caller unsafe right now, or that current
contact is inadequate to safely continue in the interim — PORF's stability
finding stands on its own as a direct assessment of current risk state and
is not undermined by the review gap. The unresolved question here concerns
proportionality and fitness of the intervention, not whether it is
currently safe to rely on the fact that the caller is not in crisis.

Gap 2 — dependency, not independently re-tested: resolving whether an
alternative configuration would serve better requires the review described
in gap 1 to have taken place; it cannot be answered ahead of that review, so
it is stated here as a dependency rather than run through its own
counterfactual test against the original decision.

What remains supportable
- That the caller's risk presentation is currently stable relative to
  baseline, with no material change in Risk State and no new plan/intent, is
  supportable and directly established.
- That there is no basis in the material for treating this caller as
  requiring escalation is supportable.

What is not supportable
- That fortnightly calls, as currently configured, remain the right or most
  proportionate intervention is not supportable from what's presented — the
  stability finding establishes the caller's risk state, not the
  intervention's continued fitness for purpose, and no review against a
  target has ever taken place to establish the latter.
- Continuation "because nothing has changed and no one is proposing
  escalation" is not, on its own, a supportable basis for concluding the
  configuration is still correct — absence of a proposal to escalate speaks
  to risk, not to whether this is still the right intervention design.

Information requirement
What is needed: a record of the intervention's original target/objective (if
one was set) and an assessment of performance against it, covering whether
the current frequency and format remain appropriate for this caller now.
Named as a type of information required, not as an instruction to run a
particular upstream skill or as a specification of what any resulting review
should contain.

Residual uncertainty
The caller's own current view of or preference regarding the call frequency
and format is not addressed in the material presented. Nothing here
indicates the review would need to resolve this before the decision under
examination can be addressed as framed, so it is named as residual
uncertainty rather than tested further, pending whatever the review itself
surfaces.
