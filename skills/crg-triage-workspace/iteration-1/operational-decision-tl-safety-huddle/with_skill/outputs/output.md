CRG TRIAGE

Source material
No prior PORF formulation or Contact Pattern Analysis has been supplied — only
a same-shift case summary: three calls tonight, each answered by a different
counsellor, none aware of the previous contacts because the system wasn't
checked between calls, and each counsellor gave the caller slightly different
guidance about what to do next. No indication of acute risk. Triage on this
basis is inherently limited by the absence of a formulation, and that
limitation is noted rather than backfilled here.

What decision is actually pending
The next counsellor who answers needs to know, before picking up, that this
is call four of the same contact tonight and what consistent guidance to give
— right now nothing tells them that, and without it they will repeat the same
fragmented, contradictory response as the first three calls. That is an
immediate operational/coordination decision about how to handle the next
contact this shift, not a standing question about the caller's care plan.

Why the surrounding features don't settle it
The case has some pull toward feeling serious — three calls in one night,
visible inconsistency across staff, and a caller who's had three different
sets of instructions — and none of that is nothing. But high contact
frequency alone isn't CRG, and staff/system anxiety about "this looks
disorganised" isn't CRG either. There's no indication of acute risk, so this
isn't the immediate pathway. And nothing here suggests the underlying
formulation or intervention approach is inadequate — the problem is that
call-to-call visibility and consistency broke down operationally tonight, not
that the caller's presentation has outgrown Helpline-level understanding.
That's what keeps this out of CRG.

Destination
TL/Safety Huddle

Therefore
Because the pending decision is a real-time, this-shift coordination problem
— ensuring the next counsellor has visibility of the prior three contacts and
gives consistent guidance — rather than a question about the adequacy of the
caller's formulation or an acute safety need, it belongs with TL/Safety
Huddle, not CRG or the immediate risk pathway.
