PORF FORMULATION

Risk Status
The information available establishes an enduring pattern of very high contact frequency with this service, but does not establish enduring clinical vulnerability against a named comparator. Compared with the general population of people who contact this Helpline, a pattern of very frequent attempted contact is itself unusual and worth naming as a standing feature of this person's relationship with the service. However, Risk Status properly concerns enduring vulnerability (previous suicidal behaviour, chronic psychiatric difficulty, longstanding adversity, established crisis pattern) rather than contact volume alone, and no information is provided here on suicidal/self-harm history, psychiatric history, substance use, or longstanding social adversity. Against the reference population of frequent Helpline contacts specifically, this presentation is consistent with — not distinguishable from — a subgroup for whom high contact frequency is itself the established baseline. Current information is insufficient to place this person's enduring vulnerability, as opposed to their contact behaviour, against any reference population with confidence.

Risk State
The clearest within-person comparison available is behavioural: a marked increase in attempted contact frequency, a high proportion of attempts terminating during the pre-call message before any counsellor connection occurs, and variable engagement across the calls that do connect. Whether this represents a change from this person's own baseline contact pattern cannot be determined without knowing what is typical for them — if very frequent contact and early termination is itself their established pattern, this is not evidence of state change; if it represents a new escalation in frequency or a new tendency to disconnect before connecting, it may indicate a material shift in urgency, ambivalence, or capacity to tolerate the wait/engagement process. The variability in engagement once connected is itself informative but ambiguous: it could reflect fluctuating distress, fluctuating willingness to use the service, situational interruption, or something else, and none of these can be distinguished from the pattern alone. No change in intent, planning, specificity, hopelessness, or capability can be established, because the calls that would carry that content are precisely the ones not reaching a counsellor.

Resources
The only resource that can be evaluated from the material given is the service contact itself, and even that is only partially usable — a high proportion of attempts do not reach the point where any supportive intervention, engagement, or safety-related conversation can occur. Where connection is achieved, variable engagement suggests the counsellor relationship is sometimes but not consistently usable as a resource in the moment. No information is available on internal coping resources, relational supports, other treatment engagement, or environmental protections, so it is not possible to say what, if anything, is containing risk during the intervals between contact attempts or during the disconnected attempts themselves.

Foreseeable Change
Several plausible developments could alter this formulation and should prompt review. Continued failure to connect despite repeated attempts could plausibly increase frustration, hopelessness, or a sense that help is not obtainable, particularly if the person is attempting contact during acute distress. Conversely, the pattern could reflect help-seeking that resolves once connection occurs, in which case the volume of attempts is not itself informative about escalating risk. A shift from early termination toward the person remaining engaged for longer, or toward disclosure of intent/planning during a connected call, would represent a materially different picture and should prompt reassessment. Any information becoming available about what occurs during the disconnected attempts — even indirectly, such as caller ID patterns, timing clustering around a particular time of day, or any content captured before disconnection — would be materially relevant and should be actively sought rather than left as an unexamined gap.

Key uncertainties
The central and clinically material uncertainty is that there is essentially no information about what is occurring psychologically during the majority of contact attempts, since these terminate before a counsellor connects. It cannot be established whether these attempts reflect acute distress, ambivalence about engaging, a specific unmet need, testing of the service, environmental/technical interruption, or something unrelated to risk. The function of the repeated contact pattern cannot be established from frequency and timing alone. It is also not possible to determine, from the material provided, whether the current pattern represents a change from this person's own baseline or is consistent with it, because no baseline contact pattern has been described.

Integrated formulation
Because the available information consists almost entirely of behavioural/contact-pattern data (frequency, timing, and point of termination) rather than clinical content, this formulation is limited to what that pattern can and cannot support. Currently, attempted contact is very frequent, a high proportion of attempts terminate during the pre-call message before reaching a counsellor, and engagement is variable across the calls that do connect — this describes a change in contact behaviour but does not, on its own, establish a change in suicide risk, since the psychological content of the disconnected attempts is unknown. The connected calls represent the only point at which any protective or supportive resource can currently be brought to bear, and their variable engagement means this resource is inconsistently usable. The main uncertainty is what is happening during the disconnected attempts and whether the current pattern differs from this person's own established contact behaviour, since both are necessary to interpret the pattern as either escalation or an unremarkable variant of their usual help-seeking. Foreseeable developments — continued failure to connect, or a shift toward longer engagement or disclosure of intent once connected — would materially change this picture in either direction and should be actively monitored for.

Therefore
Continued Helpline management is proportionate at this stage, with a TL/Safety Huddle consultation to review the contact pattern specifically (frequency, timing, termination point, and any available baseline comparison) and to consider whether proactive strategies (e.g., attempting outbound contact, reviewing any existing CMP/Access Agreement, or flagging the pattern for the next connected call) are appropriate; escalation to emergency response is not supported by the current information, since frequency and early termination alone do not establish elevated intent or imminent risk, but the case should be actively flagged for reassessment as soon as any connected call yields clinical content, or if the pattern itself changes.
