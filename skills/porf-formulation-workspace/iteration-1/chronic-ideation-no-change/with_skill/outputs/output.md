PORF FORMULATION

Risk Status
Compared with the general population, this caller carries elevated enduring vulnerability: suicidal ideation has been present continuously for at least three years, is severe enough to prompt weekly contact with a crisis service, and is described by her as a constant background experience rather than an occasional intrusion. Compared with a reference population of people who make recurrent, longstanding contact with Lifeline for chronic ideation, however, her standing is comparatively more favourable: she has no reported history of suicide attempts or self-harm across three years of contact, has sustained a stable, predictable pattern of help-seeking rather than escalating or erratic contact, and has consistently used the service as intended (to talk the thoughts through) rather than in the context of crisis presentations. Enduring vulnerability is therefore real but is accompanied by an established, non-escalating pattern that differentiates her from higher-status callers with attempt histories or unpredictable contact patterns.

Risk State
Tonight's presentation indicates no material change from her own baseline. She reports the same intensity of ideation she usually describes, no plan, no intent, a "decent day," and a contact that follows her typical weekly pattern and typical purpose (talking it through). No indicators of change are present in the domains that would ordinarily signal deterioration: no new specificity or planning, no reported increase in hopelessness, agitation, intoxication, isolation, or loss of behavioural control, and no reduction in her engagement or willingness to use support — if anything, her willingness to reach out and talk is consistent with her usual coping. Based on available information, this is a within-person comparison showing sameness, not escalation.

Resources
Her primary current resource is the Lifeline contact itself, which is available, accessible, and demonstrably usable — she is using it tonight exactly as she has for three years, which supports rather than merely exists as an option for her. Her own capacity to articulate "nothing has changed" and to identify a decent day suggests some current self-monitoring and cognitive engagement. The three-year pattern of contact without acting on the thoughts is itself evidence of an established internal capacity to hold the ideation without translating it into behaviour, though the source and durability of that capacity are not detailed in the information available.

Foreseeable Change
No specific foreseeable trigger has been identified in tonight's contact. Generic possibilities always exist (e.g., future intoxication, an unreported stressor emerging later in the call, an anniversary or precipitant not yet disclosed) but none of these has evidentiary support from the material available and should not be treated as active concerns tonight. The main foreseeable-risk-relevant marker to watch for across future contacts would be any departure from this established pattern — for example, increased frequency or urgency of calls, emergence of specificity/planning, reduced ability to be reassured, or disengagement from her usual contact rhythm — any of which would warrant fresh formulation rather than assumption of "business as usual."

Key uncertainties
Current information does not establish what, if anything, is happening in her broader context tonight beyond her own report (e.g., substance use, access to means, unreported precipitants), since these were not raised and are not stated to have been asked about. It also cannot be independently verified whether her self-assessment of "nothing changed" reflects accurate insight or some degree of minimisation; however, there is no positive evidence in the contact to suggest minimisation, and her insight into her own pattern after three years of contact is itself a relevant, if imperfect, source of information.

Integrated formulation
Because this caller has carried chronic, longstanding suicidal ideation for three years and makes weekly contact with the service, she carries elevated enduring vulnerability relative to the general population. Currently, however, her presentation shows no material change from her own established baseline: same intensity, no plan or intent, a reasonable day, and a contact that matches her habitual pattern and purpose. This is supported by a three-year history of sustained, non-escalating engagement without any attempts, and by her active, appropriate use of the service tonight as a coping resource. The main uncertainty is that no information is available about her broader context beyond her self-report, so subtle contextual change cannot be ruled out, though nothing in the contact positively suggests it. No specific foreseeable trigger is identified tonight; the relevant marker for future contacts is any departure from this established pattern. Chronicity and contact frequency should not, on their own, be read as indicators of acute risk in the absence of state change.

Therefore
The proportionate response is continued Helpline management through brief supportive intervention consistent with her established pattern — direct suicide enquiry has appropriately confirmed no plan or intent, and this should be documented alongside the assessment of no material change from baseline; no escalation (Safety Contact, TL/Safety Huddle, CRG, or emergency response) is indicated on the basis of tonight's contact, as escalation would not be supported by either Risk Status or Risk State findings and would conflate chronicity and contact frequency with acute risk.
