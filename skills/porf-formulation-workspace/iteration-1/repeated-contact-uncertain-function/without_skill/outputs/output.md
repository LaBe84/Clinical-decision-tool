# Formulation and Response Plan: Repeated Short Calls, Uncertain Risk Function

## Case Summary

- 6 calls in 48 hours, each under 5 minutes
- Pattern: caller hangs up shortly after being asked how she is coping
- 2 of 6 calls contain reference to "thinking about ending it"; 4 do not mention suicidal ideation
- No known history on file; no collateral information available

## What the Formulation Should Say

**1. Name the uncertainty explicitly — do not resolve it prematurely.**
The record does not currently support a confident read of this pattern in either direction. Two plausible (non-exclusive) accounts fit the data:

- *Escalating risk with approach-avoidance*: she is in genuine distress, wants contact, but is not yet able to tolerate being asked directly about coping — she reaches for the phone, gets close to disclosure, and the anxiety/shame/fear of the conversation becomes too much, so she disconnects. On this account, the two SI-mentioning calls may be the more accurate signal and the four "no mention" calls reflect avoidance, not absence of ideation.
- *Help-seeking behaviour testing the relationship/service*: repeated brief contact can also function as testing whether the line is safe, whether she'll be judged, or whether anyone will notice a pattern across calls — particularly if she has no other support and no history is on file. The hang-up after the coping question may be about the *question* (feels intrusive, unanswerable, or exposing) rather than about the risk content itself.

These are not mutually exclusive and the formulation should hold both rather than anchoring on the more reassuring (or more alarming) one. The honest statement for the file is: **frequency and brevity of contact plus inconsistent SI disclosure indicate an unresolved, active engagement attempt with unclear current risk level — this is a "possible escalation, insufficient data" formulation, not a "low risk, attention-seeking" formulation and not a "confirmed high risk" formulation.**

**2. Treat the pattern itself as clinical information, independent of content.**
Six calls in 48 hours from someone not previously known to the service is itself a signal of acute distress or crisis, regardless of what is or isn't said. The formulation should state this plainly: the *contact pattern* (frequency, timing, consistent point of disconnection) is itself a risk indicator, on top of and independent from the SI content in two of the six calls.

**3. Avoid two specific formulation errors.**
- Do not average the two SI-positive and four SI-negative calls into an implied "40% likely SI" and treat that as reassuring. Suicidal ideation disclosed even once in six recent contacts, from an unknown caller with an established pattern of fleeing the coping question, should be weighted toward the higher-risk end, not diluted by the non-disclosing calls.
- Do not code the pattern as "attention-seeking," "test caller," or similar minimising labels in the absence of any actual assessment completed. No call has yet reached a full risk conversation — there is no basis to characterise the calls as low-stakes contact-seeking.

**4. Flag the specific gap: no call has reached assessment.**
Every single call has terminated before coping/risk status could be established. That is the central clinical fact — this is not a caller who has been assessed six times with reassuring results; it is a caller who has been *unassessable* six times running. The formulation should record that current risk status is genuinely unknown, not low.

## Proportionate Response

**Immediate (this shift):**
- Flag the caller ID/number (if available via caller ID or the two calls where she may have given identifying detail) for priority routing so that if she calls again, it goes to a worker briefed on this pattern rather than being treated as a fresh, unconnected contact.
- Brief the next shift/handover explicitly: 6 calls/48 hrs, consistent disconnection point, mixed SI disclosure, no assessment completed, no history, no collateral.
- If policy and legal basis allow, and a number is available, consider whether a callback attempt is appropriate per local protocol — but only if this is within your service's normal practice for repeat crisis contacts, not as a one-off improvisation. Do not initiate emergency service welfare checks based on this pattern alone; there is not yet a specific, current, credible indicator of imminent risk (e.g., stated plan, means, intent, or a call ending in a way that itself signals immediate danger). That threshold has not been met by what's documented.

**Approach on the next call (whether tonight or later):**
- Slow down before reaching the coping question. The pattern suggests that question is the trigger point for disconnection — so the priority is not to skip it, but to build enough safety and pacing first that she can stay on the line through it.
- Name the pattern gently and non-judgementally early in the call if clinically appropriate: acknowledge she's called several times, that's okay, and she doesn't have to have the right words — this can reduce the shame/pressure that may be driving the hang-up.
- Prioritise staying connected over extracting a risk rating in any single call. A completed, gentler conversation this time is more valuable than a forced direct question that produces call #7.
- If she discloses SI or gives any indication of plan/means/intent, follow standard direct risk assessment and safety planning protocol from that point — the uncertainty in the formulation is about *interpreting the pattern so far*, not a reason to soften the response once clearer risk information is actually obtained.

**Documentation:**
- Record this as an active, unresolved pattern requiring continuity of care across shifts/workers, not as six discrete, closed contacts.
- Explicitly log "risk status: unknown/unable to assess" rather than defaulting to a low-risk code, so future workers don't inherit a falsely reassuring picture.

**Escalation threshold:**
- Escalate to a supervisor or duty clinician now to make a joint decision on whether proactive outreach (if a number/identity is available) is warranted given the frequency and the SI mentions, even without a completed assessment. This is a judgement call that should not sit with a single call-taker alone given the ambiguity — that itself is part of the proportionate response: shared decision-making under uncertainty, rather than either unilateral escalation to emergency services or unilateral downgrading to routine follow-up.
