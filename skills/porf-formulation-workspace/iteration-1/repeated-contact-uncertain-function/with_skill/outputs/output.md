PORF FORMULATION

Risk Status
Risk Status cannot be established against any named reference population. There is no history on file and no collateral information, so it is not possible to say whether this caller's enduring vulnerability is elevated relative to, for example, other first-time/unknown-history contacts to this service, people with a similar disclosed-ideation profile, or the general population. The only status-relevant fact available is that within a 48-hour window she has disclosed "thinking about ending it" on two of six contacts — a directly reported statement, not yet corroborated by any longitudinal pattern. This should not be treated as equivalent to an established history of suicidal behaviour, and it should not be discounted either. Status is genuinely indeterminate rather than low.

Risk State
A within-person baseline for this caller does not yet exist beyond the current 48-hour window itself, so Risk State must be assessed provisionally from the pattern of these six contacts rather than from a prior known baseline. Within that window: contact frequency is high (six calls in 48 hours), each call is brief (under five minutes), and each ends shortly after she is asked how she is coping. Content varies — two calls include a stated wish to end her life, four do not mention suicidal ideation at all. This variability could reflect fluctuating distress that is genuinely inconsistent call to call, an unwillingness or inability to sustain the coping question regardless of her internal state, or different degrees of the same underlying distress that she discloses only intermittently. The current information does not establish which of these is occurring, and does not establish whether her state is deteriorating, stable-but-distressed, or improving. What can be said is that the contact pattern itself — increasing frequency, shortening duration, disengagement specifically at the point support is offered — is different from a single, resolved contact, and warrants attention as a pattern in its own right, independent of what it implies about suicide risk specifically.

Resources
No resources can currently be confirmed as available and usable. No collateral exists to identify supportive relationships, treatment engagement, or environmental protections. The service itself is a potential resource, but she is not yet using it as one — she disengages at the point the call moves toward exploring her coping, which is also the point resources would normally be identified or mobilised. This should be read as an access/engagement gap rather than as evidence resources are absent from her life.

Foreseeable Change
Plausible developments include: continuation or further escalation of the calling pattern; a future call in which she remains on the line past the coping question, creating an opportunity to gather collateral and assess intent/means directly; cessation of contact altogether, which would remove the only current source of information and monitoring; and disclosure, in a subsequent call, of a plan, means, or timeframe that would materially change the picture from ideation to imminent risk. Each subsequent call is itself a foreseeable review point and should be treated as one.

Key uncertainties
Whether the same person is placing all six calls; the reason she disengages after being asked how she is coping (avoidance, ambivalence, distress, safety/privacy concerns, or something else); current access to means; whether there is any plan or timeframe attached to the stated thoughts of ending it; whether any supports, services, or protective factors exist in her life; and whether the four calls without mention of suicidal ideation reflect genuine absence of ideation in that moment or simply non-disclosure.

Integrated formulation
Because no history or collateral exists, Risk Status cannot be established against any comparator, and the formulation must rely on the pattern emerging across the current 48-hour window itself. Currently, that pattern shows frequent, brief contacts with inconsistent disclosure of suicidal ideation and a consistent point of disengagement — immediately after being asked how she is coping — which cannot yet be interpreted as either escalation or reassurance-seeking without further information. No protective resources have been confirmed as available or in use, largely because engagement has not reached the point where they would be identified. The main uncertainty is what is driving the repeated brief contact and disengagement, and whether the two disclosures of suicidal thinking reflect a stable or worsening state. The most significant foreseeable change is either a future call in which she remains engaged long enough to allow direct enquiry, or cessation of contact, either of which would substantially alter what can be known. Therefore the priority is to preserve and extend engagement and build a longitudinal picture across contacts, rather than to treat frequency or the ideation disclosures in isolation as grounds for emergency escalation.

Therefore
Continued Helpline management with active continuity measures is proportionate at this point: flag the pattern for the team/TL so all six contacts are read as one unfolding presentation rather than six isolated ones, use brief supportive intervention aimed specifically at sustaining engagement past the coping question (rather than repeating it in the same way each time), and conduct direct suicide enquiry — including access to means and any plan — the moment a call allows it. A TL/Safety Huddle consultation is warranted given the frequency and the two ideation disclosures, to agree how to hold this over multiple short contacts and to review after each subsequent call, but escalation to emergency response is not supported by the current formulation, since neither call frequency nor the presence of chronic-sounding ideation statements, on their own, establishes imminent risk. This should be revisited immediately if a subsequent call yields disclosure of intent, plan, or means, or if contact stops altogether.
