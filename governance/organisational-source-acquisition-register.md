## Acceptance criteria for live-use source control

Before a rule is changed from provisional to operationally approved, the source record must include:

1. title, document owner, version/document identifier, approval status, approval date, effective date and review date;
2. approving authority or governance route and, where available, an approval reference;
3. authoritative controlled-document location or persistent retrieval reference;
4. specific section, heading or other stable locator that directly supports the canonical rule;
5. relevant scope, exclusions, exceptions, authority requirements and documentation requirements;
6. confirmation that the source is current and applicable to Lifeline’s operating model;
7. resolution or explicit escalation of any conflict with another controlled source relied upon by the same canonical area.

## Rules while source control is incomplete

- Canonical drafts may state only the recorded high-level boundaries.
- Skills must not invent mandatory thresholds, destinations, procedures or restrictions.
- Regression tests may verify source deferral and documented high-level boundaries, but not unverified operational details.
- Where organisational sources conflict or remain materially ambiguous, canonical content must preserve that uncertainty and defer the matter to the accountable organisational owner rather than resolve it by inference.
- If a relied-on source is withdrawn, superseded, materially amended or subsequently found not to be the authoritative controlled version, affected canonical rules revert to provisional status pending impact review and reapproval.
- A source title, filename, summary or prior circulation is not evidence that a canonical rule is organisationally approved. Operational reliance requires verification against the current controlled source and an accountable approval decision.
